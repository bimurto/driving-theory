For questions containing a picture or video, use the recognition cues described here together with the media shown in the question.

## How to apply the rules to a question

### First establish the scene

Before judging an answer, identify your own vehicle, direction and lane. Then read the scene from far to near:

1. police, traffic lights and signs;
2. road shape, markings, crossings and junctions;
3. moving vehicles and their likely paths;
4. cyclists, pedestrians, children and other vulnerable people;
5. hidden areas behind parked or large vehicles; and
6. surface, weather, visibility and space behind you.

Do not answer from one striking object while ignoring the rest. A green light may be visible, but a pedestrian can still cross your turning path. A priority sign may apply, but a queue can prevent you clearing the junction. A slow cyclist may tempt an overtake, but a solid line or oncoming vehicle can make it impossible.

### Separate permission from safety

Many wrong answers confuse “normally permitted” with “safe now.” Work in two stages:

- **Legal permission:** Does a signal, sign, marking or general rule permit the manoeuvre?
- **Safe execution:** Is the route visible and free, is the gap sufficient, and can everyone be protected?

Both must be true. A permitted overtake is still wrong when the route is unclear. Priority does not justify a collision. A speed below the posted maximum is still too high when the stopping distance exceeds the view.

The reverse matters too: a cautious-looking action may be wrong when it creates confusion or obstruction. Do not stop without reason at every junction, drive extremely slowly on a clear road, or use hazard lights simply to park illegally.

### Trace paths, not positions

A picture freezes vehicles, but the question concerns what happens next. Extend each path mentally:

- Where will an oncoming vehicle go if it turns?
- Will your turn cross a cycle path or pedestrian route?
- Does a lorry need to swing out or cut across the inside?
- Will a merging lane end before a safe gap appears?
- Could a door open or a child emerge from a hidden space?
- If you brake, is the following traffic close?

Indicators show intention, not a guarantee. Wheel angle, speed reduction, road position and a person's gaze may be clues, but keep a reserve for a changed decision.

### Recognise when several answers are correct

German theory questions often require every correct option. Evaluate each option independently; do not stop after finding one good answer. Two actions can belong together—for example, reduce speed **and** increase distance, or brake **and** remain ready to steer. An option is not wrong merely because another option is also necessary.

Watch absolute words such as “always,” “only,” “never” and “under all circumstances.” They are correct for genuine prohibitions, such as never accelerating while being overtaken, but often turn a sensible guideline into a false statement. Check the conditions.

### Read numerical questions carefully

Identify whether the question asks for reaction, braking, stopping, following, payload, permissible mass or actual mass. Write the formula first and keep units consistent. A correct calculation using the wrong quantity is still wrong.

For signs showing mass or dimensions, use the actual loaded vehicle where the sign requires actual value. For licence entitlement, use permissible maximum masses. For payload, subtract unladen mass from permissible gross mass. For trailer nose weight, use actual trailer mass for the 4% minimum and compare every maximum.

### Read signs without relying on colour alone

In a visual question, use shape, border, symbol and supplementary plate. Then check which direction or lane it addresses. A sign on the opposite side may apply to oncoming traffic. A temporary sign can replace a permanent arrangement. A plate with arrows can define the start, continuation or end of a restriction.

For a traffic light, find the signal belonging to your lane and direction. A bicycle symbol may not address a car. A green arrow can release only one direction. Lane-control signals above the road apply to the lane below them.

### Read a developing video scene

Keep scanning; do not focus permanently on the first hazard. A pedestrian may remain safe while a cyclist appears from behind. Brake lights far ahead can reveal a queue before the nearest vehicle reacts. A ball, open gate, bus stop, parked van, construction barrier or narrowing can be an early cue rather than the final hazard.

Choose an action early enough to create options. Usually this means release the accelerator, cover the brake, increase the gap or change position smoothly. Emergency braking belongs to an immediate collision path; ordinary uncertainty calls for controlled preparation rather than panic.

### Use the safety hierarchy

When rules appear to compete, protect in this order:

1. prevent death or injury;
2. obey police, signals, signs and priority;
3. preserve control, view and stopping space;
4. avoid unnecessary obstruction; and
5. protect property and traffic flow.

This does not let a driver ignore rules for convenience. It explains why you may need to waive priority to prevent a crash, cross a line only under an authorised emergency direction, or leave a vehicle during tunnel fire rather than protect it.

**Remember:** determine the scene, test permission, test safety, trace future paths, and judge every answer option separately.

## 1. Safe and responsible driving

### The basic duty

The foundation of German road traffic law is constant caution and mutual consideration (*ständige Vorsicht und gegenseitige Rücksicht*). Drive so that nobody is harmed or endangered and nobody is obstructed or inconvenienced more than unavoidable. Having priority never gives permission to cause a collision.

Defensive driving means expecting plausible mistakes and preserving an escape margin. It does not mean stopping everywhere or giving up priority without reason. Watch the whole developing scene, not only the vehicle directly ahead. Look far enough ahead to notice brake lights, queues, junctions, children, cyclists, doors, road-surface changes and vehicles that may enter your path. Use clues—such as hesitant movement or a non-local registration plate—but do not treat them as proof of what somebody will do.

Drive predictably. Signal in good time, hold a stable course, avoid unnecessary harsh braking and do not surprise others with last-second decisions. If another road user behaves incorrectly, reduce the danger instead of trying to teach or punish them. Never copy an illegal manoeuvre merely because somebody ahead has done it.

Unjustified very slow driving can obstruct traffic and create dangerous speed differences. Slow down whenever safety requires it; the problem is driving abnormally slowly without a good reason. Likewise, avoid preventable stoppages by checking fuel or charge and learning to start smoothly.

### Fitness to drive

Fitness is not fixed for the whole journey. Illness, fever, nausea, dizziness, dehydration, heat, pain, stress, anger, shock, fatigue, alcohol, drugs and medication can all impair observation, judgement or reaction. If safe control is doubtful, do not start. If symptoms develop, stop safely, reassess, rest or change drivers.

Heavy eyelids, frequent yawning, wandering thoughts and difficulty holding the lane are urgent fatigue warnings. Stop at the first signs. A short sleep and a real break help; loud music, coffee, cold air or willpower do not make a tired driver safe. Plan breaks before a long trip and move during stops. A fit co-driver can take over, but passengers are not substitute drivers and distracting conversations should wait.

Alcohol reaches its maximum effect only after drinking has ended. Food, coffee, exercise, sleep or a cold shower do not accelerate its removal; only time does. An exam estimate is roughly **0.1 per mille per hour**, but real elimination varies, so never calculate a supposedly safe departure time. During the probationary period and before age 21, the alcohol prohibition applies. A current THC threshold is not a safety target: cannabis can impair driving below it, interacts dangerously with alcohol and may remain detectable long after the obvious effect. Other drugs have unpredictable effects and detection periods.

The general administrative alcohol threshold is **0.25 mg/l breath alcohol or 0.5 per mille blood alcohol**. The current THC administrative threshold is **3.5 ng/ml in blood serum**. These are enforcement thresholds, not safe-consumption allowances: impairment, danger or a probationary/under-21 prohibition can make driving unlawful below them. At the rough elimination estimate, 1.0 per mille takes about 10 hours—not a half-hour sleep, walk or strong coffee. Alcohol and THC together bring additional risk and legal consequences.

Medication may cause sleepiness, slower reactions, blurred vision, dizziness or poor concentration. Read the leaflet, ask a doctor or pharmacist, and take interaction warnings seriously. A prescription does not automatically mean the person is fit to drive. The decisive rule is simple: if actually impaired, do not drive regardless of a measured number.

### Attention and electronic devices

Remove distractions before moving. Secure children, animals and loose objects; set navigation, climate and audio controls in advance. Do not hold or pick up a phone or covered electronic device while driving. Permitted operation must be hands-free or require only a brief glance appropriate to the conditions. Even a legal hands-free conversation can absorb attention.

The stationary-device exception requires the motor to be fully switched off; an automatic start-stop pause is not enough. If a task needs concentration, park safely first. Assistance systems also demand supervision: the driver remains responsible for observation, speed, distance and intervention.

### Emotional control and social pressure

Do not let tailgaters, impatient passengers or competitive drivers set your speed. Keep a safe gap, let pressure pass and avoid eye contact or gestures that escalate conflict. After anger, shock or a frightening near miss, stop until concentration returns. Young groups travelling at night combine inexperience, tiredness, excitement and distraction; agree on calm conduct before departure.

### Before moving off

Adjust the seat so every control can be operated securely, set the head restraint near the top of the head, fasten the belt flat and snug, and adjust mirrors. Check that windows, lights and number plates are clean and clear of snow or ice. Check tyres, visible leaks, lights, wipers, washers, load security and warning lamps. In an unfamiliar vehicle, first locate the controls and learn its dimensions, steering, brakes and acceleration cautiously.

The driver must be able to see and hear warnings. Do not hang objects in the field of view or use headphones so loudly that sirens and horns cannot be heard. A dangerous defect that develops during a trip must be corrected; if that is impossible, take the vehicle out of traffic by the shortest safe route.

### Common exam traps

- “I have priority” is never a reason to continue into a foreseeable crash.
- Defensive driving is not needless hesitation; it is early, proportionate action.
- Very slow is not automatically safe if there is no reason for it.
- Coffee, fresh air and loud music do not cure fatigue.
- A legal medication or hands-free call can still impair driving.
- Assistance systems do not transfer responsibility away from the driver.

**Remember:** see widely, expect mistakes, keep an escape margin and choose safety over being right.

## 2. People who need special protection

### Children, older people and people needing help

When children, older people or visibly vulnerable people may be endangered, reduce speed and be ready to brake so that danger is excluded. Do not rely on them behaving perfectly, even when your light is green or you have priority.

Children may misjudge speed and distance, fail to locate sound, copy a friend, chase a ball or animal, change direction suddenly, or enter the road where a cycle path ends. A ball, toy or dog is an advance warning that a child may follow. Near playing children or an unclear school-area scene, slow immediately—down to walking speed where necessary—and stop if their next movement is uncertain.

Older people may move slowly, hesitate or turn back. A person using a wheelchair may need extra width, have difficulty at a kerb or be unable to leave the carriageway quickly. Wait rather than squeezing past. Offer help only after securing the traffic situation.

A white cane, a guide dog in a white harness, or a yellow marking with three black dots can identify severe visual impairment or disability. Do not wait for eye contact. Slow down, stop if necessary and never use the horn simply to hurry the person.

### Pedestrian crossings

At a marked pedestrian crossing (*Fußgängerüberweg*, zebra crossing), approach at moderate speed and enable pedestrians, wheelchair users and powered-wheelchair users who visibly intend to cross to do so. Brake early enough that yielding is unmistakable. Overtaking is prohibited at the crossing.

Do not stop or park on the crossing or within **5 m before it**. In a queue, keep the markings completely clear. Never pass a vehicle that hides the crossing; a person may be directly in front of it. If unexpectedly stopped on the crossing, clear it forward or backward only when that can be done safely.

A cyclist riding across does not gain automatic zebra-crossing priority merely by being on a bicycle, but you must still avoid a collision. A cyclist who dismounts and pushes is a pedestrian.

### Turning across pedestrians and cyclists

When turning, give special consideration to pedestrians and wait when necessary. Straight-ahead cyclists and e-scooter users travelling beside you must also pass before you turn across their path. Use mirrors, signal, make a direct blind-spot check and recheck immediately before turning. Large vehicles can hide people; if a lorry or bus clears your view, search the crossing again rather than following blindly.

When opening a door, use the mirror and a shoulder check. Cyclists may approach from behind on either side. Prefer the pavement side for passengers and supervise children. Nobody may endanger pedestrians on the pavement or cyclists on a cycle path while getting in or out.

### Passing pedestrians, cyclists and e-scooters

Motor vehicles overtaking cyclists, pedestrians or e-scooter users must normally keep at least **1.5 m in built-up areas** (*innerorts*) and **2 m outside built-up areas** (*außerorts*). These are minimums; use more where speed, wind, children or an unstable rider require it. If the width is insufficient, wait.

Rows of parked vehicles are danger zones. Look for feet beneath vehicles, movement through windows, brake lights, open doors and children emerging between cars. Leave door clearance and be ready to stop. A cyclist may swerve around a drain, pothole or parked vehicle.

### Buses, trams and passengers

The sequence for a scheduled bus or marked school bus using hazard lights is important:

1. A bus **approaching a stop with hazard lights** may not be overtaken.
2. Once **stopped at the stop with hazard lights**, pass only at walking speed, with enough lateral distance to exclude danger. Oncoming traffic on the same carriageway is also limited to walking speed.
3. Do not obstruct passengers; stop and wait when necessary.

A bus or tram stopped without hazard lights must still be passed cautiously. If passengers board or alight across your path, pass on the right only at walking speed and with enough distance, or wait. Expect people to run from behind the bus or cross to catch a tram. Allow a scheduled bus indicating its intention to leave a marked stop to move off; slow if needed.

Trams are normally passed on the right. Passing on the left is allowed only in limited layouts, such as where the rails are too far right or a one-way street makes the alternative necessary and safe. Never obstruct or endanger passengers.

### Common exam traps

- A green light does not remove the special duty toward children or vulnerable people.
- The horn is not a substitute for slowing and braking readiness.
- A bus approaching with hazard lights is different from one already stopped.
- Walking speed for a stopped hazard-light bus applies in both directions on the same carriageway.
- Do not assume somebody has seen you because they face your direction.

**Remember:** vulnerable or hidden person means early speed reduction, a covered brake and enough space to stop.

## 3. Road, weather and visibility

### The general adaptation rule

Choose speed and distance for the road, traffic, visibility, weather, vehicle, load and your own ability. Poor grip demands less speed, more distance and smoother steering, braking and acceleration. Never drive faster than the distance you can see and safely stop within.

On a normal road, be able to stop within the visible distance. On a dangerously narrow road where oncoming traffic may need the same space, be able to stop within **half** the visible distance. This allows both drivers to stop before meeting.

### Rain, wet roads and aquaplaning

Rain reduces tyre grip, lengthens braking distance, creates spray and hides potholes. The first rain after a dry period can mix with dust and oil to make the road especially slippery. Increase the following gap, reduce speed before bends and use gentle inputs.

Aquaplaning occurs when tyres ride on water and lose contact with the road. Risk rises with speed, water depth, worn tread and unsuitable pressure. Warning signs include very light steering, engine-speed changes without matching acceleration, or loss of directional response. Ease off the accelerator, depress the clutch in a manual vehicle if needed to remove drive, keep the steering nearly straight and avoid abrupt braking or steering until grip returns. ABS cannot prevent aquaplaning.

### Snow, ice and low-friction surfaces

Ice forms early on bridges, shaded areas, forest roads and exposed sections. Near freezing, a wet-looking surface may already be icy. Snow can narrow the usable road, hide markings and push pedestrians onto the carriageway. Leaves, mud, oil, loose gravel, wet rails and roadworks plates also reduce grip.

Slow before the slippery area. Avoid sudden braking, acceleration or steering. Cross rails and road edges at a safe angle where possible. With snow chains, the maximum is **50 km/h**. During black ice, packed snow, slush, ice or frost, the situational winter-tyre rule applies to all wheels, subject to legal exceptions.

The Alpine mountain-and-snowflake symbol identifies an approved winter tyre; it does not mean chains are automatically required. A tyre speed label such as “max. 190 km/h” applies whenever those tyres are fitted, including on dry roads.

### Fog, snow and heavy rain

If visibility through fog, snowfall or rain is below **50 m**, do not exceed **50 km/h**, and go slower when necessary. The rear fog lamp may be used only in fog with visibility below 50 m. Its brightness dazzles when used in ordinary rain or clear conditions. Front fog lamps are for serious visibility impairment caused by fog, snow or rain.

Use roadside delineator posts to estimate distance; they are usually about 50 m apart on rural roads. In fog, avoid following another vehicle too closely merely to use its lights as guidance.

### Darkness and glare

At dusk, darkness, in tunnels and in poor visibility, use dipped beam (*Abblendlicht*). Side or parking lights alone are not sufficient for driving. Main beam improves range on dark roads, but dip it early for oncoming traffic, when following closely, where continuous lighting is adequate, and whenever anybody could be dazzled.

If dazzled, look toward the right edge of the road, reduce speed and stop if necessary. Never retaliate with main beam. Dirty, wet or misted windows scatter light; clear every window before moving. Low sun, reflective wet roads and bright advertising can hide signals or people. Search for the actual traffic light and signs rather than following the brightest object.

After entering darkness, the eyes need time to adapt, so reduce speed first. Headlamp range also limits speed: with dipped beam you must be able to stop inside the illuminated distance. If a rear-heavy load raises the beam, use headlamp-range adjustment.

### Wind and wildlife

Crosswind is strongest on bridges, exposed roads, forest exits and when passing large vehicles. A lorry can briefly shelter your car; the force disappears alongside it and returns when you leave its shelter. Reduce speed, hold the wheel securely and correct smoothly.

At a wildlife warning sign, especially at dawn or dusk, slow and scan both sides. Animals often travel in groups. If wildlife appears, brake firmly, hold a straight course, dip main beam and use the horn if helpful. Do not make a violent swerve into oncoming traffic or a tree. After a collision, secure the scene, do not take the animal away, and notify the police or responsible authority.

### Assistance warnings in poor conditions

A flashing ESC/ESP lamp means the system is actively correcting instability: reduce speed and use smoother inputs. ACC, lane systems and cameras can be impaired by rain, snow, fog, darkness, dirt or a bend. Take control; a warning is not permission to continue unchanged.

### Common exam traps

- “Allowed maximum” is not always a safe speed.
- Rear fog lights are not general rain lights.
- On a narrow road the stopping distance may need to fit within half the visible distance.
- Aquaplaning calls for easing off and steady steering, not abrupt braking.
- ABS and ESC cannot create grip that the tyres and surface do not have.

**Remember:** less grip or less view means less speed, more gap and smoother control.

## 4. Speed, distance and driving physics

### Choosing speed

Inside built-up areas the general maximum is **50 km/h** unless a sign sets another limit. This is a ceiling, not a target. Reduce speed for children, unclear junctions, bends, narrow roads, poor surfaces, darkness, weather, heavy loads or limited view.

Outside built-up areas, vehicle-specific limits and signs apply. Cars without a trailer generally have a legal maximum of 100 km/h on ordinary roads. A car-trailer combination is normally limited to 80 km/h unless it qualifies for a higher authorised limit. On motorways and similar divided roads, **130 km/h** is the recommended speed (*Richtgeschwindigkeit*) for eligible cars and motorcycles under favourable conditions, not an entitlement or universal maximum.

Before a bend, reduce speed while travelling straight. Braking and steering both need tyre grip; heavy braking in the bend can exceed the available force. Steer smoothly, maintain an appropriate speed and accelerate only as the bend opens. Centrifugal demand rises approximately with the **square of speed** and falls as bend radius increases. Double the speed means about four times the lateral force.

After long high-speed travel, lower speeds may feel deceptively slow. Check the speedometer before exits, roadworks and town signs. On a motorway exit, signal early but brake mainly after entering the deceleration lane.

### Reaction, braking and stopping formulas

The theory-test estimates use speed in km/h:

| Quantity | Formula | Example at 50 km/h |
|---|---:|---:|
| Reaction distance (*Reaktionsweg*) | `(speed ÷ 10) × 3` | `5 × 3 = 15 m` |
| Normal braking distance (*Bremsweg*) | `(speed ÷ 10)²` | `5² = 25 m` |
| Emergency braking distance | normal braking distance ÷ 2 | `25 ÷ 2 = 12.5 m` |
| Normal stopping distance (*Anhalteweg*) | reaction + normal braking | `15 + 25 = 40 m` |
| Emergency stopping estimate | reaction + emergency braking | `15 + 12.5 = 27.5 m` |

These are exam approximations for normal reaction and conditions. Real distance grows with fatigue, distraction, alcohol, poor tyres, wet or icy roads, downhill gradient, vehicle mass and brake condition. Increasing speed has a disproportionate effect: double the speed gives roughly four times the braking distance before other factors.

### Following distance

The legal principle is to leave enough space to stop behind the vehicle ahead if it brakes suddenly. The standard exam rule of thumb is:

`following distance in metres ≈ half the speedometer reading`

At 50 km/h this is about 25 m; at 100 km/h, about 50 m. A practical time check is roughly two seconds in good conditions. Increase it when visibility or grip is poor, when towing, when tired, or when the lead vehicle blocks your view. A larger gap improves forward vision and gives earlier warning.

In slow-moving urban traffic on a dry road at 50 km/h, the specific exam minimum is **15 m or about three car lengths**. Outside built-up areas, use the normal half-speedometer rule. These simplified checks do not override the basic duty to leave enough room to stop; increase the gap for poor conditions.

If another vehicle enters your safety gap, ease off and brake progressively as necessary to rebuild it. Do not retaliate or continue at unchanged speed. When being overtaken, never accelerate; reduce speed if that helps the overtaker return safely.

Outside built-up areas, specified speed-restricted motor vehicles, and combinations **over 7 m** long, must leave enough space for an overtaking vehicle to merge between them except while overtaking themselves or where multiple lanes are available in their direction.

### Emergency braking and evasive action

In an immediate hazard, brake decisively. With ABS, press the brake firmly and keep pressure on it; the pedal may pulse. ABS prevents wheel lock and largely preserves steering, allowing braking and steering around an obstacle where space exists. It does not always shorten the distance, prevent aquaplaning or make high cornering speed safe.

Avoid unnecessary swerving. A straight emergency stop is usually more controllable, particularly for wildlife. Evasive steering is appropriate only when a clear escape path exists and it avoids a greater danger.

### Gradient, load and trailers

Downhill travel adds gravitational force and can overheat brakes. Select a lower gear early and use engine braking; do not ride the service brake continuously. A trailer pushes the towing vehicle downhill, increases stopping distance and may cut across the inside of a bend. A roof load raises the centre of gravity, increases body roll and sensitivity to wind, and requires smoother, slower driving.

### Common exam traps

- Reaction distance and braking distance are different; stopping distance includes both.
- The half-speedometer rule estimates following distance, not braking distance.
- Doubling speed does not merely double braking distance; it roughly quadruples it.
- ABS preserves steering but does not defeat aquaplaning or guarantee a shorter stop.
- The posted limit, recommended motorway speed and safe speed are three different ideas.

**Remember:** speed steals time twice—there is more distance during reaction and much more distance during braking.

## 5. Road position, lanes and overtaking

### Use the correct part of the road

Drive on the carriageway and as far right as reasonably possible, especially with oncoming traffic, near crests and bends, or when visibility is poor. The shoulder is not normally part of the carriageway and is not an extra overtaking lane. Use it only for an emergency or when signs expressly open it as a lane. Slow rural vehicles may have a special duty to use a suitable paved shoulder where the marking rule applies so others can pass.

Keep sufficient distance from the edge, parked vehicles and obstacles. On a narrow road, cooperate and use a passing place when needed. If the obstacle is on your side, you normally wait for oncoming traffic. Signs may reverse this: the red-arrow sign means your direction waits; the blue rectangular sign with the large white arrow gives your direction priority, but only while the way is safely clear.

Do not cross a solid line to correct a missed turn or exit. Continue legally and reroute. Hatched areas and traffic islands guide traffic; do not enter unless markings or circumstances lawfully allow it.

### Lane choice

Outside built-up areas, keep right. On a carriageway with three lanes in one direction, the middle lane may be used continuously when traffic appears on the right from time to time. The left lane remains mainly for overtaking.

Inside built-up areas, motor vehicles up to **3.5 t** may generally choose freely among marked lanes in one direction, except on motorways. They may also move faster on the right than traffic on the left. Free choice does not remove the normal lane-change duty: signal and exclude danger.

Follow lane arrows early. If you enter a wrong turn-only lane, take the indicated route and correct later. Do not force into another lane at the junction. In a tunnel, stay between the markings and never turn around.

### Changing lanes and zipper merging

Before every lane change:

1. check the interior and relevant exterior mirror;
2. signal clearly;
3. make a direct blind-spot check;
4. move only when nobody is endangered; and
5. cancel the signal and establish a safe gap.

At an ending or blocked lane, use both lanes up to just before the narrowing, then merge alternately one vehicle from each lane (*Reißverschlussverfahren*). Merging too early wastes road space; forcing in before a safe opening is also wrong.

An acceleration lane at a motorway entrance is different: motorway traffic has priority, so the entering driver must adjust speed and find a safe gap. There is no zipper entitlement. A deceleration lane belongs to the exit manoeuvre; enter it first, then reduce speed.

### Overtaking: the complete test

Overtake only when all of these are true:

- the entire necessary route is visible and free;
- no prohibition, solid line, crossing or other hazard forbids it;
- your speed is substantially higher, so the manoeuvre will be brief;
- nobody behind has started overtaking and nobody ahead is signalling left; and
- there is enough space to pass and return without endangering or obstructing anyone.

Normally overtake on the left. Check mirrors and blind spot, signal before moving out, preserve lateral distance, and do not exceed the speed limit. Return right as soon as safely clear, signal the return and do not cut in. A small speed advantage, hidden crest, bend, junction, crossing, oncoming vehicle or approaching solid line means stay behind.

Outside built-up areas, a brief horn or headlight flash may announce an overtaking intention without dazzling. It does not create priority. Inside built-up areas, warning signals are for danger, not announcing an overtake.

Do not overtake at a pedestrian crossing, where the traffic situation is unclear, or where a bus is approaching its stop with hazard lights. Respect no-overtaking signs and the vehicle categories shown. If a new danger appears, abort early: signal right, reduce speed and return behind when safe.

When overtaking bicycles, pedestrians or e-scooters, keep at least 1.5 m inside and 2 m outside built-up areas. For a motorcyclist or unstable cyclist, allow for side movement and wind. Never squeeze through a narrowing.

Trams are normally overtaken on the right. Use the left only when the layout and rules permit it. Pass stopped trams cautiously and protect passengers.

Treat riders and horse-drawn vehicles with exceptional quiet and space. Keep right when meeting them, reduce speed, avoid horn or unnecessary engine noise and leave maximum lateral clearance because a horse can shy suddenly. When overtaking, watch the rider or carriage driver: a raised arm, signalling paddle or fitted indicator can announce a turn or change of direction. Do not pass until the signal and route are clear.

For one exam worksheet, a car at 100 km/h overtakes a 70 km/h lorry. Add the lorry and car lengths plus about 50 m safety gap at each end, giving roughly 117 m. Account for the 30 km/h relative speed:

`100 ÷ (100 − 70) × 117 ≈ 390 m`, rounded to about **400 m**.

Near a crest or similarly unclear area, the worksheet doubles the visible route and gives **800 m**. This is a teaching estimate, not a universal legal number. The governing rule remains: do not begin unless the complete route is visible, free and long enough to pass and return safely.

### When others overtake you

Keep right and never accelerate. Maintain a predictable course. If an overtaker misjudges and needs a return gap, reduce speed to avoid the collision. Large vehicles and trailers need more distance and may disappear in blind areas; avoid travelling beside a lorry longer than necessary.

If you are driving a slow vehicle and a queue forms, enable others to pass at a suitable safe place. This does not require pulling onto an unsafe shoulder or stopping where visibility is poor.

### One-way streets and restricted lanes

Follow the indicated direction on a one-way street even when the road ahead is blocked. Wait or reroute; never reverse through the street as a substitute route. A bicycle may be allowed against the general direction only when supplementary signs permit it.

A cycle lane with a solid boundary is reserved. A protective cycle strip (*Schutzstreifen*) with a broken boundary may be crossed only when necessary and without endangering cyclists; stopping on it is prohibited. Bus lanes, cycle streets and pedestrian zones follow their signs and permitted exceptions.

### Common exam traps

- A shoulder is not a general driving or overtaking lane.
- Zipper merging applies at the actual lane reduction, but not as priority on a motorway entrance.
- Free lane choice inside towns is limited to marked lanes, eligible vehicles and non-motorway roads.
- A clear road immediately ahead is not enough; the whole overtaking route must be clear.
- Being overtaken means never accelerating, even if the other driver is wrong.

**Remember:** right lane, full observation, clear route, safe distance—and no manoeuvre that depends on another person braking for you.

## 6. Junctions, priority and turning

### Priority order

Use this order when deciding who goes first:

1. police instructions;
2. traffic lights;
3. priority signs;
4. the general rule “right before left” (*rechts vor links*); and
5. turning and manoeuvring duties.

Even when released, enter only if you can clear the junction. Never queue across an intersection, pedestrian crossing or level crossing. If priority is unclear, slow down, observe and communicate; do not force the issue.

### Right before left and its exceptions

At an unsigned junction, traffic from the right normally has priority (*Vorfahrt*). Road width, paving, brightness or apparent importance does not decide it. A warning sign showing a black junction can alert you that right before left applies.

Right before left does not give priority to a vehicle emerging from a field or forest path, property, car park, petrol station, traffic-calmed area, lowered kerb or across a pavement. That vehicle has a heightened duty to exclude danger and must wait for all road users, including pedestrians and cyclists.

### Priority signs

- **Yield:** slow and give priority; stop if necessary. The broken give-way line shows where to wait but does not replace observation.
- **STOP:** come to a complete standstill at the stop line. If no line exists, stop where the other road can be seen. If the view from the stop line is blocked, stop there first and edge forward for a second observation stop.
- **Priority at the next junction:** applies only to the next junction or crossing.
- **Priority road:** applies continuously until ended. A thick line on a supplementary diagram shows its course.
- **End of priority road:** normal priority rules apply afterward.

If a priority road bends, drivers following it must signal the physical change in direction; drivers leaving it straight ahead do not signal merely because they leave the thick line. Everyone must still observe turning conflicts and vulnerable users.

### Turning right or left

Use the sequence: observe, signal, position, recheck, yield, turn.

Position right for a right turn and toward the centre for a left turn without entering oncoming space. Protect cyclists and e-scooter users travelling straight beside you and give special consideration to pedestrians crossing the road you enter. Check the blind spot immediately before crossing their path.

A left-turning driver yields to oncoming traffic travelling straight or turning right, including bicycles and trams. Two opposing left-turners normally pass in front of each other, unless markings, vehicle size or layout require turning behind each other. In multi-lane turns, remain in the selected lane.

At a roundabout signed with the roundabout and yield signs, yield before entering. Do not signal on entry; signal right before leaving. Vehicles already in the roundabout have priority. If the signs are absent, ordinary junction rules may apply.

### Starting, entering and leaving the roadside

When starting from the kerb or entering from private land, exclude danger. Check mirrors, signal, make a shoulder check and wait for a safe gap. Cross pavements and cycle paths only after pedestrians and cyclists pass. If buildings or parked vehicles block the view, obtain guidance from another person; the guide must remain visible and give clear signals.

A bus leaving a marked stop should be enabled to move off, but it must not force its way into danger. Cooperation does not cancel observation.

### U-turning and reversing

Turning around and reversing are high-risk manoeuvres. Move slowly, observe all around continuously and exclude danger to others. Use a guide where the rear area cannot be seen; stop immediately if the guide disappears. Do not reverse into an unseen road or across a busy pavement on assumption alone.

U-turning and reversing are prohibited on motorways and motor roads (*Kraftfahrstraßen*). In a tunnel, never turn around. Missing a route means continue to a lawful place to change direction.

### Special formations and communication

A properly marked closed convoy is treated as one unit; do not split it. A person apparently waiving priority should be trusted only when the communication is clear. Recheck cyclists, pedestrians and other traffic because one person's gesture cannot waive somebody else's rights.

### Common exam traps

- The wider or brighter road is not automatically the priority road.
- A green light releases movement but does not remove turning duties.
- A vehicle from a lowered kerb or property waits even if it comes from the right.
- Following a bending priority road requires a signal because the vehicle changes direction.
- Entering a blocked junction is wrong even with green or priority.

**Remember:** police, lights, signs, right before left—then check who is turning or entering from outside the road.

## 7. Signs, markings, lights and police

### Read signs as a system

Identify the sign's family, symbol, direction and any supplementary plate. A supplementary plate can limit distance, time, direction, vehicle type, residents or exceptions. Temporary signs and yellow markings normally take precedence over permanent signs and white markings where they conflict.

Triangular red-bordered signs warn of danger. Round red-bordered signs prohibit or limit. Round blue signs prescribe an action. Rectangular signs usually guide, give information or establish a zone. The safest exam response is not merely naming a sign: state the behaviour it requires.

### Danger signs

Outside built-up areas, a danger is usually **150–250 m** beyond the sign unless a supplementary plate gives another distance. Slow early, increase attention and prepare to brake.

Common recognition cues and responses include:

- **Bend or double bend:** reduce speed before entering, keep right and expect oncoming traffic to cut the corner.
- **Steep descent or ascent:** the percentage is rise or fall per 100 m. Select a low gear before a descent and protect the brakes.
- **Uneven road, slippery road, loose gravel or movable bridge:** use smooth controls and allow longer braking distance.
- **Road narrows, one side narrows or roadworks:** check which side is obstructed, reduce speed and apply signed or default meeting priority.
- **Crosswind:** expect a sudden sideways force, especially after shelter.
- **Traffic lights:** expect a signal that may be hidden or newly installed.
- **Pedestrians, children or cyclists:** search both sides and be ready for unpredictable entry.
- **Wild animals or livestock:** slow and expect more than one animal.
- **Queue:** reduce speed early, increase the gap and warn following traffic with hazard lights when appropriate.
- **Level crossing:** approach ready to stop. Three striped beacons mean about 240 m, two 160 m and one 80 m.

### Regulatory signs

Important sign meanings include:

- A red circle with a vehicle symbol prohibits the shown class. “No vehicles” applies broadly, while a motor-vehicle prohibition is narrower. A bicycle or motorcycle may generally be pushed where driving it is prohibited.
- A red ring containing a mass, axle-load, width, height or length value prohibits vehicles whose **actual** measurement exceeds it. Width includes mirrors and load; height and length include the load or combination.
- A blue arrow prescribes the direction or side to pass. Signal if following it involves a real turn.
- A maximum-speed sign sets a ceiling; a minimum-speed sign requires at least that speed only while conditions and vehicle capability safely permit it.
- A snow-chain sign requires chains on the driven wheels as instructed; with chains, maximum 50 km/h.
- A no-overtaking sign applies to the vehicle categories depicted. The standard car-symbol prohibition prevents multi-track motor vehicles from overtaking motor vehicles; a heavy-vehicle version normally targets vehicles over 3.5 t and tractors, with supplementary wording able to include buses or cars with trailers.
- **No stopping** prohibits voluntary stopping. **No parking** permits only a brief stop of no more than three minutes or active boarding, alighting, loading or unloading while the driver remains able to move.
- A bicycle path, shared path or separated pedestrian/cycle path prescribes the indicated use. Watch the dividing symbol and protect users when crossing it.
- A bicycle street or bicycle zone generally has a maximum of 30 km/h and prioritises cycling; motor traffic needs permission and must adapt.
- A traffic-calmed area requires walking speed, gives pedestrians use of the whole area, forbids unnecessary obstruction, and permits parking only in marked spaces. On exit, wait for all traffic.
- “No entry” bars entry from that direction; it does not mean the road is closed in both directions.

### Directional and information signs

The priority-at-next-junction sign applies once; the yellow diamond establishes a continuing priority road; the crossed diamond ends it. A town-entry sign starts built-up-area rules, normally including 50 km/h; the back of the exit sign ends them.

Motorway and motor-road signs allow only motor vehicles whose design maximum speed is **more than 60 km/h**. Their end signs announce that special rules cease. A tunnel sign requires dipped headlights, even by day. An emergency-telephone marker arrow points toward the nearest emergency telephone, not the next exit.

Parking signs and supplementary plates show permitted area, orientation, users, times or whether a disc or ticket is needed. An accessible space requires the qualifying EU parking card displayed for the eligible person, who may be the passenger rather than the driver.

Service symbols identify the facility shown: a white cross or first-aid symbol directs to a **first-aid station**; a fuel-pump-and-plug symbol identifies an **electric-vehicle charging station**; and a customs symbol marks a **customs point** where stopping and instructions may be required. A small red-and-white band on a lamp post identifies a **part-night street lamp** that is not lit all night. When parking there in darkness, provide the vehicle lighting required for an unlit road rather than relying on the lamp.

A one-way arrow sets the permitted direction. A blue route direction, motorway exit number or destination board guides but does not override a closure, police instruction or current road layout. The road always beats outdated navigation data.

A round blue pedestrian symbol prescribes a footpath; vehicles may not use it unless an exception is signed. On shared or separated walking/cycling paths, the arrangement of symbols shows whether users share space or have designated sides. A zone prohibition, such as a no-parking zone, applies throughout the zone until its matching end sign, not only beside the entry sign.

Other information signs are best read literally. A cul-de-sac symbol means no through route, though walking or cycling continuation may be shown. A yellow route-number sign identifies a federal road (*Bundesstraße*). A numbered diversion arrow belongs to that numbered detour. Motorway exit numbers are consecutive orientation numbers, not speed or distance. A traffic-calming, pedestrian or cycle-zone sign starts a set of zone rules that continues until its matching end sign.

Some danger symbols describe the physical edge of the road: a bank or quay symbol warns that the road leads toward open water; roadworks means workers, machinery, dirt, narrowed lanes and changed routing; sidewind means sudden steering effect; falling-rock or uneven-surface signs call for speed reduction and increased observation. Read any distance or length plate separately: “100 m” may mean the hazard begins in 100 m, while arrows or wording can indicate its length.

Red-and-white diagonal boards can mark an obstruction, parked trailer or the outer outline of an exceptionally wide vehicle. They do not identify dangerous goods. A red-and-white plate on the traffic side of a parked trailer makes its boundary visible at night. An orange panel marks regulated dangerous goods, and a white plate with black “A” marks waste transport.

### Road markings

- A **solid line** must not be crossed or straddled. If it blocks a planned route, continue and reroute.
- A **broken lane line** may be crossed only after a safe lane-change check.
- A **stop line** marks where a STOP sign or red light requires stopping.
- A **give-way line** marks the waiting position but does not create the duty by itself.
- **Directional arrows** bind the lane at the junction; choose early.
- **Hatched areas** separate streams and are normally kept clear.
- **Yellow temporary markings** replace conflicting white markings.
- Triangular “shark teeth” highlight an existing duty to yield; they do not invent a separate priority rule.

Keep at least **3 m** of usable lane beside a solid line when parking. Never cover markings needed by others.

### Traffic lights

Green releases traffic but only when the junction can be cleared. Turning duties remain. Yellow means wait before the junction: stop if a normal safe stop is possible; never accelerate merely to beat it. Red means stop at the line or before the junction.

Flashing yellow warns of danger or an inactive signal; signs and general priority rules decide movement. A lit directional arrow releases only its direction. Bicycle symbols apply to bicycle traffic addressed by them.

A static green-arrow plate beside a red light permits an optional right turn only after a **complete stop**, careful checks, and yielding to all released traffic and pedestrians. It never requires turning.

Lane-control signals above the carriageway apply to the lane beneath them: a red crossed symbol closes it, a green downward arrow opens it, and a flashing diagonal arrow tells traffic to change in that direction. Do not stop in a lane with permanently illuminated control signals.

### Police instructions

Police instructions outrank lights, signs and general rules:

- An officer facing you with chest or back means stop before the junction.
- Seeing the officer's side/profile means your direction may proceed carefully.
- One arm raised means all directions wait; traffic already inside clears the junction.
- “STOP POLIZEI” means stop safely as directed.
- “BITTE FOLGEN” means follow the police vehicle, do not pass it, and stop when it stops.

Instructions change who may move, not the duty to avoid danger.

### Blue and yellow flashing lights

Blue light **with siren** orders everyone to clear the way immediately. Locate the vehicle, reduce speed and make a safe passage without an automatic panic stop. From behind, move as far right as safely possible. In a queue, form the emergency corridor (*Rettungsgasse*) between the far-left lane and every lane to its right: left lane moves left; all others move right.

Blue light alone warns of an emergency deployment or specified operation but does not carry the same immediate clear-way command. Facilitate it calmly. Yellow flashing lights warn of a hazard, unusual vehicle or work site: slow, give space and expect an obstruction.

### Common exam traps

- A supplementary plate can completely change who, when or where a sign applies.
- A STOP sign always requires actual wheel standstill.
- Yellow temporary markings beat conflicting white ones.
- A green arrow at red requires a full stop and yielding; it is optional.
- Blue light alone and blue light with siren do not impose identical duties.
- Sign shape identifies the family, but the symbol and plate decide the exact rule.

**Remember:** identify family, symbol, plate and direction—then translate the sign into speed, position, priority or prohibition.

## 8. Stopping, parking and leaving the vehicle

### Stopping versus parking

Stopping (*Halten*) is a voluntary interruption lasting no more than **3 minutes** while the driver does not leave the vehicle. It becomes parking (*Parken*) when the driver leaves or waits longer than three minutes, except where active boarding, alighting, loading or unloading rules apply. Being forced to stop by traffic, a red light or a queue is not voluntary stopping.

Never stop at narrow or unclear places, sharp bends, merge or diverge lanes, on level crossings, in marked fire-service access, on motorways or motor roads including the hard shoulder, or wherever signs prohibit it. Do not park where it blocks signs, entrances, lowered kerbs, emergency access, tracks, cycle facilities or required traffic width.

### Important distances

- Pedestrian crossing: no stopping on it or within **5 m before** it.
- Junction or intersection: generally no parking within **5 m** of the carriageway edges; where a cycle path runs on the right, keep **8 m before** and 5 m after the intersection clear.
- Andreaskreuz at a level crossing: no parking within **5 m inside built-up areas** or **50 m outside** on either side.
- Bus-stop sign: no parking within **15 m before or after** it.
- Keep **10 m before** a traffic light, STOP sign or Andreaskreuz when the parked vehicle would conceal it.
- Leave at least **3 m** of carriageway beside a solid boundary line.

These distances do not make parking legal if another prohibition or obstruction applies.

### Where and how to park

Park on the right edge in the direction of travel. Left-side parking is allowed on a one-way street and where rails on the right make right-side parking impractical. Use marked bays efficiently and do not occupy more space than necessary. The first driver who reaches a parking space has priority, including while manoeuvring into it, but everyone must cooperate safely.

Where ordinary stopping in a second row would obstruct traffic, it remains prohibited. A taxi may stop in the second row briefly for passengers to board or alight only when traffic conditions permit and nobody is endangered or unreasonably obstructed; it is not a general taxi parking privilege.

Pavement parking is permitted only when signs or markings allow it and only for eligible vehicles; the relevant maximum permissible mass is **2.8 t**. Do not leave wheels on the pavement merely to widen the road.

An unhitched trailer may normally remain in the same public parking place for no more than **two weeks**. Trailers over 2 t and certain heavy vehicles have additional night, Sunday and holiday restrictions in specified built-up areas.

### Parking discs, meters and tickets

Where a parking disc (*Parkscheibe*) is required, set it to the next half-hour mark after arrival. Arrival at 10:40 means set **11:00**. Display it clearly and obey the maximum duration.

At a working ticket machine, obtain and display a valid readable ticket and leave by expiry. If a meter still has paid time, it may be used. If the machine or meter is defective, use a parking disc and obey the stated maximum duration. Without activating a device, only boarding, alighting, loading or unloading may be allowed, and all other restrictions still apply.

### Securing and leaving the vehicle

Before opening any door, check mirrors and blind spot. On leaving, prevent rolling with parking brake and the correct gear or selector position; use wheel chocks where required. Switch off, remove the key where appropriate, close windows and lock doors and luggage compartments. Never leave children or animals in dangerous heat or cold.

### Common exam traps

- More than three minutes or leaving the vehicle usually means parking.
- Hazard lights do not legalise double parking or a prohibited stop.
- A defective ticket machine does not create unlimited free parking.
- Pavement parking needs permission; apparent spare space is not enough.
- A numeric clearance rule does not override a stricter local prohibition.

**Remember:** ask whether the stop is voluntary, whether it became parking, and whether location, distance, sign and remaining width all permit it.

## 9. Special roads and traffic situations

### Motorways and motor roads

An *Autobahn* and a motor road (*Kraftfahrstraße*) may be used only by motor vehicles whose design maximum speed is **more than 60 km/h**; a trailer being towed must also be suitable for the route. This eligibility figure is not the speed you must drive.

On a motorway, keep right and overtake on the left. Do not stop, park, reverse or turn around, including on the hard shoulder. Walking is prohibited. Use the shoulder only for a genuine emergency or when signs open it as a lane.

At an entrance, use the acceleration lane to approach traffic speed, observe motorway traffic and merge into a safe gap. Through traffic has priority. If no gap exists, wait rather than forcing entry. At an exit, signal early, enter the deceleration lane and then reduce speed. Long high-speed driving distorts speed perception, so check the speedometer.

The recommended motorway speed (*Richtgeschwindigkeit*) of 130 km/h is for eligible vehicles in favourable conditions. Weather, traffic, view, roadworks, tyres or towing may require far less. Temporary signs and yellow markings at roadworks override conflicting permanent arrangements.

If warned of a wrong-way driver, reduce speed without harsh surprise, move as far right as safely possible, greatly increase the gap and do not overtake. Watch ramps and the left side for the oncoming vehicle, warn others if necessary, leave the motorway at the next safe exit or service area, and follow police/radio instructions. If you discover the danger first, notify police hands-free or from a safe stop; never attempt to block or pursue the vehicle.

When approaching a queue, slow early, increase distance and use hazard lights to warn behind. As soon as traffic slows to walking speed or stops, create a rescue corridor between the leftmost lane and all lanes to its right. Do not wait until an emergency vehicle appears and do not use the corridor yourself.

If a vehicle breaks down, move to the shoulder or an emergency bay if possible, use hazard lights, put on a warning vest before entering the traffic side, leave the vehicle away from traffic and place the triangle at an adequate distance. Occupants should wait behind a barrier where possible. A vehicle towed after breaking down on the motorway must leave at the next exit; a vehicle being towed from elsewhere may not enter the motorway.

### Tunnels

Use dipped headlights, even by day, and remove sunglasses if they impair the view. Observe height limits, lane signals, gaps and speed. Know the emergency bays, telephones and exits. Do not stop except for traffic or emergency, and never reverse or turn around.

In a queue, switch on hazard lights, keep distance and switch off the engine during a long standstill when appropriate. After a breakdown, use an emergency bay if reachable, hazards on, engine off, leave safely and call from an emergency telephone where possible because it identifies the location.

When tunnel traffic comes to a standstill, leave a **5 m standstill gap** to the vehicle ahead. The space reduces collision risk, allows manoeuvring and gives emergency access. Do not close it merely because traffic behind stops.

For smoke or fire, do not rely on saving the vehicle. Stop as safely as possible, hazards on, engine off, leave the key, alert the control centre and move through the marked emergency exit away from smoke. Help others only without losing your own escape route.

### Level crossings

Approach a level crossing at moderate speed, look and listen, and be able to stop. Wait when a red or yellow signal shows, barriers lower or are closed, staff order a stop, or a train is approaching. Stop before the Andreaskreuz or at the marked waiting point without blocking a junction.

An open barrier is not proof that no train is coming. Enter only when the tracks can be crossed completely without stopping. Never follow a queue onto the rails. After one train, wait until all lights, barriers and staff release traffic; another train may follow on a second track. Never drive around a barrier or bypass a failed installation. Report a fault.

If a sign combination sets **10 km/h** at the level crossing, that posted maximum applies through the indicated section. It is still only a ceiling: use less when view, surface or traffic requires it.

Near a crossing, the three-, two- and one-stripe beacons usually indicate about 240 m, 160 m and 80 m. Parking clearance around the Andreaskreuz is 5 m inside towns and 50 m outside; overtaking restrictions and speed adaptation may also apply.

### Large vehicles, trams and blind areas

Lorries and buses have large blind areas in front, behind and beside them. If you cannot see the driver's mirrors, assume the driver may not see you. Do not remain beside a large vehicle near a turn. It may move left before turning right, need the full lane, hide signals or pedestrians, and its rear wheels cut inside the front-wheel path.

Agricultural vehicles may be much wider than their visible front lights suggest and can carry projecting equipment. A farm tractor may lawfully tow two wide trailers under its special exception, unlike an ordinary car. Reduce speed, keep right and judge the full combination before meeting or overtaking it.

Trams cannot steer around an obstruction and need a long stopping distance. Keep tracks clear, do not stop inside their swept path and wait outside a narrowing. A turning lorry or tram may need more room than expected; patience is safer than squeezing into the gap.

### Traffic obstructions and exceptional transport

Anyone who causes dangerous mud, oil, cargo or objects on the road must remove them immediately where safe. Until removal, secure and mark the danger and illuminate it when necessary. If clearing it is unsafe, protect yourself, warn traffic and contact police or road services.

Oversized or unusually heavy transport requires permission and must follow its approved route, times, escort and marking conditions. A supplementary plate stating “200 m” normally gives the distance to the start of a restriction, not its length.

An illegal motor race is both extreme danger and a criminal offence, including racing alone to achieve maximum speed. It can cause serious crashes, imprisonment, licence confiscation or withdrawal, and much greater penalties when people or valuable property are endangered.

The Sunday and public-holiday goods-transport ban generally runs **00:00–22:00** and covers lorries over 7.5 t and any lorry with a trailer when carrying goods commercially or for payment, subject to exceptions. It does not automatically cover a passenger car with trailer.

### Common exam traps

- Design speed over 60 km/h determines motorway eligibility; it is not a minimum travel speed.
- Motorway traffic does not owe an entering vehicle a zipper merge.
- An open level-crossing barrier does not replace looking and listening.
- Never enter tracks, a junction or a crossing when the exit is blocked.
- Large vehicles need turning space and may hide people, signals and load hazards.

**Remember:** special roads reward early planning—correct lane, enough gap, clear exit and no improvised reversal.

## 10. Breakdowns, accidents and emergencies

### Securing a breakdown

If a stationary vehicle cannot be recognised in time, switch on hazard lights immediately. Move it out of the traffic flow when possible. Put on a warning vest before stepping into danger and place a warning triangle far enough away to warn approaching traffic. About **100 m** is a useful orientation on fast rural roads; bends, crests or motorways may require more.

Stand away from the carriageway and never work on the traffic side if it is unsafe. A warning device is not a shield: personal safety comes first. A motorway emergency telephone marker arrow points to the nearest phone.

### Towing a broken-down vehicle

Use towing only for a genuine breakdown, not ordinary transport. Both vehicles use hazard lights. A rope must remain taut, be clearly marked and the gap must not exceed **5 m**. Agree signals and route before moving. Without engine assistance, steering becomes heavy and the brake pedal needs much more force.

The tow must leave a motorway at the next exit; do not tow onto a motorway from elsewhere. Motorcycles are transported rather than towed. Electric vehicles and automatics may be damaged by ordinary towing, so follow manufacturer instructions or use professional recovery.

### The accident sequence

Use this order:

1. **Stop.** Never leave the scene.
2. **Secure.** Hazard lights, vest, triangle and protection from traffic.
3. **Assess.** Number of injured people, hazards, fire, leaking substances and location.
4. **Help.** Give first aid within your ability.
5. **Call.** Use **112** for fire/rescue throughout the EU; in Germany **110** reaches police.

Make the emergency call early. Give location, what happened, number of injured people and special hazards, then answer questions and wait for instructions. Emergency call systems (*eCall*) can automatically send location and accident time after a serious crash; occupants can also trigger an urgent call manually.

For minor damage, first secure and document, then move vehicles aside promptly so they do not create another hazard. Exchange names, addresses, registration and insurance details. Photograph positions and damage where safe. The European Accident Statement is multilingual factual documentation, not an admission of liability.

If you damage an unattended vehicle, waiting briefly and leaving a note alone are not enough. Wait an appropriate time, leave identifying details and report promptly to police so the owner can be found.

### First aid essentials

Protect yourself before approaching. Check response and normal breathing. Call 112 or direct a specific person to call. If the person is not breathing normally, begin chest compressions and follow emergency instructions; use an AED when available. If unconscious but breathing normally, place them in the recovery position and monitor breathing. Control severe bleeding with direct pressure and keep injured people warm. Do not remove a motorcyclist's helmet casually, but airway and breathing take priority when lifesaving action is needed.

Do not give food, drink, alcohol or medication to a seriously injured person. Do not move them unless traffic, fire or another immediate danger makes it necessary.

### Fire, dangerous goods and wildlife

An orange panel identifies regulated dangerous-goods transport. If numbers are visible, report them to emergency services from a safe distance. Stay upwind, avoid leaks and ignition sources, and do not touch unknown substances. A black “A” plate identifies waste transport; red-and-white boards often mark vehicle outline or projection and are not dangerous-goods panels.

For a vehicle fire, stop, switch off, evacuate and call. Use an extinguisher only on a small fire with a clear escape path. In a tunnel, move to an emergency exit rather than remaining in smoke.

After a wildlife collision, secure the scene and inform police or the responsible authority. Do not take the animal away or follow an injured animal into danger.

### Common exam traps

- Secure yourself and traffic before focusing on property damage.
- A warning triangle belongs far enough before the danger, not beside the bumper.
- A note on an unattended car does not replace waiting and reporting.
- Hazard lights are required on both vehicles during towing.
- Orange panels, projecting-load markers and waste plates mean different things.

**Remember:** stop—secure—assess—help—call; protect life before vehicle or cargo.

## 11. Licensing, documents and vehicle legality

### Documents and insurance

Carry the driving licence (*Führerschein*) and registration certificate Part I (*Zulassungsbescheinigung Teil I*) while driving. Part II is the ownership/title document and is not the routine document to carry in the car. A public-road vehicle needs valid registration, operating permission and liability insurance.

The driver checks roadworthiness; the keeper must not permit unsafe or unlawful use. Changes to ownership, address, insurance or reportable vehicle data must be notified as required.

### Modifications and operating permission

Modified wheels, suspension, lighting, engine or other components may require approved parts, inspection, acceptance and updated papers. Assess a modification promptly rather than waiting for the next periodic inspection. Carry proof where required and update Part I when directed.

If the operating permit (*Betriebserlaubnis*) expires because a modification creates danger, worsens regulated emissions/noise or changes the approved vehicle type, do not continue normal public-road operation. A technically possible modification is not automatically legal.

### Main inspection

The next main inspection (*Hauptuntersuchung*, HU) date appears on the rear-number-plate tag and in Part I. A registered trailer remains due even if unused. For a car already three years old and for later inspections, the normal interval is two years. A used-car purchase does not restart the period; follow the existing due date.

**Memory tip:** plate, papers, then two years.

### Probation and offences

The normal probationary period (*Probezeit*) is **2 years**. A serious A offence or two B offences can lead to an entry and an advanced seminar (*Aufbauseminar für Fahranfänger*, ASF), extending probation to **4 years**. The seminar develops risk-aware, considerate driving; it does not erase points. Ignoring an ASF order can lead to licence withdrawal.

Unsupervised learner practice on public roads is driving without the required licence. It can cause criminal proceedings and a review of fitness before issue.

For register entries, common repayment periods are **2 years 6 months** for one-point offences, **5 years** for two-point offences, and **10 years** for offences in the three-point withdrawal category. The period normally begins when the decision becomes final; each entry runs independently.

### Regulated commercial driving and tachographs

Private Class B driving is normally outside commercial drivers' hours rules, but a Class B vehicle or combination can enter their scope in regulated goods transport. Since **1 July 2026**, this includes qualifying international transport or cabotage where the vehicle's permissible maximum mass, including trailer, exceeds 2.5 t. Specific exemptions exist, including conditions for some own-account transport where driving is not the person's main activity, so determine the operation before applying the limits.

For an in-scope driver, the core limits are:

- daily driving normally no more than **9 hours**, extendable to 10 hours no more than twice in a week;
- weekly driving no more than **56 hours**;
- total driving in two consecutive weeks no more than **90 hours**;
- after 4.5 hours of driving, a break of at least **45 minutes**, which may be split into at least 15 minutes followed by at least 30 minutes;
- regular daily rest at least **11 hours**, or split into at least 3 hours plus 9 hours; a reduced daily rest is at least 9 hours under its conditions; and
- regular weekly rest at least **45 hours**, with a conditional reduced weekly rest of at least 24 hours and compensation rules.

A tachograph records driving, other work, availability, breaks and rest. Insert and use only your personal driver card; never share, falsify or omit it. Select the correct mode and make manual entries for qualifying activity not captured automatically. During a roadside check, the driver must be able to produce the current day's records and the **preceding 56 days**, including required record sheets, manual records and printouts. If a card or unit fails, follow the printout/manual-record procedure and arrange repair or replacement promptly. A warning that the continuous-driving limit is near is a prompt to take a lawful break, not to search for a place after the limit expires.

### Receiving, carrying and delivering goods

Before accepting goods, compare the consignment and transport documents with the actual quantity, type and destination. Inspect packaging for damage, leakage or insecure condition; identify dangerous goods and required markings before loading. Do not accept a load that cannot be carried legally and safely.

Distribute mass so total mass, axle loads and nose weight remain within limits. Secure every item for full braking and sudden avoidance, then recheck straps and closures after the load settles. Keep delivery documents accurate and report shortages, damage or discrepancies rather than silently signing them away. At delivery, secure the vehicle against rolling, protect the unloading area and open doors or restraints carefully because a shifted load may fall outward.

### Class B scope

Class B generally covers a motor vehicle up to **3,500 kg permissible maximum mass**, designed for no more than eight passengers in addition to the driver. Trailer entitlement depends on the trailer and combination masses; technical vehicle limits still apply. B96 and BE are separate extensions, not ordinary Class B.

### Common exam traps

- Part I is carried; Part II is normally stored safely elsewhere.
- Buying a used car does not reset its HU date.
- A modification may need immediate assessment, not the next scheduled inspection.
- ASF extends probation but does not delete points.
- Licence entitlement never overrides the vehicle's technical limit.

**Remember:** legal driver, legal vehicle, correct papers, valid insurance and every required approval.

## 12. Passengers, loads and trailers

### Seat belts, airbags and children

Every available seat belt must be worn. It should lie flat, untwisted and snug across strong parts of the body. A defective belt, latch or retractor must be repaired; an airbag does not replace it. Sit upright and keep the airbag deployment zone clear.

A child who is **under 12 years old and under 150 cm** must use an approved restraint suitable for size and weight. If either threshold is no longer met, the special child-restraint requirement ends, but the normal seat belt remains compulsory. A rear-facing infant carrier on the front passenger seat requires the front airbag to be deactivated; an active airbag can be fatal.

Engage the rear-door child-safety locks when children could open a door from inside. Seat them on the pavement side where possible, supervise every exit and ensure the lock does not interfere with emergency assistance by an adult.

Even a collision around **20 km/h** can cause severe injury without a belt; reckon with serious or fatal injuries to **unbelted occupants from about 30 km/h**. Belt up on every seat and every journey, however short.

Secure passengers and luggage before moving. Do not carry people in a trailer's load area. Lorry passengers normally belong in the cab, not on the load platform except for narrow work-related exceptions. A wheelchair needs both an approved wheelchair restraint and a separate user restraint.

### Load security

Cargo must not slide, tip, roll, fall or create avoidable noise during full braking or sudden evasive movement. Use suitable tensioning straps, anti-slip mats, nets, partitions and anchor points. Heavy objects go low and close to the vehicle centre or seat backs. Secure even small objects; in a crash they become projectiles. Do not block mirrors, lights, number plates, controls or required emergency equipment.

Respect permissible gross mass and every axle load. Payload is:

`payload (Nutzlast) = permissible gross mass − unladen mass (Leermasse)`

For example, 2,000 kg permitted minus 1,400 kg unladen leaves 600 kg payload. Actual load includes occupants and baggage. A legal total mass can still hide an overloaded axle.

Vehicle and load are generally limited to **2.55 m wide** and **4.00 m high**. Measure the actual loaded vehicle; width includes mirrors, and a roof load can change height. A passenger-car-and-trailer combination without projecting load may be up to **18.00 m** long.

Up to **2.50 m above the road**, a load may not project forward. Above that height it may project at most **50 cm**. Rear projection beyond the reflectors must be marked once it exceeds **1 m**. In darkness or poor visibility, use the required red rear light and reflector; the marking light may be no more than **1.50 m above the road**, and a lateral projection may need white front marking. The normal rear projection limit is 1.5 m, or up to 3 m for a journey no longer than 100 km under the conditions. Secure loading does not legalise excessive dimensions.

For a roof rack or rear bicycle carrier, check vehicle approval, maker's load limit, fastenings, tyre pressure, lights, plate visibility and coupling nose-load limit. Remove unused racks to reduce consumption. A high load increases body roll, crosswind sensitivity and stopping distance.

### Class B trailer limits

An ordinary Class B vehicle may tow:

- a trailer with permissible maximum mass up to **750 kg**; or
- a heavier trailer when the combination's permissible maximum mass remains at or below **3,500 kg**.

Apply every limit, using the lowest: licence entitlement, car's permitted towed load (*Anhängelast*), hitch, trailer, axle, actual mass and combination limits. A car may normally tow only one trailer.

For an example, a car with 2,400 kg permissible maximum mass leaves at most 1,100 kg under the 3,500 kg Class B combination ceiling, even if its technical towing limit is 1,500 kg. A listed 1,000 kg trailer may fit; 1,200 kg would not. B96 can cover qualifying combinations over 3,500 kg up to 4,250 kg, but it is an additional authorisation.

### Nose weight and coupling

Nose weight (*Stützlast*) is the vertical load on the coupling. Keep it within the lowest maximum for car, hitch and trailer. For a centre-axle or rigid-drawbar trailer up to 3.5 t, the minimum is generally 4% of the trailer's actual mass, but this minimum need not exceed 25 kg. Thus 600 kg × 4% = **24 kg**. Load near the axle while maintaining sufficient positive nose weight; rear-heavy loading promotes sway.

Before moving:

1. lower the coupling fully over the ball and verify its locked indicator;
2. lift or test mechanically to confirm it cannot open;
3. attach the breakaway cable (*Abreißseil*) to the designated point;
4. connect the electrical plug and test every light;
5. raise and secure the jockey wheel and supports;
6. release the trailer brake, remove chocks and check tyres and load; and
7. fit extension mirrors when the trailer is wider than the towing vehicle's view.

An overrun brake (*Auflaufbremse*) acts when the trailer pushes against the decelerating towing vehicle. The breakaway cable applies the trailer brake if separation occurs. Before uncoupling, apply the trailer parking brake, chock it and support it securely.

### Driving with a trailer

A trailer increases length, mass, stopping and overtaking distance; reduces acceleration; cuts inside bends; and pushes downhill. Keep more distance, brake earlier and use a lower gear on descents. Observe its speed limit, usually 80 km/h unless a valid higher authorisation applies.

If the trailer begins to sway, hold the steering steady, ease off and reduce speed smoothly. Do not accelerate or make sudden counter-steering movements. Check loading, nose weight, tyre pressure, suspension and speed before continuing.

Reverse slowly: steer initially opposite the direction the trailer should move, then follow it carefully. Use a guide where available and stop if the guide disappears. Practise in a safe place because small steering changes grow rapidly.

### Common exam traps

- Child restraint requires both conditions: under 12 **and** under 150 cm.
- Airbags supplement belts; they never replace them.
- Payload, permissible mass, actual towed load and nose weight are different values.
- The 750 kg trailer rule does not override a lower technical towing limit.
- Rear-heavy loading and acceleration worsen trailer sway.
- A locked-looking coupling still needs visual and mechanical verification.

**Remember:** people belted, load unable to move, every mass within its limit, and trailer locked—cable—plug—lights—nose weight.

## 13. Vehicle technology and maintenance

### Tyres: the only road contact

Check tyre pressure cold, regularly and after every wheel or tyre change. Use the vehicle maker's value for the current load, not a guessed universal pressure. Very low pressure deforms and heats the tyre, increases wear and consumption, worsens steering and can destroy cornering stability. Excessive pressure reduces comfort and changes the contact patch. A pressure-warning light can mean pressure loss or an unrecognised/reset sensor after a wheel change; always inspect before assuming it is only electronic.

Inspect the full visible tyre for cuts, cracks, bulges, embedded objects and uneven wear. The legal minimum tread in the main profile is **1.6 mm**, but wet grip can become poor earlier. Uneven wear may indicate wrong pressure, alignment, suspension or damping problems.

A four-digit tyre date code gives week then year: `1217` means week 12 of 2017. The Alpine mountain-and-snowflake symbol marks current winter suitability. During black ice, snow, slush, ice or frost, suitable winter tyres are required on all wheels under the situational rule. Older M+S-only markings are not generally enough under the current rule.

An emergency or temporary spare can have a strict manufacturer limit. For the exam's marked temporary wheel, the maximum for the **temporary wheel is 80 km/h**. Treat it as a short-term measure, drive gently and repair or replace the normal wheel promptly. The marking and handbook control if a different limit is specified.

A winter-tyre dashboard label such as “M+S 160” or “max. 190” is an absolute tyre-speed ceiling whenever fitted, including on dry roads. Winter tyres can alter dry-road handling. Follow the manufacturer's pressure figure; an exam rule of thumb may mention around 0.2 bar more than summer tyres, but never override the vehicle instructions.

For a wheel change, stop on firm, level, non-slip ground, secure against rolling and use the specified jacking point. Loosen fasteners while the wheel is on the ground, lift, change the wheel, tighten in the correct pattern and torque, then recheck after the distance stated by the maker. Never work beneath a vehicle supported only by its emergency jack.

### Braking systems

The service brake (*Betriebsbremse*) slows all relevant wheels; the parking brake secures the stationary vehicle. Test the service brake gently after setting off, after taking over another vehicle or after it has stood for a long time. The vehicle should brake promptly, evenly and without abnormal pedal travel, noise or pulling.

A pedal that reaches the floor, improves only after pumping, feels unusually soft, or a warning lamp that remains on can indicate a serious hydraulic defect. One-sided braking can make the vehicle veer or skid and lengthen stopping distance. Stop safely and arrange repair; counter-steering, slower driving or the parking brake is not a proper substitute.

Brake fluid absorbs water over time. Water lowers its boiling point, so hard or repeated braking can create vapour and reduce braking force. Follow the manufacturer's replacement interval. Worn, oil-contaminated or damaged pads and discs need repair. On a long descent, select a lower gear and use engine braking to prevent heat fade.

After driving through deep water, wet drum brakes can respond late or unevenly. When traffic permits, dry wet drum brakes by applying them lightly and carefully over a short distance, then test their response. Do not perform this check where it would endanger following traffic.

ABS rapidly controls brake pressure to prevent wheel lock and retain much of the steering ability under hard braking. Press firmly and continue steering toward a genuinely clear path. Pedal pulsing is normal. ABS does not prevent aquaplaning, wheelspin, excessive bend speed or every long stop.

An overrun-braked trailer applies its brake when it pushes against the towing vehicle during deceleration. The breakaway cable activates independent braking after separation; it does not replace correct coupling.

### Steering, suspension and stability

Unexpected heavy steering can result from failed power assistance, steering defects or low tyre pressure. Pulling to one side may result from pressure difference, alignment, bent suspension or one-sided braking. Play, vibration, shimmy, damaged dampers or failure to track straight requires inspection.

Worn shock absorbers reduce tyre contact, especially on uneven surfaces, and worsen braking and cornering. Bent chassis or suspension parts, worn bearings and damaged or unbalanced wheels can make the vehicle unstable. Avoid turning the steering while stationary because it puts high loads on tyres and linkages.

ESC/ESP brakes individual wheels and may reduce engine power to stabilise a skid. A flashing lamp means it is intervening: reduce speed. A lamp that remains on can mean the system is switched off or defective. Traction control (ASR/TCS) reduces driven-wheel spin during acceleration; it is not ABS and cannot create grip on ice.

### Lights, electrical equipment and visibility

Before departure, check dipped and main beam, indicators, brake lights, tail lights, number-plate lighting and trailer lights. Keep lamps clean and unobstructed. If an indicator flashes much faster than usual, a bulb has probably failed. A failed rear or brake lamp may be a bulb or fuse; a starter fault is unrelated.

Use only approved bulbs of the correct type and seat them properly. After repairs, check headlamp alignment. Rear-heavy loading raises the beam, so use range adjustment. If oncoming drivers flash at night, you may be dazzling them or may have forgotten dipped beam; it is not an invitation to retaliate with main beam.

Wipers must clear effectively. Smearing can come from worn blades, oil film or dirt; complete failure may be a fuse, switch or motor. Washer failure may be an empty reservoir, blocked jet, fuse or pump. Replace broken mirror glass rather than relying on a partial view.

An airbag warning lamp remaining on after start requires inspection. Do not assume a warning lamp is harmless because the vehicle still moves. Learn the symbols in the vehicle handbook.

### Engine fluids and mechanical warnings

Use engine oil meeting the manufacturer's specification and keep it between the permitted marks—never overfill. Oil lubricates, cools and cleans. If the oil-pressure warning remains on after starting or illuminates while driving, stop safely and switch off immediately; continuing can destroy the engine. Check the level only as instructed and arrange repair if pressure is not restored.

Too little coolant can cause overheating and engine damage. If temperature becomes excessive, stop, switch off and let the system cool. Possible causes include a leak, low level, dirty radiator or failed fan. Never open a hot pressurised system. Use the correct coolant and antifreeze mixture.

An exhaust that suddenly becomes loud may be corroded, loose or leaking. Repair it promptly: parts can fall onto the road, fumes may enter the vehicle and noise/emissions rise.

Unburned fuel can overheat and damage a catalytic converter. Avoid repeated unsuccessful starting attempts and do not tow-start a vehicle when the manufacturer prohibits it; diagnose the fault instead. A hot catalytic converter can also ignite dry grass, so do not park over combustible vegetation.

In slow traffic with a manual gearbox, choose a low gear and release the clutch whenever possible. Riding it or holding it at the biting point causes heat and wear. With an automatic, use the selector modes intended by the maker. On long descents, choose a lower range early rather than relying entirely on the brakes.

### Driver-assistance systems

Ordinary cruise control (*Tempomat*) maintains a selected speed, not following distance. Cancel or reduce it before a lower limit, bend, descent, poor grip or slower vehicle. Braking or accelerating can override it. Late cancellation can produce speeding, tailgating or entering a bend too fast.

Adaptive cruise control (ACC, *Abstandsregeltempomat*) senses a lead vehicle and can change speed and gap. It may accelerate toward the set speed when the lead vehicle leaves sensor view, including in a bend. Rain, snow, fog, dirt, darkness, unusual vehicles or stationary objects can reduce detection. Continue observing and brake whenever necessary.

Emergency brake assistance can warn, increase brake pressure or brake to reduce collision severity. Lane assistance can warn or steer when markings are detected. Parking systems estimate obstacles. None guarantees detection of every child, cyclist, kerb, trailer, faded marking or cross-traffic movement. Know what the system controls, what its symbols mean, and how to override it.

The safe rule for every assistance system is: **understand, supervise, verify, override**.

### Electric and alternative-drive vehicles

Electric vehicles deliver strong torque immediately and are quiet at low speed, so pedestrians may not hear them. Accelerate smoothly and watch vulnerable users closely. Regenerative braking (*Rekuperation*) converts some motion back into electrical energy, but its strength varies with battery charge, temperature and drive mode. A full or cold battery may accept little regeneration; the friction brakes must remain ready.

Heating, cooling, high speed, heavy load, hills and cold weather reduce range. Plan charging with a reserve rather than assuming the displayed range is exact. After a crash, do not touch damaged high-voltage parts or orange cables; alert emergency services.

Quads, vans and unfamiliar vehicles can have different width, height, centre of gravity, steering and rollover risk. Learn their response before driving at normal pace.

### Fault response

Stop and investigate when braking, steering, tyres, oil pressure, coolant temperature, lights or visibility make safe control doubtful. A defect should be repaired, not compensated for through habit. If continued travel is legally and physically possible, use only the shortest safe route out of traffic or to repair; a serious safety defect means do not continue.

### Common exam traps

- 1.6 mm is a legal tread floor, not a promise of good wet grip.
- Low tyre pressure increases heat and instability; it does not save wear.
- Fast indicator flashing points to a lamp fault, not the starter.
- ABS, ESC, ACC and emergency braking each solve different problems and none replaces attention.
- Topping up fluid does not repair a leak or air in a brake system.
- Warning lamps require the correct response even when the vehicle feels normal.

**Remember:** tyres provide grip, brakes manage energy, steering chooses direction, lights provide sight—and the driver must detect when any system is no longer trustworthy.

## 14. Environment and journey planning

### Economical, low-emission driving

Drive smoothly and anticipate. Accelerate positively without full throttle, shift up early, use the highest suitable gear at low engine speed, keep a steady pace and release the accelerator early when slowing. Unnecessary acceleration followed by braking wastes fuel and tyre/brake material.

Switch off the engine during a long wait when safe and appropriate; avoid unnecessary idling and never warm a cold engine by leaving it stationary. Drive off gently so it reaches operating temperature under light load. Avoid unnecessary noise from revving, doors, audio or avoidable routes through residential areas.

While refuelling, switch off ignition, avoid flames and smoking, and remain beside the nozzle. Stop after the **automatic nozzle shutoff**; forcing in more fuel can defeat the expansion space, spill fuel and release vapour. Replace the cap securely and deal with any spill according to site instructions.

Correct tyre pressure, maintained engine and exhaust systems, low rolling resistance and removing unused roof racks reduce consumption. Carry only necessary load. Smoke, unusual consumption, leaks or warning lamps need inspection. Oil, batteries, tyres and fluids belong in authorised return or collection systems, never drains or ordinary surroundings.

Speed has a large energy cost: in the source comparison, driving at 160 km/h instead of 130 km/h increases fuel use by about **35%** while often saving little time. A single drop of oil can pollute up to **600 litres** of drinking water, so contain a leak promptly and have it repaired. Wash a vehicle where contaminated water is properly collected, such as an appropriate car wash, rather than allowing oil and cleaners into soil or drains.

The **600-litre figure is an exam mnemonic**, not a universal physical conversion: the real effect depends on oil type and conditions. The lesson is that even a tiny leak can contaminate a large amount of water and must never be ignored.

Electric driving also consumes more at high speed, in cold weather and with strong heating. Use recuperation where appropriate, but do not compromise safe braking or traffic flow.

### Planning the trip

Combine journeys, share rides and use public transport or park-and-ride where practical. Choose a route and departure time that avoid congestion, but never follow navigation against signs, police directions or actual road layout. Maps and software can be outdated.

Before driving abroad, check local speed rules, tolls, environmental zones, required safety equipment and city restrictions. Enter the destination and deal with messages before moving. Plan fuel or charging, rest breaks and an alternative route.

Environmental zones (*Umweltzonen*) require the displayed emission sticker unless an exemption applies. A route planner showing access does not replace the roadside sign.

### Common exam traps

- Warming the engine by idling wastes fuel and creates emissions.
- Coasting unsafely or obstructing traffic is not ecological driving.
- Correct pressure means the manufacturer's load-dependent value.
- Navigation guidance never outranks signs, police or the road itself.

**Remember:** plan before moving; drive smoothly; maintain the vehicle; avoid needless speed, load, idling and detours.

## Quick exam cheat sheet

### Behaviour priorities

- Constant caution and mutual consideration.
- Prevent the collision even when another person is wrong.
- Children, older people and people needing help: reduce speed and be ready to stop.
- Hidden view means hidden hazard: lose speed before it appears.
- Fatigue, illness, anger, alcohol, drugs or impairing medication: do not drive.
- Assistance systems help; the driver observes and remains responsible.

### Priority and manoeuvres

- Order: **police → traffic lights → signs → right before left → turning duties**.
- Vehicle from property, petrol station, car park, lowered kerb or traffic-calmed area waits for everyone.
- Left turn: yield to oncoming straight/right-turning traffic.
- Turning: protect pedestrians, cyclists and e-scooters crossing your path.
- Roundabout with yield sign: yield on entry, no entry signal, signal on exit.
- Starting/lane change: mirrors, signal, blind-spot check, safe gap.
- U-turn/reverse: continuous all-around observation; never on motorway or motor road.

### Speed and distance formulas

| Rule | Calculation |
|---|---:|
| Reaction distance | `(speed ÷ 10) × 3` |
| Normal braking distance | `(speed ÷ 10)²` |
| Emergency braking distance | normal braking ÷ 2 |
| Stopping distance | reaction + braking |
| Following gap | about half speedometer reading in metres |

At 50 km/h: reaction 15 m; normal braking 25 m; normal stop 40 m; emergency braking 12.5 m; emergency stop 27.5 m.

- Double speed ≈ four times braking distance and four times bend force.
- Stop within visible distance; on a dangerously narrow road, within half of it.
- Fog, snow or rain visibility under 50 m: maximum 50 km/h or less.
- Snow chains: maximum 50 km/h.
- Motorway recommended speed: 130 km/h under favourable conditions.
- Ordinary trailer: usually maximum 80 km/h unless specially authorised.

### Critical distances and dimensions

- Cyclist/pedestrian/e-scooter overtake: **1.5 m innerorts; 2 m außerorts**.
- Zebra crossing: **5 m before** clear; never stop on it.
- Junction parking: normally **5 m**; **8 m before** where a right-side cycle path exists.
- Bus stop: no parking **15 m before/after**.
- Andreaskreuz parking: **5 m innerorts; 50 m außerorts**.
- Concealed signal/STOP/Andreaskreuz: keep **10 m before** clear.
- Breakdown triangle on fast rural road: about **100 m** orientation.
- Tow-rope gap: maximum **5 m**.
- Tyre tread: minimum **1.6 mm**.
- Vehicle plus load: generally **2.55 m wide, 4.00 m high**.
- No forward load projection up to **2.50 m** height; above it, max **50 cm**.
- Mark rear projection once more than **1 m** beyond rear reflectors.

### Numbers that answer different questions

Do not combine figures merely because they sound related. The **50 km/h** general built-up maximum is a speed ceiling; the “half the speedometer” figure gives following distance in metres. The **50 m** fog reference describes visibility and triggers the special maximum and rear-fog-lamp condition. At 50 km/h, however, the exam reaction distance is 15 m and the normal braking distance is 25 m. Each number answers a different question.

The **1.5 m / 2 m** pair is lateral clearance while overtaking vulnerable users, not following distance. The **1.6 mm** figure is minimum tyre tread, not a safe wet-road stopping guarantee. The **3 m** rule can describe the remaining carriageway width needed beside a parked vehicle or, in another context, a specially permitted rear load projection for a short journey; read the subject before selecting it.

For parking, locate the reference point: 5 m before a zebra crossing; generally 5 m from junction edges; 8 m before a junction with a right-side cycle path; 15 m around a bus-stop sign; and 5 m innerorts or 50 m außerorts around an Andreaskreuz. A 10 m clearance applies only when parking would hide a signal, STOP sign or Andreaskreuz.

For mass questions, identify whether the number is **actual mass**, **permissible maximum mass**, **unladen mass**, **payload**, **axle load**, **towed load** or **nose weight**. A restriction sign commonly tests actual loaded mass or dimensions. Licence Class B calculations use permissible maximum masses. Payload is subtraction; nose-weight minimum uses actual trailer mass. Always compare the result with the separate technical limit.

Finally, the **over 60 km/h** motorway figure is vehicle design capability, not a command to drive above 60. The **130 km/h** Richtgeschwindigkeit is a recommendation in favourable conditions, not an unlimited-road entitlement. The posted limit and safe adapted speed can both be lower.

### Lights and warnings

- Dusk, darkness, tunnel, poor visibility: dipped beam.
- Main beam: dip before dazzling oncoming or preceding traffic.
- Front fog lights: serious impairment by fog, snow or rain.
- Rear fog light: fog only, visibility below 50 m.
- Bus approaching stop with hazard lights: no overtaking.
- Bus stopped with hazards: walking speed in both directions on same carriageway; protect passengers.
- Blue light + siren: clear the way immediately and safely.
- Yellow flashing: hazard warning—slow and expect obstruction.
- Queue on multi-lane motorway: rescue corridor between far-left and every other lane.

### Signs and signals

- Triangle: danger ahead; outside towns usually 150–250 m unless stated.
- Red circle: prohibition or limit. Blue circle: required action.
- STOP: full standstill at line, then observe again if view blocked.
- Yellow diamond: continuing priority road. Priority-next-junction sign: once only.
- Green: proceed only if clear. Yellow: stop if safely possible. Red: stop.
- Static green arrow at red: full stop, yield, optional right turn.
- Yellow temporary markings beat conflicting white markings.
- Red lane cross: closed. Green downward arrow: open. Diagonal arrow: change lane.

### Motorway, tunnel and rail

- Autobahn/Kraftfahrstraße eligibility: design maximum speed **over 60 km/h**.
- Motorway: keep right, overtake left, no stopping, reversing or U-turn.
- Entry: through traffic has priority; find a gap. Exit: brake on deceleration lane.
- Tunnel: dipped beam, observe lane signals, never reverse; fire means engine off and use emergency exit.
- Level crossing: look and listen even with open barrier; enter only with a clear exit.
- Rail beacons: 3 stripes ≈ 240 m; 2 ≈ 160 m; 1 ≈ 80 m.

### Parking and vehicle security

- Stop no more than 3 minutes without leaving; otherwise it is parking.
- No-stopping and no-parking signs are different; hazards do not create permission.
- Parking disc: set to next half hour; 10:40 → 11:00.
- Leaving: door mirror/shoulder check, parking brake, gear/P, key, windows, lock.
- Unhitched trailer: normally no more than two weeks in one public parking place.

### Passengers, loads and trailers

- Belt flat, snug and latched; airbag supplements it.
- Child under 12 **and** under 150 cm: suitable child restraint.
- Rear-facing child seat in front: passenger airbag off.
- Load must survive full braking and sudden avoidance without sliding, rolling, tipping or falling.
- `payload = permissible gross mass − unladen mass`.
- Class B: vehicle up to 3,500 kg; trailer up to 750 kg, or heavier if qualifying combination stays within 3,500 kg.
- Trailer limits: use the lowest of licence, vehicle, hitch, trailer, axle and actual limits.
- Nose weight: generally at least 4% actual trailer mass, minimum capped at 25 kg, never above maker's maximum.
- Couple: lock, mechanical check, breakaway cable, plug, lights, supports, tyres, load.
- Trailer sway: ease off and slow smoothly; do not accelerate or jerk steering.

### Defects and maintenance

- Tyres: cold pressure, tread, age and damage.
- Brake or steering abnormality: stop safely and arrange repair.
- Oil-pressure lamp: stop engine immediately.
- High coolant temperature: stop, switch off, cool safely.
- Fast indicator flashing: likely failed bulb.
- Persistent ESC or airbag lamp: inspect the system.
- Brake fluid absorbs water: replace on schedule.
- Wheel change: secure, loosen on ground, correct jack point, torque and recheck.

### Emergency sequence

1. Stop.
2. Hazard lights, vest and triangle.
3. Assess injuries, traffic, fire and substances.
4. Give first aid.
5. Call **112**; Germany police **110**.

Minor damage: document and move aside. Unattended vehicle: wait, leave details and report. Orange dangerous-goods panel: tell emergency services the numbers and keep away.

### Recurring situation decisions

Use these short patterns to connect recognition with action:

**Child near parked vehicles:** release the accelerator immediately, reduce to a speed that permits stopping and watch gaps beneath and between vehicles. Do not horn as the primary response; the child may be startled into the road.

**Cyclist before a narrowing:** stay behind unless the full statutory lateral distance fits throughout the pass. A brief wide point is not enough if the lane closes while alongside.

**Cyclist beside you before a turn:** mirrors, signal and shoulder check; let the cyclist continue straight before crossing the cycle path. Do not rely on an indicator or engine noise being noticed.

**Pedestrian at a zebra crossing:** approach moderately and make yielding clear early. Never overtake a vehicle hiding the crossing and never queue over the markings.

**Bus showing hazard lights while approaching a stop:** remain behind; overtaking is prohibited. Once it stops, pass only at walking speed and stop for passengers as necessary.

**Green light but blocked exit:** stop before entering the junction. Green permits entry only when you can clear it without blocking cross traffic or crossings.

**Unsigned junction in a residential area:** look especially right and reduce speed enough to yield. The wider or straighter road does not gain priority by appearance.

**Vehicle emerging across a lowered kerb:** it waits for everyone, including pedestrians and cyclists. Still avoid a collision if it enters incorrectly.

**Another driver waves you through:** accept only clear communication, move cautiously and recheck all other road users. The gesture cannot surrender a cyclist's or pedestrian's right.

**Obstacle on your side with oncoming traffic:** slow and wait before moving left, unless signs clearly give your direction priority. Even signed priority applies only when passage remains safe.

**Lane ends:** use the lane to the narrowing, match speed and merge alternately. At a motorway entrance, instead yield to through traffic and select a real gap.

**Overtake with a distant bend or crest:** stay behind if the complete route, including return space, cannot be seen. A high-powered car does not make an unseen route safe.

**Overtaker needs to return:** never accelerate. Ease off and create space even when the other driver caused the problem.

**Vehicle cuts into your following gap:** release the accelerator and brake smoothly until the safe distance is restored. Do not punish the driver with horn, lights or harsh braking.

**Approaching a bend too quickly:** brake while straight before entry. In the bend, avoid abrupt inputs and look toward the safe path. More steering cannot compensate indefinitely for excess speed.

**Aquaplaning begins:** ease off, disconnect drive where appropriate, hold nearly straight and avoid sudden braking. Wait for tyre contact to return before making larger corrections.

**Crosswind after passing a lorry:** expect the steering force to change twice—less in the lorry's shelter, then more as you emerge. Reduce speed and correct smoothly.

**Wildlife enters the road:** brake firmly in a straight line, hold the lane, dip high beam and use the horn if useful. Avoid a violent swerve into oncoming traffic or roadside objects.

**Fog with about 50 m view:** drive no faster than 50 km/h and slower if stopping requires it. The rear fog light is permitted only when fog reduces visibility below 50 m.

**Emergency vehicle with blue light and siren:** locate it, slow and create a clear route without an unsafe panic stop. In a queue, preserve the Rettungsgasse.

**Traffic queue appears on a motorway:** brake early, use hazards to warn behind, keep distance and form the rescue corridor before vehicles are trapped.

**Red light with a static green arrow:** first stop completely. Turn right only after yielding to pedestrians, cyclists and released traffic; waiting for normal green is also allowed.

**Yellow light appears:** stop when a normal safe stop is possible. Continue only when stopping would require dangerous harsh braking or leave you inside the conflict area.

**Level-crossing barrier opens after a train:** wait until all signals release traffic, then look and listen. A second train may approach on another track.

**Breakdown in fast traffic:** move out of the lane if possible, hazards on, vest on, leave away from traffic and place the triangle far enough before the vehicle. Call from safety.

**Smoke in a tunnel:** stop, hazards, engine off, key left, alert others and use the emergency exit. Do not wait in the vehicle for conditions to improve.

**Brake pedal becomes abnormal:** stop safely and arrange repair. Pumping, topping up fluid or compensating with the parking brake does not make the system roadworthy.

**Oil-pressure warning stays on:** switch off the engine immediately after reaching a safe place. Continuing can cause severe damage.

**Trailer starts to sway:** hold steady, ease off and reduce speed smoothly. Do not accelerate and do not make sharp counter-steering movements.

**Guide disappears while reversing:** stop immediately. Resume only when communication and the observed space are restored.

**Navigation contradicts a sign:** obey the sign and reroute. The on-site road arrangement and police instructions outrank stored map data.

**Tiredness or loss of concentration:** interrupt the journey. A timetable, destination or passenger pressure never makes an unfit driver safe.

### Final memory rule

When several answers look reasonable, choose the one that creates time, space and visibility while respecting the exact sign or priority rule. The theory test repeatedly rewards the same pattern: **observe early, slow before the hazard, keep enough distance, communicate clearly and never rely on another person's perfect behaviour.**
