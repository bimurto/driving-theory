# Dimensions, Weights and Speed Limiters — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

Safe driving starts with knowing the vehicle's real dimensions and mass, including any load and the parts that matter in a confined space. The registration certificate is a useful reference, but the driver must check the actual loaded vehicle before setting off.

## Learning goals

After studying this chapter, you should be able to:

- calculate payload (Nutzlast) from the permitted gross mass and unladen mass;
- apply the statutory length and width limits to vehicle combinations;
- interpret width restrictions as applying to the actual width (tatsächliche Breite); and
- explain when a speed limiter (Geschwindigkeitsbegrenzer) must be tested.

## Core rules

### Measure the real height before driving

<!-- questions: 2.6.06-101, 2.6.06-104 -->

Before departure, establish the vehicle's actual height and recalculate it when the load projects above the body. Compare that height with the clearance at bridges, tunnels and underpasses; a clearance sign is a prohibition, not a suggestion. The vehicle's recorded dimensions are found in the registration certificate Part I (Zulassungsbescheinigung Teil I), but a load can make the real height greater. The general statutory maximum height for vehicles and combinations is 4.00 m, subject to the detailed exceptions in the vehicle rules—not 4.50 m. [StVZO §32(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__32.html) [FZV §13 and Annex 4 (registration certificate Part I)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fzv_2023/__13.html)

### Payload is the mass you may add

<!-- questions: 2.6.06-102 -->

**Payload (Nutzlast) = permissible gross mass (zulässige Gesamtmasse) − unladen mass (Leermasse).** It is the maximum load, not the gross mass itself. For example, a 2,000 kg permissible gross mass and 1,400 kg unladen mass leave 600 kg payload. The legal definition of unladen mass includes the operational equipment and prescribed fluids, so use the vehicle's stated values consistently. [StVZO §42(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__42.html) [StVZO §34(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__34.html)

### Length limits depend on the combination

<!-- questions: 2.6.06-103 -->

For a passenger car with a trailer, the maximum permitted combination length without a protruding load is **18.00 m**. Do not confuse this with the **18.75 m** limit for a truck (Lastkraftwagen) and one trailer, or with the 20.75 m value used in the worksheet for a lorry and two-axle trailer including load; those are different vehicle categories. [StVZO §32(4)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__32.html)

### Width signs use actual width

<!-- questions: 2.6.06-213, 2.6.06-216 -->

A width restriction applies to the vehicle's **actual width**, including exterior mirrors and the load. Therefore, a lane marked 2 m is available only when the loaded vehicle including mirrors is no more than 2 m wide; a 2 m value in the registration document alone is not enough. When a supplementary plate says the narrowing begins in 200 m, prepare for the narrowing there, not for a 200 m-long narrowing zone. On uneven roads, body roll or sway may require additional lateral clearance. [StVO Annex 2, sign 264](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/anlage_2.html) [StVZO §32(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__32.html)

### Test the speed limiter after relevant changes

For vehicles subject to a speed limiter, the limiter must be checked before first use, after installation or repair, after a change in the effective tyre circumference (wirksamer Reifenumfang), and after a change to the fuel-supply system that affects the limitation. The check must be performed by an authorised party (Berechtigter), such as an officially recognised manufacturer or an authority-authorised workshop—not just any commercial-vehicle garage. [StVZO §57d(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__57d.html) [StVZO Annex XVIIId](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/anlage_xviiid.html)

## Numbers and formulas

| Situation | Number to remember |
|---|---:|
| Car + trailer, no protruding load | 18.00 m |
| Lorry + trailer (specified combination) | 18.75 m |
| Payload | permitted gross mass − unladen mass |
| Width restriction | actual loaded width, including mirrors |

These values are legal limits for the stated vehicle categories; always check the vehicle documents and any sign for a lower limit. [StVZO §32](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__32.html) [StVZO §42](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__42.html)

## Common exam traps

- Treating the document's height or width as the loaded vehicle's actual dimension.
- Forgetting that exterior mirrors count for a width-restriction sign.
- Calling the permissible gross mass “payload”; payload is the difference.
- Applying 18.75 m to a car-and-trailer question, or treating different combination categories as interchangeable.
- Assuming a speed-limiter check is needed only after changing the exhaust system.
- Assuming every workshop may issue the limiter test certificate.

## Remember this

- Measure the **vehicle + load** before a height or width restriction.
- **Nutzlast = zulässige Gesamtmasse − Leermasse.**
- A width sign means actual width, including **Außenspiegel** and load.
- A 200 m supplementary plate indicates the distance to the start of the narrowing.
- Test the limiter after changes to tyre circumference or relevant fuel supply, using an authorised tester.
