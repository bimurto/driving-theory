# Reading a Road Map and Route Planning — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

Route planning (Streckenplanung) is a safety task, not merely a way to find the
shortest route. Check the route before moving, treat maps and navigation
systems (Navigationssysteme) as aids rather than authorities, and keep your
attention on the traffic situation.

## Learning goals

After studying this chapter, you should be able to:

- check whether map and navigation data may be stale or inaccurate;
- identify local rules before driving in another country; and
- plan or change a route without creating driver distraction.

## Core rules

### Navigation data is useful but not guaranteed

<!-- questions: 2.6.07-001 -->

Road maps and sat-nav databases can become outdated soon after purchase.
Construction, closures, changed one-way arrangements, speed limits, and new
access restrictions may not yet be represented. Even data described as current
can differ from reality, so compare the route with road signs and the actual
traffic situation and obey the signs. The worksheet's explanation captures
these exam points: [question 2.6.07-001](https://www.clickclickdrive.de/fragenkatalog/en/2.6.-rules-on-the-operation-of-the-vehicles/2.6.07-reading-a-road-map-and-route-planning/2.6.07-001).

The official training curriculum treats maps and route planning as part of
responsible road use and international motor traffic, rather than as a
substitute for observation. [FahrschAusbO Annex 1, sections 3(f) and 4](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fahrschausbo_2012/anlage_1.html)

### Check the rules of every country on the route

<!-- questions: 2.6.07-101 -->

Before driving abroad, identify the applicable local requirements (geltende
Verkehrsvorschriften), including:

- **inner-city access:** city tolls, congestion charges, limited-traffic areas,
  or low-emission/environment zones (Umweltzonen) may require payment or a
  vehicle label;
- **labels and permits:** check whether the vehicle needs a windscreen sticker,
  vignette, digital registration, or other marking; and
- **speed limits:** limits differ by country, road type, vehicle, and sometimes
  weather or driving conditions.

There are no single EU-wide road-traffic rules for all these details; official
EU guidance specifically warns that countries differ in speed limits and
required vehicle equipment and directs drivers to check each destination and
transit country. [European Commission, Road rules and safety](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/driving-abroad/road-rules-and-safety/index_en.htm)
The Commission also describes urban access rules such as low-emission zones,
tolls, congestion charging, and limited-traffic zones. [European Commission,
Urban Vehicle Access Regulations](https://transport.ec.europa.eu/transport-themes/urban-transport/urban-vehicle-access-regulations_en)

### Program and adjust the route before driving

<!-- questions: 2.6.07-220 -->

Entering an address into a sat-nav, writing a text message, or conducting a
phone call—even through a hands-free system—can divert attention from traffic.
Do these tasks before departure or after stopping safely. German law permits
use of an electronic information or navigation device while driving only when
it is not held and the permitted interaction is limited to voice/read-out
functions or a brief, conditions-appropriate glance; the device must never
replace observation of the road. [StVO §23(1a)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__23.html)

The general duty of constant care (*ständige Vorsicht*) and mutual
consideration also means that a driver must not allow route planning or
communication to endanger, obstruct, or inconvenience others. [StVO §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)

## Situations and exceptions

- If the device gives an instruction that conflicts with a sign, road closure,
  police direction, or the real situation, follow the applicable traffic rule
  and reassess the route.
- A hands-free call is not automatically safe: §23 controls device operation,
  while §1 still requires attention and safe conduct.
- A vehicle stopped with the engine completely switched off is generally exempt
  from the §23(1a) restriction; automatic stop-start or merely resting electric
  drive is not the same as switching off the engine. [StVO §23(1b)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__23.html)

## Common exam traps

- Assuming automatic updating guarantees correct navigation data.
- Treating a current map as proof that the road layout is unchanged.
- Learning only German rules before an international journey.
- Forgetting city tolls, environmental-zone labels, or country-specific speed
  limits.
- Typing a destination or composing a message while the vehicle is moving.
- Assuming hands-free means distraction-free.

## Remember this

- **Map ≠ reality:** verify signs and conditions as you drive.
- **Abroad:** check access charges, labels, and speed limits for every country.
- **Plan first:** enter destinations and messages before moving or after a safe
  stop.
- **Hands-free is not risk-free:** attention and safe control remain mandatory.
