# Behaviour at Pedestrian Crossings and Towards Pedestrians — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

A pedestrian crossing (*Fußgängerüberweg*, marked by Zeichen 293, commonly a
“zebra crossing”) must remain usable. If traffic unexpectedly stops while your
vehicle is on it, clear the crossing as soon as traffic conditions safely allow:
move a little forward or backward, checking around the vehicle and protecting
pedestrians. Never use the sidewalk (*Gehweg*) as a shortcut.

## Learning goals

After studying this chapter, you should be able to:

- keep a pedestrian crossing clear when traffic queues;
- choose a safe small forward or reverse movement after stopping on the marking;
- distinguish clearing the crossing from driving onto the sidewalk; and
- apply the safety checks required before reversing.

## Core rules

### Keep the pedestrian crossing clear

<!-- questions: 2.2.26-102 -->

Do not enter a *Fußgängerüberweg* if congestion means you would have to wait
on it. This prevents a stationary vehicle from blocking people who are crossing
or about to cross. [StVO §26(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__26.html)

If you nevertheless stop there unexpectedly, clear the marked area promptly by
moving only as far as the traffic situation permits. A short movement forward
may work; if the road behind is clear, a short movement backward is also
permitted. Reversing is not prohibited in every circumstance, but it requires
that danger to other road users is excluded and, when necessary, that another
person guides you. [StVO §9(5)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__9.html)

### The sidewalk is for pedestrians

<!-- questions: 2.2.26-102 -->

Do not move half onto the sidewalk (*Gehweg*) to clear the crossing. Vehicles
must use the carriageway (*Fahrbahn*), while the sidewalk is reserved for
pedestrian traffic unless a specific sign creates an exception. [StVO §2(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__2.html)
[StVO Anlage 2, Zeichen 239](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/anlage_2.html)

The correct priority is therefore: check pedestrians and surrounding traffic,
move forward or backward only enough to leave the crossing, and remain wholly
on the roadway. Do not solve one obstruction by creating a danger or blocking
the pedestrian space.

## Situations and exceptions

- At a crossing, approach only at moderate speed and be ready to wait for
  pedestrians who clearly intend to use it. [StVO §26(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__26.html)
- If reversing is the only practical way to clear the marking, first check the
  full area behind and yield to pedestrians and other road users; stop if safe
  movement cannot be guaranteed. [StVO §9(5)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__9.html)
- “Clear it quickly” does not mean moving abruptly or entering the sidewalk: the
  movement remains subject to the general duty not to endanger or obstruct
  others. [StVO §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)

## Common exam traps

- Choosing “never reverse” even though a safe reverse would clear the crossing.
- Driving onto the sidewalk instead of remaining on the carriageway.
- Moving without checking behind when reversing.
- Entering a crossing while the queue ahead means you will have to stop on it.

## Remember this

- **Fußgängerüberweg:** do not queue on it; keep it clear.
- If unexpectedly stopped there, clear it forward or backward as safely and
  quickly as traffic permits.
- Reverse only with the required observation and without endangering anyone.
- **Gehweg:** pedestrian space, not a vehicle escape route.
