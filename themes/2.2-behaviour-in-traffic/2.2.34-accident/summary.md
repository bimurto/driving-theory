# Accident — Class B Study Summary

## Core rules

### Approach an accident scene defensively

<!-- questions: 2.2.34-001-M, 2.2.34-002-M, 2.2.34-003-M -->

If the scene is already adequately secured and there is no new hazard, continue
normally: sudden hard braking can create a rear-end collision, and a driver
must not take a photograph while driving. If the scene is unsecured or unclear,
reduce speed, increase the safety margin, and drive as far left as safely
possible to keep clear of vehicles or people on the right. These actions apply
the general duty to avoid endangering or obstructing others in [StVO §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)
and the driver's duty to maintain a safe vehicle view and control in [StVO §23(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__23.html).
The prohibition on holding or operating a communication/information device
while driving also follows from [StVO §23(1a)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__23.html).

### Secure an accident and help

<!-- questions: 2.2.34-101 -->

After a collision, stop and secure the traffic (Verkehr sichern): switch on
the hazard warning lights (Warnblinkanlage), put on the high-visibility vest
(Warnweste), and place the warning triangle (Warndreieck) at a suitable
distance. For a vehicle that cannot be recognised in time as a stationary
obstacle, [StVO §15](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__15.html)
requires hazard warning lights immediately and a clearly visible warning sign;
on fast traffic roads the regulation gives about 100 m as the reference
distance. Hazard warning lights may also be used to warn others of a danger
under [StVO §16(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__16.html).

Check the consequences, help injured persons where reasonably possible, and
call the police/emergency services when needed. Accident participants must
stop, secure traffic, check consequences, help injured people, exchange details,
and remain or enable later identification under [StVO §34](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__34.html).
The duty to render reasonable assistance (without substantial personal danger)
is also [StGB §323c](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__323c.html).

### Wildlife collision: secure, report, do not load the animal

<!-- questions: 2.2.34-101 -->

After hitting a wild animal, do not put it in the car or drive away with it.
Secure the site, keep away from a frightened or injured animal, and notify the
police (Polizei); the police can contact the responsible game warden
(Jagdpächter) or other competent person. Give the exact location and explain
what happened. The immediate securing steps are grounded in [StVO §15](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__15.html)
and the accident duties in [StVO §34](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__34.html).

### The European accident report records facts, not liability

<!-- questions: 2.2.34-102 -->

Keep a European accident report (Europäischer Unfallbericht) and a pen in the
vehicle. It is a standardised form: each party can record the circumstances and
damage independently, and the matching multilingual versions make it easier
when the other driver speaks another language. Completing it does not itself
admit liability; insurers use the recorded facts to assess the claim. The EU
describes the common European Accident Statement as a way to collect the
necessary information and make claims easier to settle in [Your Europe — car
insurance and accidents](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/vehicles/insurance/accident/indexamp_en.htm).

### eCall sends the minimum emergency data

<!-- questions: 2.2.34-103 -->

In a serious crash, vehicle sensors (often linked to the airbag system) can
trigger the 112-based eCall automatically. It establishes a connection to the
nearest emergency call centre and transmits a minimum data set including the
vehicle's precise position and the time of the incident (as well as other
standard data such as direction of travel). The European Commission explains
the automatic trigger and these data in [Interoperable EU-wide eCall](https://transport.ec.europa.eu/transport-themes/smart-mobility/road/its-directive-and-action-plan/interoperable-eu-wide-ecall_en).

### Manual eCall is for a real emergency

<!-- questions: 2.2.34-104 -->

An occupant may press the eCall button manually when urgent help is needed—for
example, after witnessing a serious accident or when a passenger has a medical
emergency such as a heart attack. A puncture alone is not an emergency-call
reason. Manual activation and the automatic 112 connection are described in
[Your Europe — eCall](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/security-and-emergencies/emergency-assistance-vehicles-ecall/indexamp_en.htm).

## Situations and exceptions

- Do not stop abruptly merely to look at a scene that is already secured; avoid creating a new hazard.
- At an unclear scene, slow down and maximise lateral clearance while remaining in your lane and in control.
- If you are involved, do not remove accident traces before necessary findings are made; see [StVO §34(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__34.html).
- eCall is for serious accidents or urgent emergencies; it is not a roadside breakdown service.

## Remember this

- **Secure first:** Warnblinker, Warnweste, Warndreieck; protect yourself from traffic.
- **Unclear scene:** slower, farther left, larger safety margin.
- **Wildunfall:** secure and notify Polizei; leave the animal where it is.
- **Europäischer Unfallbericht:** multilingual factual documentation, not a liability verdict.
- **eCall:** serious crash can call 112 automatically; it sends position and accident time; urgent occupants can trigger it manually.
