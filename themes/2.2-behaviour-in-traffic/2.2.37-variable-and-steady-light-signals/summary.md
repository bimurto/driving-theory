# Variable and Steady Light Signals — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

Traffic signals (*Lichtzeichen*) regulate whether traffic may proceed and, for
lane-control signals, whether a particular lane may be used. Read the signal
above your own lane, obey it before ordinary priority rules, and approach every
change with enough speed and distance to stop safely.

## Learning goals

After studying this chapter, you should be able to:

- interpret green, yellow, and red at a signalised junction;
- react correctly when green has been showing for a long time or changes to yellow;
- read red crossed bars, a green downward arrow, and a flashing yellow diagonal arrow;
- apply the stopping-distance rule of thumb to the yellow-light decision; and
- know that a Dauerlichtzeichen also prohibits stopping on the controlled lane.

## Core rules

### Ordinary traffic-light colours

<!-- questions: 2.2.37-002, 2.2.37-003, 2.2.37-004, 2.2.37-006 -->

At a junction, **green** (*Grün*) means traffic is released (*Der Verkehr ist
freigegeben*), subject to the normal turning rules and the duty of care.
**Yellow/amber** (*Gelb*) means “wait for the next signal before the junction”
(*Vor der Kreuzung auf das nächste Zeichen warten*); **red** (*Rot*) means stop
before the junction. These meanings and the colour sequence are set out in
§37(2) StVO. [StVO §37(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__37.html)

If green has been showing for some time, do not accelerate beyond the limit or
try to “beat” a change. Keep a controlled speed, watch the signal, and be ready
to stop. Once you are so close that a normal stop is no longer possible, brake
only as appropriate for safety and continue through; a sudden, unsafe stop is
not the intended response to yellow. The general duty of care still applies.
[§1 StVO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)

### Yellow: use the stopping-distance decision

<!-- questions: 2.2.37-002, 2.2.37-003, 2.2.37-006 -->

When yellow appears, stop if you can do so safely before the junction. At about
40 km/h, the theory-test normal-braking rule of thumb is:

```text
normal braking distance ≈ (40 ÷ 10)² = 16 m
```

The exam worksheet uses a longer practical stopping estimate (about **28 m**,
including reaction distance and the situation's safety margin). Therefore, at
40 km/h and only 10 m from the signal, continuing is the safer answer because a
hard stop could leave the vehicle in the junction or cause a rear-end collision.
At 40 m, stop because there is enough distance to approach and halt normally.
These are training estimates, not statutory stopping distances; choose a speed
that permits control and stopping. [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

If the light has just changed to yellow while you are still far enough away,
stop even when turning left. Turning direction does not cancel the yellow
waiting instruction. [StVO §37(2) no. 1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__37.html)

### Permanently illuminated lane signals (*Dauerlichtzeichen*)

<!-- questions: 2.2.37-001-B, 2.2.37-007, 2.2.37-008 -->

Lane-control signals apply directly to the lane beneath them. Under §37(3)
StVO:

- **Red crossed diagonal bars** (*rote gekreuzte Schrägbalken*) mean the lane
  may not be used (*Der Fahrstreifen darf nicht benutzt werden*).
- A **green downward arrow** (*grüner, nach unten gerichteter Pfeil*) means
  traffic on that lane is released; you may continue on it.
- A **flashing yellow arrow pointing diagonally downward** (*gelb blinkender,
  schräg nach unten gerichteter Pfeil*) orders you to change into the lane in
  the arrow's direction.

These signals can therefore open or close lanes; they do not mean “overtaking
 only” merely because the lane is on the left. Read each signal over the lane
 you occupy. [StVO §37(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__37.html)

Do not stop on a lane controlled by a permanently illuminated signal
(*Fahrstreifen mit Dauerlichtzeichen*). This is a separate rule from whether
the lane is currently open. [StVO §37(5)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__37.html)

## Common exam traps

- Treating yellow as permission to accelerate; it means wait, unless stopping
  would no longer be safe or normally possible.
- Choosing “stop” at 10 m and 40 km/h without considering stopping distance and
  the danger of an abrupt stop.
- Continuing at 40 m when there is ample distance to stop.
- Assuming a long-running green light will remain green, or exceeding the
  speed limit to reach it.
- Confusing a red crossed lane signal with a no-overtaking instruction.
- Reading a lane signal as applying to every lane rather than the lane directly
  beneath it.
- Forgetting that a flashing yellow diagonal arrow requires a lane change and
  that stopping on a Dauerlichtzeichen lane is prohibited.

## Remember this

- **Green:** traffic released, subject to care and turning rules.
- **Yellow:** wait before the junction; stop when a safe normal stop is possible.
- **Red:** stop before the junction.
- **10 m at 40 km/h:** continue if stopping would be unsafe; **40 m:** stop.
- **Red crossed bars:** lane closed.
- **Green down arrow:** lane open.
- **Flashing yellow diagonal arrow:** change in the arrow's direction.
- Never stop on a lane with a permanently illuminated lane signal.
