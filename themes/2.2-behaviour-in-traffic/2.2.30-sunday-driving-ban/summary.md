# Sunday and public-holiday driving ban — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

The German **Sonn- und Feiertagsfahrverbot** is a time-limited ban on certain
goods vehicles making commercial or paid goods journeys. It is not a general
ban on cars, and it does not prohibit every vehicle that happens to have a
trailer.

## Learning goals

After studying this chapter, you should be able to:

- state the ban's hours (**00:00–22:00**);
- identify the two vehicle categories covered by the basic rule; and
- distinguish a truck (Lastkraftwagen, **Lkw**) from a car with a trailer.

## Core rules

### Hours and purpose of the ban

<!-- questions: 2.2.30-101, 2.2.30-103 -->

On Sundays and the statutory public holidays listed in the rule, from **00:00
until 22:00**, the covered vehicles may not be driven for **geschäftsmäßige
oder entgeltliche Beförderung von Gütern** (commercial or paid carriage of
goods), including associated empty journeys (**Leerfahrten**). The restriction
comes from § 30(3) StVO. [§ 30(3) StVO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__30.html)

The ban ends at 22:00; the ordinary rule therefore does not automatically
continue through the Sunday night. “Public holiday” has the federal-law list
and regional qualifications in § 30(4), so do not assume every local holiday
has the same status nationwide. [§ 30(4) StVO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__30.html)

### Which vehicles are covered?

<!-- questions: 2.2.30-102, 2.2.30-103 -->

The basic categories are:

- an **Lkw with a permissible gross mass (zulässige Gesamtmasse) over 7.5 t**;
- an **Lkw with a trailer (Anhänger hinter einem Lastkraftwagen)**, regardless
  of the truck's mass threshold.

The trigger is the vehicle's permitted mass (**zGM**, not its actual scale
weight) and/or the Lkw–trailer combination, together with the commercial or
paid carriage of goods. A **Pkw towing a trailer** is not covered by this
specific Sunday/public-holiday goods-transport ban merely because it has a
trailer. [§ 30(3) StVO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__30.html)

### Exceptions exist, but do not change the exam rule

The StVO lists exceptions for specified urgent or specially protected
transports (for example, some combined rail–road journeys, certain fresh-food
and live-animal transports, and emergency or public-service operations).
Apply an exception only when its statutory conditions are met; otherwise use
the two-category rule above. [§ 30(3) StVO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__30.html)

## Situations and exceptions

- A truck over 7.5 t carrying goods commercially is banned during the stated
  window, even if it is currently empty on a related return journey.
- A truck below or at 7.5 t is still banned when it is towing a trailer, under
  the Lkw-with-trailer limb of § 30(3).
- A private car with a trailer is not transformed into a banned Lkw by the
  trailer alone.
- The separate **Ferienreiseverordnung** can impose additional Saturday bans
  in July and August on designated roads; that is a different rule from the
  Sunday/public-holiday ban. [Ferienreiseverordnung § 1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ferreisev_1985/__1.html)

## Common exam traps

- Answering “all vehicles with trailers” instead of “Lkw with trailers.”
- Treating 7.5 t as an actual weighed mass rather than **zulässige
  Gesamtmasse**.
- Forgetting that the ban is tied to commercial/paid goods carriage and related
  empty trips.
- Extending the ban past 22:00 or confusing it with the summer holiday
  Saturday restriction.

## Remember this

- **When?** Sunday/public holiday, **00:00–22:00**.
- **Who?** Lkw **over 7.5 t**, or **any Lkw with a trailer**, when carrying goods
  commercially or for payment.
- **Not automatically covered:** Pkw + trailer.
- **Legal anchor:** § 30(3) StVO; check its exceptions before relying on one.
