# Distance — Class B Study Summary

## Core rules

### Normal following distance

<!-- questions: 2.2.04-001, 2.2.04-003, 2.2.04-004, 2.2.04-005 -->

German law requires a gap large enough to stop behind the vehicle ahead even if
it brakes suddenly. [StVO § 4(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__4.html)
For revision, use the **two-second distance** (*Zwei-Sekunden-Abstand*): choose
a roadside point and ensure at least two seconds pass between the vehicle ahead
and your own vehicle reaching it. At 80 km/h this is about 40 m; outside built-up
areas, the familiar exam approximation is half the speedometer reading in
metres (100 km/h ≈ 50 m). These are rules of thumb, not permission to ignore
the actual conditions. [StVO § 4(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__4.html)

### Urban traffic and poor conditions

<!-- questions: 2.2.04-002 -->

In slow, attentive urban traffic on a dry road, the worksheet's one-second rule
of thumb gives about 15 m at 50 km/h (roughly three car lengths). This shorter
gap is a favourable-condition exam approximation; the legal test remains
whether you can stop safely, so use more distance whenever traffic or grip is
uncertain. [StVO § 4(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__4.html)

Rain, snow, ice, poor visibility or a slippery surface increase the required
gap. As a study rule, at least twice the normal distance is a useful warning
starting point, but reduce speed further if necessary. [StVO § 3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

### Heavy loads

<!-- questions: 2.2.04-106 -->

An heavily loaded car may handle differently and can need a longer braking
distance: extra mass increases the energy the brakes must dissipate and can
change steering and stability. Increase the gap and adapt speed rather than
relying on the normal dry-road estimate. [StVO § 3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

## Situations and exceptions

### Long combinations must leave a merge gap

<!-- questions: 2.2.04-101, 2.2.04-102, 2.2.04-105-M, 2.2.04-107 -->

Outside built-up areas, a vehicle with a special speed restriction and any
combination (*Zug*) longer than 7 m must continuously leave enough distance for
an overtaking vehicle to pull in (*einscheren*). This special rule does not
apply where there is more than one lane in your direction, where overtaking is
prohibited, or while you are yourself pulling out to overtake and have
announced it. [StVO § 4(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__4.html)

After the overtaking vehicle has merged, restore an appropriate following gap;
the gap should not be needlessly excessive. Continue to obey the ordinary
stopping-distance rule. [StVO § 4(1)-(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__4.html)

## Remember this

- **Ordinary rule:** leave enough room to stop if the vehicle ahead brakes hard.
- **Two seconds:** a practical all-weather starting check; conditions may need
  more.
- **Half-speed rule:** speedometer reading ÷ 2 in metres (exam approximation).
- **Poor conditions:** slow down and increase the gap, often at least twofold.
- **Combination over 7 m / special speed restriction:** leave an overtaking
  vehicle room to merge outside built-up areas.
