# Speed — Class B Study Summary

## Core rules

### Adapt speed and stop within what you can see

<!-- questions: 2.2.03-005, 2.2.03-012, 2.2.03-023, 2.2.03-024, 2.2.03-025, 2.2.03-303 -->

Drive only as fast as you can constantly control, adapting to road, traffic,
visibility, weather, personal ability, vehicle and load. If fog, snowfall or
rain reduces visibility below 50 m, the maximum is 50 km/h (or less if needed).
You must be able to stop within the visible distance (*übersehbare Strecke*);
on a road too narrow for safe passing, stop within half that distance.
[StVO § 3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

At night on a normal road, the usable visible distance is the range of the
dipped headlights (*Abblendlicht*), not the much longer reach of high beam.
The headlights do not remove the obligation to slow down enough to stop within
what is visible. [StVO § 3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

### Reaction, braking and stopping distance

<!-- questions: 2.2.03-006, 2.2.03-007, 2.2.03-008, 2.2.03-009, 2.2.03-010, 2.2.03-011 -->

For the theory-test rule of thumb:

```text
reaction distance (m) ≈ (speed in km/h ÷ 10) × 3
normal braking distance (m) ≈ (speed in km/h ÷ 10)²
stopping distance = reaction distance + braking distance
```

Thus 100 km/h gives 30 m reaction distance plus 100 m normal braking distance
= 130 m stopping distance. At 50 km/h the corresponding values are 15 m, 25 m
and 40 m. These are exam approximations; real distances depend on reaction,
brakes, tyres, grip, gradient and conditions. [StVO § 3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

### Emergency braking and evasive braking

<!-- questions: 2.2.03-015, 2.2.03-016 -->

For emergency/evasive braking (*Gefahrbremsung*), the worksheet rule of thumb
halves the normal braking-distance result:

```text
evasive-braking distance ≈ ((speed ÷ 10)²) ÷ 2
```

At 50 km/h this is 25 ÷ 2 = 12.5 m; reaction distance is a separate part of
stopping distance. Treat this as a test formula, not a statutory performance
promise. [StVO § 3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

## Numbers and formulas

### General maximums outside built-up areas

<!-- questions: 2.2.03-026-M, 2.2.03-104, 2.2.03-105 -->

Unless a sign sets a lower limit, 50 km/h is the general built-up-area maximum.
Outside built-up areas, passenger cars and other motor vehicles up to 3.5 t
without a trailer may normally drive up to 100 km/h. A limit shown on a sign
still controls. [StVO § 3(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

After turning, do not carry a previous sign's 30-km/h limit into the new road
unless the sign or another rule still applies; the ordinary built-up-area limit
is 50 km/h. [StVO § 3(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

### Trailers, autobahns and motorways

<!-- questions: 2.2.03-018, 2.2.03-019, 2.2.03-106, 2.2.03-109, 2.2.03-110 -->

The standard maximum outside built-up areas for a passenger car with trailer is
80 km/h. The same 80-km/h class covers a truck up to 3.5 t with a trailer. On
Autobahnen and Kraftfahrstraßen, § 18(5) likewise sets 80 km/h for passenger
cars with trailers and trucks with trailers. A trailer's 100-km/h approval does
not turn an ordinary federal road (*Bundesstraße*) into a 100-km/h road: that
approval matters only where the road and regulations permit 100 km/h.
[StVO § 3(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html) and
[StVO § 18(5)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__18.html)

### Snow chains and winter tyres

<!-- questions: 2.2.03-101, 2.2.03-107 -->

With snow chains (*Schneeketten*), the vehicle maximum is 50 km/h even in good
conditions. [StVO § 3(4)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

For winter tyres (*Winterreifen*), including tyres marked M+S where relevant to
the question bank, never exceed the tyre's permitted speed. If that speed is
below the vehicle's design speed, it must be displayed to the driver and must
not be exceeded in operation. Current winter-weather tyres are identified by
the Alpine symbol under the StVZO. [StVZO § 36(4)-(5)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__36.html)

### Advisory speed of 130 km/h

<!-- questions: 2.2.03-304, 2.2.03-305 -->

The 130-km/h *Autobahn-Richtgeschwindigkeit* is a recommendation for passenger
cars and other motor vehicles up to 3.5 t on Autobahnen, on non-built-up divided
roads, and on non-built-up roads with at least two marked lanes per direction.
It applies only where no lower statutory or signed maximum applies. It is not a
general permission to drive 130 km/h: conditions may require less.
[Autobahn-Richtgeschwindigkeits-V § 1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/babrigeschwv_1978/__1.html)

## Remember this

- **Fog/snow/rain below 50 m visibility:** maximum 50 km/h, or less if needed.
- **Reaction:** `(speed ÷ 10) × 3`; **normal braking:** `(speed ÷ 10)²`.
- **Stopping:** reaction + braking; **evasive braking:** normal braking ÷ 2.
- **Snow chains:** 50 km/h maximum.
- **Trailer:** usually 80 km/h; check the road and vehicle category.
- **Richtgeschwindigkeit:** recommended 130 km/h, not an entitlement.
- Always be able to stop within the visible distance (half on a dangerously
  narrow road).
