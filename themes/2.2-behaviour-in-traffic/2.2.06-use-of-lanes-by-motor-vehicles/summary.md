# Use of Lanes by Motor Vehicles — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

At a bottleneck (*Fahrbahnverengung*), the vehicle whose path is obstructed
must yield. Reduce speed early and stop before the constricted point when the
available gap is not clearly sufficient for a safe passage. Waiting is safer
than attempting to squeeze between a cyclist, an oncoming vehicle, and the
obstruction.

## Learning goals

After studying this chapter, you should be able to:

- identify which lane is obstructed at a bottleneck;
- yield to traffic that can pass through the usable lane; and
- recognise when slowing is not enough and a complete stop (*warten*) is
  required.

## Core rules

### Yield when your lane is blocked

<!-- questions: 2.2.06-101-M -->

If a construction barrier or another obstruction narrows your lane, approach
slowly and wait before the bottleneck until the road user already using the
available space has passed. Do not continue at reduced speed if the remaining
distance would force you close to or into the cyclist's path. This follows the
StVO's basic duty of constant caution and mutual consideration (*ständige
Vorsicht und gegenseitige Rücksicht*): nobody may endanger or unnecessarily
obstruct another road user. [StVO §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)

The practical exam answer is therefore **slow down and stop/wait**. A reduced
speed by itself does not make an insufficient gap safe; wait until the cyclist
has cleared the bottleneck, then pass with adequate lateral clearance. When a
motor vehicle overtakes a cyclist, the required side distance is at least
**1.5 m in built-up areas** and **2 m outside built-up areas**. [StVO
§5(4)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__5.html)

### The obstructed side yields at a constriction

<!-- questions: 2.2.06-101-M -->

The same principle applies when an oncoming car is approaching a constriction:
if the bottleneck is in your lane, stop before it and let the oncoming vehicle
pass through. Under the specific *Vorbeifahren* rule, a driver who wants to
pass a narrowing, roadway obstacle, or stopped vehicle on the left must let
oncoming vehicles go first, unless signs 208 or 308 establish a different
priority. [StVO §6](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__6.html)

## Situations and exceptions

- **Cyclist already at the bottleneck:** stop early and wait if the gap is not
  clearly enough to pass without danger; do not rely on merely reducing speed.
  [StVO §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)
- **Oncoming traffic:** the side whose lane is narrowed waits. Check for a
  priority sign before applying the default rule. [StVO
  §6](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__6.html)
- **Passing a cyclist:** only pass when the full manoeuvre is safe and the
  statutory side distance can be maintained. [StVO
  §5(2), §5(4)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__5.html)

## Common exam traps

- Continuing slowly through a gap that is too short for a cyclist to pass
  safely.
- Assuming the vehicle that arrives first automatically has priority.
- Forgetting that the obstruction's location—not the driver's intention—shows
  who must wait.
- Treating *langsamer fahren* (slowing) as sufficient when the correct action is
  to stop and wait (*anhalten und warten*).

## Remember this

- **Your lane narrowed?** Slow down, stop before the bottleneck, and let the
  other road user clear it.
- **Cyclist in the constricted area?** Wait until the distance is safely open;
  never squeeze past.
- **Overtaking a cyclist:** keep at least 1.5 m inside and 2 m outside built-up
  areas.
- **Different sign?** Signs 208 or 308 can change the default priority at a
  narrowing.
