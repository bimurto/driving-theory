# Combustion-engine fluids and power transmission — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

Two practical checks in this chapter protect the engine and the manual
transmission: maintain the correct **coolant** (*Kühlmittel/Kühlflüssigkeit*)
level, and operate the **clutch** (*Kupplung*) so it is fully engaged whenever
slipping is not needed. The vehicle must remain safe to operate; [StVZO §30](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__30.html)
requires vehicles to be constructed and maintained so ordinary operation does
not endanger others.

## Learning goals

- explain why too little coolant can overheat and damage an engine; and
- minimise manual-clutch wear in slow-moving traffic (*Stop-and-go-Verkehr*).

## Core rules

### Keep the coolant level correct

<!-- questions: 2.7.03-101 -->

The cooling system carries heat away from the engine. If the coolant level is
too low, the system may no longer remove enough heat: the engine can
**overheat** (*überhitzen*), and continued operation can cause **engine damage**
(*Motorschaden*). The ADAC identifies insufficient coolant as a common cause of
overheating and warns that a damaged cooling system can lead to engine damage
([ADAC: Coolant checks and filling](https://www.adac.de/verkehr/tanken-kraftstoff-antrieb/tipps-zum-tanken/kuehlmittel-motor/);
[ADAC: Warning lamps](https://www.adac.de/rund-ums-fahrzeug/ausstattung-technik-zubehoer/ausstattung/kontrollleuchten-auto/)).

Check the level at the reservoir between its **MIN** and **MAX** marks when the
engine is cold. Do not open a hot cooling system: it is pressurised and can
cause scalding. If the temperature warning appears, stop safely, switch off,
and let the engine cool before checking; follow the vehicle handbook for the
correct coolant mixture and repair any leak. [ADAC coolant guidance](https://www.adac.de/verkehr/tanken-kraftstoff-antrieb/tipps-zum-tanken/kuehlmittel-motor/)

### Let the clutch engage fully in a traffic jam

<!-- questions: 2.7.03-102 -->

At walking pace in a manual-transmission vehicle, select a **low gear**
(*niedriger Gang*) and, whenever the traffic gap permits, drive with the clutch
fully engaged (*Kupplung vollständig eingekuppelt*) and without continuously
pressing the pedal. This avoids unnecessary friction and heat in the clutch
(*Kupplungsverschleiß*). Repeatedly starting and stopping is not the least-wear
choice when the vehicle can roll smoothly in a low gear; driving slowly with a
**slipping clutch** (*schleifende Kupplung*) keeps the friction surfaces
slipping and increases wear. The ADAC likewise warns that driving with a
slipping clutch can damage it over time ([ADAC: Automatic and manual
transmissions](https://www.adac.de/rund-ums-fahrzeug/ausstattung-technik-zubehoer/ausstattung/automatik-fahren/)).

If you must stop, disengage the clutch as needed and avoid holding the vehicle
on the clutch. When waiting for longer, select neutral (*Leerlauf*) and release
the clutch pedal; this is a practical wear-reduction rule, not a special legal
speed rule.

## Situations and exceptions

- Check coolant only with the engine cool; a hot cap can release pressurised
  coolant and cause serious burns. [ADAC coolant guidance](https://www.adac.de/verkehr/tanken-kraftstoff-antrieb/tipps-zum-tanken/kuehlmittel-motor/)
- A low coolant level is a fault clue, not permission to continue driving until
  the engine overheats. Stop safely and arrange inspection if the level falls
  again or a warning remains. [ADAC coolant guidance](https://www.adac.de/verkehr/tanken-kraftstoff-antrieb/tipps-zum-tanken/kuehlmittel-motor/)
- The low-gear, fully-engaged-clutch method applies when the vehicle can move
  smoothly; traffic, gradient, or an impending stop may require clutch use.

## Common exam traps

- Treating low coolant as merely a fuel-consumption issue: the tested results
  are overheating and possible engine damage.
- Keeping the clutch partly engaged (*Schleifpunkt*) throughout a traffic jam.
- Confusing a low gear with a slipping clutch: low gear can allow the clutch to
  be fully engaged, which reduces wear.
- Opening the coolant reservoir while the engine is hot.

## Remember this

- **Too little coolant → overheating → possible engine damage.**
- Check coolant cold and between **MIN/MAX**; follow the handbook.
- In crawling traffic: **low gear + clutch fully engaged whenever possible**.
- Avoid riding the clutch; use neutral and release the pedal when waiting.
