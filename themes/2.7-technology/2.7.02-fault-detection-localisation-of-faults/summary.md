# Fault detection and localisation — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

The driver is responsible for noticing safety-critical defects and arranging
prompt repair. A vehicle that steers, brakes, illuminates, or restrains its
occupants abnormally must not be treated as “good enough”. Stop where safe,
protect other road users, and use a qualified workshop when the defect affects
roadworthiness (*Verkehrssicherheit*). The duty to keep a vehicle in a safe
condition follows [StVO §23](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__23.html)
and the construction requirements are in the [StVZO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/).

## Learning goals

- inspect tyres, brakes, steering, suspension, lights, glazing, wipers and belts;
- interpret common warning-lamp and symptom clues; and
- stop driving and arrange repair when control or roadworthiness is compromised.

## Core rules

### Tyres: inspect pressure, tread, age and damage

<!-- questions: 2.7.02-015, 2.7.02-021, 2.7.02-022, 2.7.02-023, 2.7.02-024, 2.7.02-032, 2.7.02-037, 2.7.02-124, 2.7.02-125, 2.7.02-131, 2.7.02-132, 2.7.02-136 -->

At regular intervals check *Reifendruck* (tyre inflation pressure), tread depth,
even wear, and visible damage. The legal minimum tread depth for the main
tread of passenger-car and motorcycle tyres is **1.6 mm**; measure the main
grooves, not a decorative shoulder. [StVZO §36(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__36.html)
The DOT code identifies manufacture date, so age is also a useful condition
check even though it is not a substitute for inspecting the rubber.

Pressure that is too low or too high worsens handling (*Fahrverhalten*) and
increases wear; very low pressure also causes flexing heat, reduced stability,
rolling resistance and fuel consumption. Use the vehicle maker’s pressure
specification, normally shown on the vehicle label or handbook. After a hard
impact with a kerb or obstacle, stop and have tyre and rim checked: hidden
sidewall, bead or rim damage can cause a slow loss of pressure or failure.
Uneven wear calls for checks of pressure, wheel alignment/chassis settings
(*Achsgeometrie/Fahrwerk*) and shock absorbers. Trembling during acceleration
also warrants checking front wheels for damage and secure mounting.

### Brakes: test, interpret and repair

<!-- questions: 2.7.02-016, 2.7.02-017, 2.7.02-019, 2.7.02-020, 2.7.02-030, 2.7.02-033, 2.7.02-034, 2.7.02-040, 2.7.02-049, 2.7.02-107, 2.7.02-109, 2.7.02-112, 2.7.02-128, 2.7.02-129, 2.7.02-135 -->

Test the service brake (*Betriebsbremse*) gently immediately after setting off,
and repeat the test when taking over another vehicle or after it has stood for
a long time. A brake pedal that reaches the floor and becomes shorter only
after pumping indicates air or another hydraulic defect: park safely and have
the system repaired immediately; topping up fluid alone is not a repair.
Brake pads must be replaced when contaminated with oil or at their wear limit.

Brake fluid absorbs water over time. Follow the manufacturer’s replacement
interval because water lowers the boiling margin and can produce vapour bubbles
under heavy braking, causing brake failure. A brake warning lamp, pulling to one
side, persistent braking noise, or a car that cannot stay in lane requires an
immediate workshop inspection. One-sided braking can make the vehicle veer or
skid and lengthens stopping distance; ABS does not thereby cease to function.
Never compensate by counter-steering, driving on at low speed, or using the
handbrake instead of repairing the service brake. [StVZO §41](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__41.html)

### Steering, suspension and directional stability

<!-- questions: 2.7.02-103, 2.7.02-123, 2.7.02-126, 2.7.02-127, 2.7.02-130, 2.7.02-133 -->

Unexpectedly heavy steering can result from defective steering gear, failed
power steering (*Servolenkung*), or low tyre pressure—not excessive pressure.
A pull to one side may indicate incorrect wheel alignment or a bent axle guide;
check pressure first, then go to a workshop if needed. Bent suspension parts,
frame or fork, play in wheel or fork bearings, a defective steering damper, and
damaged or unbalanced wheels can impair tracking, cause shimmy, or reduce
roadworthiness (*Fahrstabilität*). Steering while stationary puts unusually
large loads on tyres and steering/linkage components; avoid it where possible.
Any steering, chassis or suspension defect demands prompt inspection under
[StVZO §30](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__30.html).

### Electrical equipment and warning indications

<!-- questions: 2.7.02-025, 2.7.02-026, 2.7.02-028, 2.7.02-029, 2.7.02-038, 2.7.02-044, 2.7.02-113, 2.7.02-134, 2.7.02-139 -->

If a tail/rear light or brake light fails, likely causes include its bulb or
fuse; a starter fault is unrelated. A turn indicator that flashes much faster
than normal usually signals a failed bulb. Confirm operation before moving off
and repair the defect promptly. A continuously illuminated ESC/ESP lamp means
the system may be switched off or defective; if you did not deliberately
deactivate it, have the system checked. These lamps are warnings, not a reason
to ignore altered handling. Vehicle lighting requirements are set out in
[StVZO §§49a–54](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__49a.html).

### Exhaust, engine temperature and oil pressure

<!-- questions: 2.7.02-027, 2.7.02-034, 2.7.02-050 -->

An exhaust that suddenly becomes louder may be damaged or corroded. Arrange
immediate inspection: excessive noise is pollution and loose exhaust parts can
fall onto the road. If the oil-pressure warning lamp remains lit after starting,
switch off the engine immediately and check the oil level; continuing risks
severe engine damage. If coolant temperature is too high, stop at a suitable
place and switch off. Possible causes include a dirty radiator, failed fan, or
coolant leak/low level; inspect only when safe and have the cooling system
repaired. [StVZO §47](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__47.html)

### Mirrors, windows and washers

<!-- questions: 2.7.02-114, 2.7.02-115, 2.7.02-117, 2.7.02-118, 2.7.02-119, 2.7.02-120, 2.7.02-138 -->

Keep the required left exterior mirror (*Außenspiegel*) intact; replace broken
glass rather than relying on a partial view. If oncoming drivers flash at night,
you may be dazzling them: check dipped-beam aim and headlamp-range adjustment
for the load. In built-up areas it may also mean you forgot dipped headlights;
it is not an invitation to use main beam. A washer failure can be an empty
reservoir, blocked jets, a fuse, or a defective pump. Worn wiper blades or an
oil film explain poor wiping; a blown fuse or wiper motor can stop the wipers.
Clean visibility is a legal safety requirement. [StVZO §§50, 56](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__50.html)

### Seat belts and occupant protection

<!-- questions: 2.7.02-121, 2.7.02-122, 2.7.02-137, 2.7.02-140 -->

Every installed seat belt (*Sicherheitsgurt*) must latch and retract correctly.
A twisted belt or failed retractor can prevent winding; a lock or belt that is
defective must be repaired or professionally replaced. Do not use a passenger
seat with a defective belt, and do not rely on an airbag as a substitute.
[StVO §21a](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__21a.html) requires
the available seat belt to be worn; [StVZO §35a](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__35a.html)
sets equipment requirements.

## Common exam traps

- Low speed or counter-steering does not make a defective brake or steering system safe.
- Do not confuse brake-fluid level with hydraulic air, worn pads, or a leak.
- Very low tyre pressure increases heat and wear; it does not lengthen reaction time.
- Fast indicator flashing usually means a bulb fault; the starter is unrelated to lamps.
- A continuously lit ESC/ESP lamp can mean deactivation or a fault.
- Headlamp flashes from oncoming traffic are a warning about dazzling or forgotten dipped beam.

## Remember this

**1.6 mm; inspect pressure, tread, wear and damage. Test brakes after standing or vehicle handover. Any abnormal brake/steering behaviour means stop safely and arrange repair.**
