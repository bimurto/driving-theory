# Equipment of Vehicles — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

Tyres are safety equipment, not interchangeable accessories. Their permitted
speed, pressure, construction, and condition must match the vehicle and the
operating conditions. [StVZO §36](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__36.html)

## Learning goals

- interpret a dashboard speed sticker for low-speed winter tyres;
- understand the effect of winter tyres on dry-road handling; and
- set tyre pressure from the vehicle manufacturer's specification.

## Core rules

### A “Mit M+S 160” sticker is a binding speed ceiling

<!-- questions: 2.7.10-101 -->

The sticker means that the fitted tyres are approved only up to **160 km/h**.
While those tyres are fitted, do not exceed that speed—even if the road is dry
or wet. The speed limit belongs to the tyre's permitted maximum, not to the
weather. Exceeding it can overheat and damage the tyre. German law requires the
lower tyre speed to be displayed in the driver's field of view and not exceeded
when winter tyres have a lower permitted speed than the vehicle's design speed.
[StVZO §36(1), §36(5)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__36.html)

### Winter tyres change the vehicle's behaviour

<!-- questions: 2.7.10-102 -->

Winter tyres (*Winterreifen*) use a softer compound and different tread. They
can therefore feel and respond differently from summer tyres on a dry road:
steering precision, braking, noise, and cornering can change. Drive accordingly
and do not assume that “dry” makes them behave like summer tyres. In winter
weather, all wheels of a car must have tyres meeting the legal winter-tyre
requirements; the current legal symbol is the Alpine symbol (mountain and
snowflake), not merely the old M+S marking. [StVO §2(3a)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__2.html), [StVZO §36(4)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__36.html)

Check pressure when changing tyres. Follow the vehicle's placard/handbook; as a
theory-test rule of thumb, winter-tyre pressure may be set about **0.2 bar
higher** than the summer-tyre setting when the manufacturer gives that
recommendation. Do not lower it automatically: under-inflation worsens handling,
wear, and heat build-up. [StVZO §36(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__36.html)

## Situations and exceptions

- The sticker applies whenever the marked tyres are fitted, regardless of dry,
  rainy, or snowy road conditions.
- A speed sticker does not make it safe to drive at that speed; adapt speed to
  traffic, visibility, grip, and weather. [StVO §3](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)
- For current winter-weather compliance, use tyres bearing the Alpine symbol;
  M+S-only tyres are not the current legal definition of winter tyres.
- Pressure guidance is vehicle-specific. The approximately 0.2-bar increase is
  an exam convention, not a universal statutory value.

## Common exam traps

- Thinking “Mit M+S 160” applies only on snow or only on wet roads.
- Treating 160 km/h as a recommendation instead of a maximum.
- Assuming winter tyres handle identically to summer tyres on dry asphalt.
- Selecting lower, rather than slightly higher, pressure as the general rule.

## Remember this

- **Mit M+S 160 = never exceed 160 km/h while those tyres are fitted.**
- Winter tyres can handle differently on dry roads.
- Check the maker's pressure specification; the worksheet rule of thumb is about
  **+0.2 bar** versus summer tyres.
- Current winter-weather tyres require the **Alpine symbol**.
