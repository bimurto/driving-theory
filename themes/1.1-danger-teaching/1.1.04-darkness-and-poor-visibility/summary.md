# Darkness and Poor Visibility — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

At night, the danger is not only “less light.” Your eyes need time to adapt,
glare can erase detail, bright advertising can compete with signals, and pools
of darkness can hide people or vehicles. Reduce speed before your view becomes
insufficient and always keep enough visible distance to stop safely.

## Learning goals

After studying this chapter, you should be able to:

- manage the transition between bright and dark areas;
- respond safely to glare (*Blendung*);
- search dark gaps for pedestrians, cyclists, and poorly lit vehicles;
- distinguish traffic controls from distracting lights; and
- use lighting and speed correctly in poor visibility.

## Core rules

### Let your vision catch up with the light

<!-- questions: 1.1.04-001, 1.1.04-110 -->

Eyes adapt gradually when moving from a bright road or daylight into an unlit
road or underground car park. During that interval, obstacles and people are
harder to detect. Slow **before** entering the darker area, switch on the
required lights, and remain ready to brake. Entrances may also be narrow, with
pedestrians sharing vehicle lanes and kerbs close to the tyres.

German law requires lighting during darkness, twilight, or whenever visibility
conditions demand it, and requires speed to be adapted to visibility and the
road. [StVO §17(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__17.html) [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

### If dazzled, look right and slow down

<!-- questions: 1.1.04-103-B, 1.1.04-111 -->

Do not stare into oncoming headlights. Use the right-hand edge of the
carriageway as a guide, keep right, and reduce speed as far as necessary. If you
cannot see a safe stopping distance, you must slow until the visible-distance
rule is restored; in an extreme case this may mean stopping. [StVO §2(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__2.html) [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

Dip main beam in good time when an oncoming vehicle, a vehicle close ahead, or
anyone else could be dazzled. Do not answer glare with your own main beam.
[StVO §17(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__17.html)

### Search the shadows and ignore visual clutter

<!-- questions: 1.1.04-107, 1.1.04-108 -->

Bright shop signs and coloured advertising can make signals less conspicuous,
while alternating light and shadow can conceal pedestrians, parked vehicles,
and poorly lit road users. Scan deliberately beyond the brightest object, check
dark gaps, and choose a speed that lets you stop for something not yet fully
recognised. Other drivers may also detect you or the signal late, so preserve a
defensive margin even when your light is green. [StVO §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html) [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

### Road appearance does not decide priority

<!-- questions: 1.1.04-109 -->

A broad or continuously lit road may look like the priority route, and parked
vehicles may partly hide the junction. Neither impression overrides a
**Give way** sign (*Vorfahrt gewähren*, Zeichen 205). Act on the actual sign and
crossing rules, not on lighting or road width. Sign 205 requires yielding and
approaching at moderate speed; stop if the junction cannot be seen adequately.
[StVO Annex 2, sign 205](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/anlage_2.html)

## Situations and exceptions

- **Bright to dark:** reduce speed before the transition; expect delayed visual
  adaptation.
- **Glare:** look toward the right edge, keep right, and slow down; do not use
  main beam where it would dazzle others.
- **Urban lighting:** traffic lights and signs remain controlling even when
  advertising resembles or hides them.
- **Pools of darkness:** assume an unlit person or vehicle may be present.
- **Underground car park:** use the lighting required by visibility, drive
  slowly, allow for pedestrians, and protect tyres from narrow kerbs.

## Common exam traps

- Continuing at the same speed while waiting for the eyes to adapt.
- Looking directly at the dazzling headlights.
- Switching to main beam despite oncoming traffic.
- Assuming that a brightly lit or wider road has priority.
- Watching neon signs and failing to search for the actual signal.
- Treating illuminated sections as proof that the whole road is visible.
- Forgetting that other drivers may also be dazzled or distracted.

## Remember this

- **Dark transition:** slow first; eyes adapt later.
- **Dazzled:** look right, keep right, reduce speed.
- **Main beam:** dip before it dazzles anyone.
- **Dark gaps:** expect pedestrians and poorly lit vehicles.
- **Visual clutter:** find the real signal and signs.
- **Priority:** comes from the rules and signs, not road brightness or width.
- **Always:** be able to stop within the distance you can see.
