# Behavior Towards Pedestrians — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

Pedestrians have no protective shell, and some people cannot reliably see,
hear, understand, or react to traffic. German law therefore requires more than
simply keeping your formal priority: drive defensively, recognise vulnerability
early, reduce speed, remain ready to brake (*bremsbereit*), and stop whenever
that is needed to rule out danger.

This chapter combines that general duty with three recurring exam settings:
children and other vulnerable people, pedestrian crossings, and public-transport
stops. The safe approach is to expect the plausible mistake before it happens.

## Learning goals

After studying this chapter, you should be able to:

- react safely to children, older people, and people with disabilities;
- recognise official signs of severe visual impairment;
- approach a pedestrian crossing (*Fußgängerüberweg*, often *Zebrastreifen*);
- apply the different rules for buses and trams, with and without hazard lights;
- protect pedestrians and cyclists while turning or passing; and
- scan around parked vehicles and other visual obstructions.

## Core rules

### Vulnerable people: reduce speed and prepare to stop

<!-- questions: 1.1.02-004, 1.1.02-009-B, 1.1.02-027, 1.1.02-028, 1.1.02-029, 1.1.02-039-M, 1.1.02-048-M, 1.1.02-049, 1.1.02-050-M, 1.1.02-113, 1.1.02-117, 1.1.02-119, 1.1.02-126, 1.1.02-132, 1.1.02-135, 1.1.02-138-M -->

Toward children, people needing help, and older people, you must behave so that
their endangerment is excluded—especially by reducing speed and being ready to
brake. This is the statutory core of the exam phrase “the principle of trust
does not apply.” It does not assign blame; it tells you not to rely on perfect
behaviour from a visibly vulnerable person. [StVO §3(2a)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

Translate that duty into action:

- ease off the accelerator early and cover the brake;
- create lateral room and wait behind a wheelchair or pedestrian until passing
  is unquestionably safe;
- expect a person to stop, hesitate, reverse direction, or move sideways;
- stop rather than squeezing through an uncertain gap; and
- if an immediate collision is developing, brake decisively.

The general rule also requires constant caution and consideration, forbidding
avoidable danger or obstruction. A warning horn is not a substitute for speed
reduction; acoustic warnings are allowed only in the limited situations in
StVO §16 and may startle rather than protect a vulnerable person. [StVO §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html) [StVO §16](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__16.html)

If a wheelchair user is stuck at a kerb, secure the immediate situation, stop,
and offer practical help when it is safe to do so. Hazard warning lights may be
used to warn others of a danger created by your vehicle or of a danger ahead;
they do not create a right to stop unsafely. [StVO §16(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__16.html)

### Children: expect distraction and sudden movement

<!-- questions: 1.1.02-005, 1.1.02-022, 1.1.02-023, 1.1.02-038-M, 1.1.02-040-M, 1.1.02-041-M, 1.1.02-042-M, 1.1.02-043-M, 1.1.02-052, 1.1.02-107, 1.1.02-112, 1.1.02-114, 1.1.02-127, 1.1.02-134-M, 1.1.02-137-M, 1.1.02-202-M -->

Children may be distracted, misjudge distance or speed, locate sounds poorly,
copy another child, or change direction without warning. At lights or crossings
they may start too early, run back, or be pushed. A child cyclist may enter the
carriageway when a cycle path ends, turn without signalling, or follow a friend.
These are the precise kinds of hazard perception that the official theory
curriculum requires for conduct toward children and cyclists. [Annex 1,
section 7 FahrschAusbO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fahrschausbo_2012/anlage_1.html)

Treat a ball, toy, or dog as an advance warning that a child may follow it. Near
playing children, slow immediately—down to walking speed when the scene is so
close or unclear that only this lets you stop—and remain ready to brake. Stop if
the child's next move is uncertain. This applies even if your signal is green or
you otherwise have priority, because StVO §3(2a) requires danger to children to
be excluded. [StVO §3(2a)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

### Recognising and protecting visually impaired people

<!-- questions: 1.1.02-032, 1.1.02-036, 1.1.02-051-M, 1.1.02-129, 1.1.02-133 -->

Official indicators include a **white cane**, a guide dog in a **white harness**,
and a **yellow badge or armband with three black dots**. The yellow marking can
indicate physical disability generally; together with the other signs it helps
identify severe visual impairment. These markings are defined by federal law.
[FeV §2(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fev_2010/__2.html)

Do not wait for eye contact. Reduce speed, stop if necessary, and allow the
person to cross safely. Do not horn merely to hurry them: your heightened duty
is to eliminate the danger through speed choice and braking readiness.
[StVO §3(2a)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

### Pedestrian crossings: make your intention unmistakable

<!-- questions: 1.1.02-120-M, 1.1.02-121-M, 1.1.02-128 -->

At a marked pedestrian crossing, you must enable pedestrians—and users of
powered wheelchairs or wheelchairs—who visibly intend to use it to cross. Drive
up only at moderate speed and wait if necessary. Brake early enough that people
on either side can clearly recognise that you are yielding. Do not assume they
will look, continue steadily, or never turn back. Overtaking at the crossing is
prohibited. [StVO §26](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__26.html)

The legal phrase is not “pedestrians always have absolute priority everywhere.”
The special crossing duty applies when they visibly intend to use the marked
crossing; elsewhere, other rules—such as the turning rule—may require you to
wait. [StVO §26(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__26.html)

### Passing pedestrians, cyclists, and parked vehicles

<!-- questions: 1.1.02-124-M, 1.1.02-125-M, 1.1.02-130-M, 1.1.02-131 -->

Rows of parked vehicles create a combined hazard: people or children can emerge
from gaps, doors can open, vehicles can pull away, and your view is shortened.
Scan under and through vehicles where possible, leave door clearance, reduce
speed, and be prepared for hard braking. The danger sign **Zeichen 136 Kinder**
reinforces the need for special care, but the same observation duty applies
without the sign. [StVO Annex 1, sign 136](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/anlage_1.html) [StVO §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)

When overtaking a person on a bicycle, an e-scooter, or on foot, keep sufficient
lateral distance. For motor vehicles overtaking cyclists, pedestrians, and
e-scooter users, the statutory minimum is **1.5 m in built-up areas** and **2 m
outside built-up areas**; if that cannot be achieved, wait. [StVO §5(4)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__5.html)

### Turning: protect straight-ahead foot and cycle traffic

<!-- questions: 1.1.02-123-M -->

When turning, give particular consideration to pedestrians and wait if needed;
straight-ahead cyclists and e-scooter users travelling alongside you must also
be allowed through. A large turning vehicle can hide a pedestrian who waited
for it or somebody approaching from the other side, so do not follow through
blindly—recheck the crossing after the vehicle clears. [StVO §9(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__9.html)

## Situations and exceptions

### Buses with hazard warning lights

<!-- questions: 1.1.02-026-B, 1.1.02-046-M, 1.1.02-108-B, 1.1.02-110-B -->

The sequence matters:

1. A scheduled-service or marked school bus **approaching a stop with hazard
   lights on may not be overtaken**.
2. Once it is **stopped at the stop with hazard lights on**, vehicles may pass
   only at walking speed (*Schrittgeschwindigkeit*), at enough distance to rule
   out danger to passengers. The same walking-speed rule applies to oncoming
   traffic on the same carriageway.
3. Passengers may not be obstructed; stop and wait if necessary.

These are explicit requirements of [StVO §20(3)–(4)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__20.html).

### Buses and trams without hazard warning lights

<!-- questions: 1.1.02-024-B, 1.1.02-037, 1.1.02-109-B, 1.1.02-115, 1.1.02-116, 1.1.02-122, 1.1.02-136-M, 1.1.02-201-M -->

Pass a bus or tram stopped at a marked stop only cautiously, including from the
opposite direction. If passengers are boarding or alighting across your path,
you may pass on the right only at walking speed and with enough distance to
exclude danger; do not obstruct them, and wait when necessary. These duties do
not depend on passengers yielding to moving traffic. [StVO §20(1)–(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__20.html)

Even where a stop island means passengers should wait on the island, anticipate
people running across the road to catch the tram or stepping out from in front
of a bus. The absence of hazard lights removes the special all-traffic
walking-speed rule of §20(4), but it does **not** remove the duty to pass
cautiously or the special rule while boarding and alighting. [StVO §20(1),
(2), and (6)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__20.html)

## Common exam traps

- Treating a green light or formal priority as permission to ignore a child.
- Honking instead of slowing and preparing to stop.
- Assuming a child, older person, or wheelchair user will continue predictably.
- Confusing a stopped bus with hazard lights with one without them.
- Forgetting that the hazard-light walking-speed rule also reaches oncoming
  traffic on the **same carriageway**.
- Passing a bus that is still approaching its stop with hazard lights on.
- Waiting for eye contact from a blind or severely visually impaired person.
- Looking only at the visible pedestrian and not behind a bus, lorry, van, or
  row of parked cars.
- Treating a zebra crossing as a place where late, ambiguous braking is enough.

## Remember this

- **Children, older people, people needing help:** slow down, cover the brake,
  and exclude danger.
- **Ball or dog:** expect a child to follow.
- **White cane / white guide-dog harness / yellow three-dot marking:** heightened
  care; stop if necessary.
- **Zebra crossing:** moderate approach speed, yield clearly, no overtaking.
- **Bus approaching with hazards:** no overtaking.
- **Bus stopped with hazards:** walking speed in both directions on the same
  carriageway; do not endanger or obstruct passengers.
- **Bus or tram stopped without hazards:** pass cautiously; walking speed on the
  right while passengers board or alight.
- **Turning or obstructed view:** search again for hidden pedestrians and cyclists.
