# Speed — Class B Study Summary

## Core rules

### Match speed to sight distance and conflict points

Crossroads, junctions, pedestrian crossings, blind bends, narrowings, and level
crossings combine limited space or visibility with likely conflicts. High speed
means less time to notice a person, vehicle, train, obstacle, or surface change,
and more distance before the vehicle stops. Slow early enough that you can keep
control and stop within the distance you can see. [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

At a dangerously narrow road where an oncoming vehicle could be endangered,
you must be able to stop within **half** the visible distance. At level
crossings, approach at moderate speed and obey the separate waiting duties when
a rail vehicle approaches or signals require it. [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html) [StVO §19(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__19.html)

### Brake before a tight bend

Reduce speed while the vehicle is still straight, enter the bend at a speed
that preserves grip and lane position, and accelerate again only as the road
straightens and visibility permits. Abrupt braking or acceleration in the bend
uses grip needed for steering and can start a skid. The legal basis remains the
duty to retain control and adapt speed to the road and visibility. [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

### A wide residential road can hide multiple hazards

A broad, quiet road can tempt drivers to accelerate, but parked vehicles may
hide children and make side junctions hard to recognise. Expect children to run
out and vehicles to approach from the right. Where no sign, signal, or other
special rule controls the junction, **right before left** (*rechts vor links*)
applies. Reduce speed enough to check each junction and stop for a child.
[StVO §8(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__8.html) [StVO §3(2a)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

## Situations and exceptions

### Bus stopped with hazard warning lights

If a scheduled-service or marked school bus is stopped at a marked stop with
hazard warning lights on, pass only at **walking speed**
(*Schrittgeschwindigkeit*) and with enough distance to exclude danger to
passengers. The same walking-speed rule applies to oncoming traffic on the same
carriageway. Do not obstruct passengers; stop and wait when necessary.
[StVO §20(4)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__20.html)

Search in front of and behind the bus for people crossing or running to catch
it. If the bus is still approaching the stop with hazard lights on, it may not
be overtaken at all. [StVO §20(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__20.html)

## Common exam traps

- Treating a posted maximum as the safe speed for a blind or narrow location.
- Braking hard only after entering a tight bend.
- Accelerating before the vehicle straightens and the exit is visible.
- Assuming a wide residential road has priority or contains no children.
- Looking only at the bus and not for people hidden by it.
- Confusing “bus approaching with hazards” (no overtaking) with “bus stopped
  with hazards” (pass only under the walking-speed conditions).

## Remember this

- **Blind, narrow, crossing, or junction:** lose speed early.
- **Bend:** brake before, steer smoothly, accelerate after it straightens.
- **Residential road:** scan for children and *rechts vor links* junctions.
- **Bus approaching with hazards:** do not overtake.
- **Bus stopped with hazards:** walking speed, safe distance, stop if necessary.
- **Always:** stop within the visible distance and retain control.
