# Road and Weather Conditions — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

Rain, snow, ice, oil, leaves, rails, and uneven surfaces reduce the margin
between normal driving and loss of control. The correct response is a package:
identify the hazard early, reduce speed before reaching it, increase distance,
make steering and pedal inputs smooth, and preserve enough clear road to stop.

The posted maximum is only a ceiling. German law requires a speed adapted to
the road, traffic, visibility, weather, driver, vehicle, and load, and requires
you to be able to stop within the visible distance. [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

## Learning goals

After studying this chapter, you should be able to:

- recognise places where ice, mud, water, leaves, oil, or low grip is likely;
- adapt speed, distance, steering, acceleration, and braking to reduced grip;
- apply the visibility and lighting rules in fog and heavy rain;
- handle crossings, bends, pedestrians, and lane changes under poor conditions;
  and
- distinguish statutory rules from cautious techniques such as a grip test.

## Core rules

### Wet roads: more distance, less speed, clearer observation

<!-- questions: 1.1.03-002, 1.1.03-007-M, 1.1.03-119-M, 1.1.03-121 -->

Water reduces tyre-road contact and lengthens braking distance. Spray from the
vehicle ahead can suddenly obscure your view, and standing water can hide
potholes or uneven surfaces. Reduce speed, avoid strong acceleration, and
increase the following distance so that you can still react and stop within the
distance you can see. [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html) [StVO §4(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__4.html)

In heavy rain, use dipped headlights even in daytime when visibility is
substantially impaired. Rear fog lights are **not** rain lights; they are allowed
only when fog reduces visibility below 50 m. [StVO §17(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__17.html)

### Low-grip surfaces: finish major inputs before the hazard

<!-- questions: 1.1.03-001, 1.1.03-004, 1.1.03-005, 1.1.03-102-B, 1.1.03-104, 1.1.03-120 -->

Expect mud at building sites and farm-track junctions; wet leaves and frost in
shaded tree-lined roads; and varying grip on cobbles and tram rails, especially
when wet. Oil is an acute skid hazard. On any such surface:

- reduce speed **before** it;
- avoid abrupt acceleration, braking, or steering while on it;
- keep the vehicle as straight and balanced as the situation permits; and
- leave extra distance because available tyre grip is reduced.

These actions follow the statutory duty to adapt speed to the road and to retain
control of the vehicle. [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

Do not make a sudden evasive manoeuvre around an oil spill. Warn or protect the
scene without endangering yourself and report the hazard to the responsible
emergency service; substances that can endanger or impede traffic must be
removed promptly or adequately marked. [StVO §32(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__32.html)

### Ice and snow: anticipate the hidden patches

<!-- questions: 1.1.03-003, 1.1.03-105, 1.1.03-108-B -->

Bridges cool from above and below, while forests and shaded roads retain cold
and moisture. Both can ice before an open road. Snow also narrows usable space,
hides markings or edges, lengthens braking, and makes a bend or obstacle more
demanding. Slow before these areas and keep well right where visibility is
limited. [StVO §2(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__2.html) [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

If you need to assess possible ice, a **careful, light brake application at very
low speed** can be an exam-safe technique only where it cannot affect traffic
behind or cause loss of control. It is not a statutory test and never justifies
abrupt braking. When ice is already visible, avoid acceleration, braking, and
steering changes on the patch as far as possible.

For a Class B car, winter-condition tyres are legally required on all wheels
when driving in black ice, packed snow, slush, ice, or frost. Suitable tyres do
not cancel the need for adapted speed. [StVO §2(3a)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__2.html)

### Read the whole scene, not only the surface

<!-- questions: 1.1.03-006-M, 1.1.03-114-M -->

Poor weather compounds ordinary hazards. At an unregulated junction, a vehicle
may still approach from the right; snow may narrow the carriageway; and an
uneven, blind bend may conceal oncoming traffic cutting the corner. Reduce speed
far enough in advance to stop in the visible section and keep right at bends or
other unclear locations. [StVO §2(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__2.html) [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html) [StVO §8(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__8.html)

## Numbers and formulas

### The 50-metre fog threshold

<!-- questions: 1.1.03-109, 1.1.03-110, 1.1.03-118 -->

Fog can form suddenly near rivers, lakes, and marshy ground. Adapt speed early,
increase distance, and keep enough clear view to stop. If fog, snowfall, or rain
reduces visibility below **50 m**, the maximum is **50 km/h**, and a lower speed
may still be required. [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

Use dipped headlights in significantly impaired visibility. The **rear fog
light** (*Nebelschlussleuchte*) may be used only when **fog**—not rain or
snow—reduces visibility below 50 m. [StVO §17(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__17.html)

The familiar roadside delineator spacing is often about 50 m, but treat it only
as a practical visual aid, not a universal measurement guarantee.

## Situations and exceptions

### Crossings and turns on slippery roads

<!-- questions: 1.1.03-112-M, 1.1.03-116 -->

Brake early and gently for a pedestrian crossing; a missing or snow-covered
road marking does not erase duties signalled by the remaining crossing signs.
Pedestrians who visibly intend to use the crossing must be enabled to cross,
with a moderate-speed approach and waiting where necessary. [StVO §26(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__26.html)

At a slippery turn, reduce speed before steering. Expect your vehicle or a
vehicle emerging from the side road to skid, and preserve stopping distance for
pedestrians crossing the side street. A turning driver must show special
consideration to pedestrians and wait if necessary. [StVO §9(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__9.html)

### Snow can put pedestrians on the carriageway

<!-- questions: 1.1.03-117 -->

Outside built-up areas, snow may make a footway unusable and force people
walking to or from a parking place onto the road. Expect them around parked
vehicles, slow down, and leave room. Pedestrians generally use the carriageway
when neither a usable pavement nor shoulder is available; outside built-up
areas they normally walk at the left edge where reasonable. [StVO §25(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__25.html)

### Motorway exits: weather does not replace the blind-spot check

<!-- questions: 1.1.03-115 -->

Before moving onto a motorway exit lane, check mirrors, signal, and perform the
direct shoulder/blind-spot check. A motorcycle already on the deceleration lane
can disappear beside the car. A lane change is permitted only when danger to
other traffic is excluded and must be announced clearly in good time.
[StVO §7(5)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__7.html)

## Common exam traps

- Braking or steering sharply **on** ice instead of slowing beforehand.
- Keeping the dry-road gap in rain because the vehicle ahead still seems clear.
- Watching spray but missing water-hidden potholes and puddles.
- Assuming only bridges ice early; shade, forests, and avenues matter too.
- Treating winter tyres as permission to maintain normal speed.
- Using the rear fog light in heavy rain or when fog visibility is 50 m or more.
- Driving at 50 km/h in sub-50-m visibility when a lower speed is necessary.
- Concentrating on snow while forgetting priority, pedestrians, bends, and
  blind spots.
- Testing grip with traffic close behind or at more than very low speed.

## Remember this

- **Less grip:** less speed, more distance, smoother inputs.
- **Wet road:** expect longer braking, spray, and hidden unevenness.
- **Bridge / forest / shade:** expect early or sudden ice.
- **Oil / mud / leaves / rails:** avoid abrupt braking and steering.
- **Visibility below 50 m:** no more than 50 km/h; slower if necessary.
- **Rear fog light:** only fog and visibility below 50 m.
- **Snow:** expect narrower roads, hidden markings, and pedestrians in the road.
- **Lane change:** mirrors, signal, direct blind-spot check.
