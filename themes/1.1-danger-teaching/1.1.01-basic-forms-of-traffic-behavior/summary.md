# Basic Forms of Traffic Behavior — Class B Study Summary

## Core rules

### Defensive driving means preventing conflict, not claiming priority

The StVO's basic rule is **constant caution and mutual consideration** (*ständige
Vorsicht und gegenseitige Rücksicht*). You must behave so that nobody is harmed
or endangered and nobody is obstructed or inconvenienced more than unavoidable.
That is the legal foundation of defensive driving. [StVO §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)

In practice, allow for another person's error and, where necessary to avoid a
danger, do not insist on your right of way (*nicht auf Vorfahrt bestehen*). This
does not mean stopping at every junction without cause: the official curriculum
teaches thinking for others, patience, and sometimes giving up priority as
situation-dependent choices. [FahrschAusbO Annex 1, topic 5](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fahrschausbo_2012/anlage_1.html)

### Observe the developing situation, not only the vehicle in front

Anticipatory driving means scanning the wider traffic space, reading other road
users' behaviour and likely intentions, and responding early to a probable
change. The practical curriculum expressly requires observation of other road
users, their driving behaviour, and the whole traffic space. [FahrschAusbO Annex 3, topic 14](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fahrschausbo_2012/anlage_3.html)

Use clues, but do not turn them into assumptions. For example, a non-local
registration plate may suggest that a driver is searching for a route. Leave
space and be ready for late signalling, sudden braking, or an unexpected stop.
The safe response follows the duties of caution and consideration; the plate is
only a prompt to observe, not proof of what the driver will do. [StVO §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)

### Following distance protects against sudden braking

Rear-end collisions commonly combine three factors: the lead vehicle brakes
hard, the following driver notices too late, and the gap is too short. Your
distance must normally be large enough to stop behind the vehicle ahead even if
it brakes suddenly; conversely, a driver in front must not brake sharply without
a compelling reason. [StVO §4(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__4.html)

Serious crashes are therefore not explained by one isolated mistake. Poor
observation, insufficient distance, and speed not adapted to the situation can
reinforce one another. The StVO requires a speed at which the vehicle remains
under control, adapted to road, traffic, visibility, weather, personal ability,
vehicle, and load. [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

### Learn an unfamiliar vehicle before demanding normal performance

Before driving a vehicle you do not know, locate and understand its controls and
learn how it steers, turns, accelerates, and brakes. Dimensions, mass, centre of
gravity, transmission, and control layout may differ from the vehicle you know.
Begin cautiously and create room to learn its responses in a safe setting. The
official practical curriculum starts with checking roadworthiness and learning
the controls; it also requires practice in speed choice and emergency braking.
[FahrschAusbO Annex 3, topics 1 and 8](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fahrschausbo_2012/anlage_3.html)

The driver remains responsible for an unobstructed view and hearing, a compliant
vehicle, and its traffic safety. If a defect arising during the trip materially
impairs safety and cannot promptly be corrected, the vehicle must be removed
from traffic by the shortest route. [StVO §23(1)–(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__23.html)

### Avoid creating hazards through unjustified slow driving or breakdown

A motor vehicle must not be driven so slowly **without good reason** (*ohne
triftigen Grund*) that it obstructs traffic flow. The qualification matters:
slowing for a real hazard, poor visibility, or a vulnerable road user can be
necessary; creeping along for no reason can produce large speed differences,
rear-end risk, queues, and pressure for unsafe overtaking. [StVO §3(2), with the
general speed duty in §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

Prevent foreseeable stoppages: practise smooth starts so you are less likely to
stall in dense traffic, and check fuel or charge before the journey. A stationary
or broken-down vehicle can itself become an obstacle. If it cannot be recognised
in time, switch on the hazard-warning lights immediately and place a conspicuous
warning sign at an adequate distance—about 100 m in fast traffic. [StVO §15](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__15.html)

Correct speed is therefore neither automatically fast nor automatically slow.
It is the speed that keeps the vehicle controlled and fits the current situation.
A stationary vehicle and unsuitable speed are dangers; properly used lights are
not a danger merely because it is daytime. Lighting is mandatory when visibility
requires it and may improve conspicuity, but it must not dazzle others. [StVO
§17(1)–(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__17.html)

### Keep tyres and headlights safe

Worn tyres reduce the available grip, especially on wet roads, and leave less
margin for braking and steering. For ordinary motor-vehicle and trailer
pneumatic tyres, the legal minimum main-tread depth is **1.6 mm** around the full
circumference; a tyre can still warrant earlier replacement because condition
and performance matter as well as the minimum number. [StVZO §36(1), (3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__36.html)

Headlights set too high can dazzle oncoming traffic. Headlamps must be adjustable,
secured against unintended movement, and meet the dipped-beam glare limits; the
driver must also dip in good time for oncoming or closely preceding traffic.
[StVZO §50(3), (6)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__50.html) and
[StVO §17(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__17.html)

### Treat tiredness as an immediate loss of driving capability

Heavy eyelids and repeated yawning are warning signs, not challenges to overcome.
Fatigue reduces attention and concentration, restricts accurate perception and
judgment, and delays reactions. If ignored, it can progress to driving errors and
involuntary **microsleep** (*Sekundenschlaf*). German driver training explicitly
covers fatigue, distraction, attention deficits, and lack of concentration as
limitations of physical ability. [FahrschAusbO Annex 1, topic 1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fahrschausbo_2012/anlage_1.html)

At the first signs, stop safely and take a break straight away. A short sleep is
the effective response; movement and fresh air during the break can help activate
the circulation. Music, an open window, or willpower is not a substitute for rest.
[German Road Safety Council (DVR): fatigue advice](https://www.dvr.de/presse/presseinformationen/hellwach-trotz-zeitumstellung)

Do not continue if fatigue makes you unable to drive safely. Driving despite
mental or physical incapacity can become a criminal offence if it endangers
another person or valuable property. [StGB §315c(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__315c.html)

### Remove distractions before they remove your control

Distraction, inattention, and fatigue can make a driver drift out of the lane or
leave the road. Keep attention on the traffic space and do not hold or pick up a
phone or other covered electronic device while driving. Permitted use requires
hands-free operation plus voice/read-aloud functions, or only a brief glance
adapted to the conditions. [StVO §23(1a)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__23.html)

## Numbers and checks

- **1.6 mm:** statutory minimum main-tread depth for ordinary motor-vehicle and
  trailer pneumatic tyres; it is a legal floor, not a guarantee of strong wet
  grip. [StVZO §36(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__36.html)
- **About 100 m:** orientation distance for a warning sign after a breakdown in
  fast traffic; the legally controlling phrase is an adequate distance for the
  actual situation. [StVO §15](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__15.html)
- Before moving an unfamiliar vehicle: controls, seat and mirrors, steering
  response, dimensions, brakes, tyres, lights, and fuel or charge.

## Situations and exceptions

- Yielding a right may prevent a collision, but stopping everywhere without a
  reason can itself confuse or obstruct traffic.
- A hesitant or non-local driver deserves extra space, not impatience; confirm
  intentions through observation rather than relying on the number plate.
- Slow down whenever safety requires it. The prohibition concerns unjustified
  slow driving that obstructs traffic, not a necessary safety reduction.
- Daytime lights are not inherently hazardous. Incorrect aim, inappropriate
  high beam, or other dazzling use is hazardous.
- If tiredness appears, the decision point has already arrived: stop somewhere
  safe and recover before deciding whether to continue.

## Common exam traps

- Treating defensive driving as automatic stopping or excessive caution.
- Watching only the vehicle directly ahead instead of the developing traffic
  situation.
- Expecting a local-looking driver to behave predictably, or treating a
  non-local plate as proof rather than a warning clue.
- Blaming only the vehicle that brakes; the following driver still needs enough
  distance for sudden braking.
- Thinking that very slow always means very safe.
- Remembering tyre tread but forgetting headlight aim and glare.
- Believing stimulating music makes a tired driver fit to continue.
- Mistaking fatigue for faster reactions; it delays reaction and degrades
  attention, perception, and judgment.
- Treating lights used during daytime as dangerous in themselves.

## Remember this

- **Defensive:** expect mistakes; prevent harm; do not insist on priority.
- **Anticipatory:** scan far and wide; identify likely changes; act early.
- Keep enough distance to stop if the vehicle ahead brakes suddenly.
- Learn the controls, braking, and manoeuvrability of an unfamiliar vehicle.
- Avoid both unsuitable speed and avoidable stoppages.
- Worn tyres and dazzling headlights are genuine safety defects.
- Heavy eyelids or repeated yawning mean: stop safely and rest now.
- Distraction, inattention, and fatigue can all take the vehicle out of its lane.
