# Alcohol, Drugs, and Medication — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

Alcohol, cannabis, other intoxicants, some medicines, and fatigue can impair
perception, concentration, judgement, reaction, and vehicle control. Feeling
awake or confident is not evidence of fitness to drive (*Fahrtüchtigkeit*).
The safe rule is simple: if a substance or condition may impair you, do not
drive and arrange another way home.

## Learning goals

After studying this chapter, you should be able to:

- explain why coffee, sleep, or exercise cannot speed alcohol elimination;
- recognise early alcohol-related performance losses;
- distinguish current alcohol, THC, and other-drug legal rules;
- understand why cannabis and other drugs remain unpredictable;
- check medicines for driving impairment; and
- distinguish immediate driving ability from long-term driving fitness
  (*Fahreignung*).

## Core rules

### There is no quick way to sober up

<!-- questions: 1.1.09-001, 1.1.09-006, 1.1.09-029 -->

Coffee, a short sleep, a walk, cold air, or exercise may change how alert you
feel, but they do not accelerate alcohol elimination. Only time does. As a
theory-test rule of thumb, use about **0.1‰ per hour**, so **1.0‰ takes roughly
10 hours** to clear; real rates vary, so never calculate a departure time with
no safety margin. Germany's federal alcohol-prevention service gives typical
rates of about 0.1‰ per hour for women and 0.1–0.2‰ for men and explicitly says
the process cannot be accelerated by coffee or exercise. [BIÖG “Kenn dein
Limit” — alcohol breakdown](https://www.kenn-dein-limit.de/fakten-ueber-alkohol/alkoholabbau-im-check-wie-lange-bleibt-alkohol-im-blut/)

Residual alcohol (*Restalkohol*) can therefore remain the next morning. If in
doubt, do not drive.

### Small amounts can impair before you feel drunk

<!-- questions: 1.1.09-007, 1.1.09-008, 1.1.09-024 -->

Alcohol can narrow the useful field of view, degrade spatial and distance
judgement, slow reactions, reduce hearing and visual performance, and increase
risk-taking. Drinking quickly or on an empty stomach makes the blood-alcohol
level rise faster; sedatives, sleeping tablets, or some painkillers can amplify
dangerous effects. Federal health information notes changes in inhibition and
fine motor control even below 0.5‰ and worsening judgement, coordination, and
reaction as levels rise. [gesund.bund.de — alcohol poisoning and effects](https://gesund.bund.de/alkoholvergiftung)

Do not use a statutory threshold as a “safe amount.” Driving while actually
unable to drive safely because of alcohol or another intoxicant is a criminal
offence regardless of whether a particular administrative threshold is the
issue. [StGB §316](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__316.html)

### Drugs and medication can impair in different ways

<!-- questions: 1.1.09-003, 1.1.09-004, 1.1.09-009, 1.1.09-030, 1.1.09-032, 1.1.09-033 -->

Cannabis, cocaine, heroin, amphetamines, LSD, methamphetamine, and other
psychoactive substances can cause intoxication, faulty perceptions,
hallucinations, reduced concentration, distorted speed estimates, increased
risk-taking, overconfidence, or sudden exhaustion. They can also cause
dependence. Fatigue can independently produce the same dangerous losses of
attention and reaction.

Some prescription and non-prescription medicines also impair driving. Read the
leaflet, follow medical and pharmacy advice, and ask specifically about driving
and alcohol interactions. A prescription does not guarantee that you are safe
to drive; although StVG contains a limited prescribed-use exception to certain
administrative drug provisions, criminal liability for actually unsafe driving
can still apply. [StVG §24a(4)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvg/__24a.html) [StGB §316](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__316.html)

### Cannabis effects and detection are not a reliable clock

<!-- questions: 1.1.09-015, 1.1.09-018, 1.1.09-019, 1.1.09-025, 1.1.09-026, 1.1.09-027, 1.1.09-031 -->

Cannabis can reduce attention to other traffic, concentration, reaction, danger
awareness, and the ability to judge distance and speed; confusion, depression,
sensory distortion, or hallucinations are possible. Effects vary with product,
dose, route, person, and co-use, so elapsed time alone cannot prove safe driving.
The official federal training curriculum explicitly includes alcohol, drugs,
medicines, fatigue, and attention deficits as fitness risks. [Annex 1,
section 1 FahrschAusbO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fahrschausbo_2012/anlage_1.html)

THC is metabolised differently from alcohol. Active THC in blood and inactive
metabolites in urine have different detection windows; with repeated use,
metabolites can remain detectable for weeks. A positive urine screen therefore
does not itself measure current driving ability. The federal prevention portal
notes that duration depends on amount, frequency, individual metabolism, time,
and test sensitivity. [drugcom.de — cannabis detection](https://www.drugcom.de/wissenstests/cannabis/weitere-informationen-zu-cannabis/nachweis-von-cannabis/)

### One use can be enough to make driving unsafe

<!-- questions: 1.1.09-010, 1.1.09-028 -->

Hashish/marijuana, heroin, cocaine, amphetamines, LSD, or crystal meth can make
a person temporarily unfit after a single use. Methamphetamine can combine
hallucinations and severe overconfidence with a later crash in alertness. Never
drive based on feeling “normal”; if there is any possible remaining effect, do
not drive. [StGB §316](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__316.html)

## Numbers and formulas

### Current statutory thresholds are not safety targets

<!-- questions: 1.1.09-021, 1.1.09-022 -->

- **Probationary period or under 21:** consuming alcohol or THC while driving,
  or starting while under its effect, is prohibited. The prescribed-medicine
  exception in §24c(3) applies to THC from proper use for a specific medical
  case. [StVG §24c](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvg/__24c.html)
- **Alcohol administrative threshold:** 0.25 mg/l breath alcohol or **0.5‰**
  blood alcohol. [StVG §24a(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvg/__24a.html)
- **THC administrative threshold:** **3.5 ng/ml THC in blood serum**.
  [StVG §24a(1a)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvg/__24a.html)
- **Other listed intoxicants:** driving under their effect is an administrative
  offence when the listed substance is detected in blood serum, subject to the
  statutory prescribed-use exception. [StVG §24a(2), (4) and its
  Annex](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvg/anlage.html)

These provisions do not authorise impaired driving below a number. If alcohol
or another intoxicant makes you unable to drive safely, StGB §316 can apply; if
that state creates danger to another person or valuable property, StGB §315c
allows imprisonment up to five years in the intentional offence described
there. [StGB §316](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__316.html) [StGB
§315c](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__315c.html)

## Situations and exceptions

### Consequences and return to driving fitness

<!-- questions: 1.1.09-014, 1.1.09-017 -->

Drug-impaired driving can lead to fines, a driving ban, criminal penalties, and
loss of the licence, depending on the facts. A medical or medical-psychological
assessment (*medizinisch-psychologisches Gutachten*, commonly MPU) may be
required where the FeV criteria are met; it is not an automatic consequence of
every single case. For cannabis, FeV §13a specifies assessment grounds such as
suspected dependence or misuse and repeated violations. [FeV §13a](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fev_2010/__13a.html)

Dependence means no fitness to drive. After cannabis dependence, fitness may
normally be accepted only when dependence has ended and **usually one year of
abstinence** is proven; after detoxification and withdrawal from other addictive
drugs, Annex 4 likewise specifies one year of abstinence with regular controls.
[FeV Annex 4, items 9.2–9.5](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fev_2010/anlage_4.html)

## Dataset warning

The worksheet explanations for **1.1.09-004** and **1.1.09-010** use outdated
or over-broad shorthand. A blood-alcohol value of 0.3‰ does not by itself make
every driver automatically criminally unfit; actual unsafe driving and the
applicable statutory provisions matter. Nor is it current to say there are “no
limits for drugs”: since 2024, StVG §24a expressly sets **3.5 ng/ml THC in blood
serum**, while listed other intoxicants follow the detection rule in §24a(2).
The safest study rule remains zero consumption before driving. [StVG §24a](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvg/__24a.html) [StGB §316](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__316.html)

## Common exam traps

- Believing coffee, sleep, a walk, or a cold shower removes alcohol faster.
- Using 0.1‰ per hour as a guarantee rather than a rough exam estimate.
- Treating an administrative threshold as a safe-driving target.
- Assuming legal cannabis means driving after cannabis is permitted.
- Confusing urine-metabolite detection with current impairment.
- Ignoring medicine warnings because the product was prescribed.
- Assuming one drug use cannot impair for hours.
- Treating an MPU or one-year abstinence rule as identical in every case.

## Remember this

- **Sober-up tricks:** none work; only time reduces blood alcohol.
- **Exam estimate:** roughly 0.1‰ per hour, with real variation.
- **Under 21 / probation:** alcohol and THC prohibition under §24c.
- **Current THC threshold:** 3.5 ng/ml blood serum—but not a safety target.
- **Medicine:** read the leaflet and ask; prescription does not ensure fitness.
- **Drugs:** effects and detection vary; never calculate your way into driving.
- **Any actual impairment:** do not drive, regardless of the number.
