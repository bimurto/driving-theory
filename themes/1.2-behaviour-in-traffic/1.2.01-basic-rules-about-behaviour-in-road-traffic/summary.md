# Basic Rules About Behaviour in Road Traffic — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

German road traffic begins with a general duty, not a claim to priority:
constant caution (*ständige Vorsicht*) and mutual consideration
(*gegenseitige Rücksicht*). Drive so that another person's foreseeable mistake
does not automatically become a crash.

## Learning goals

After studying this chapter, you should be able to:

- translate constant caution into anticipatory driving; and
- identify road users who require heightened care.

## Core rules

### Drive ahead of the situation, not merely within your rights

<!-- questions: 1.2.01-001 -->

Expect that another person may overlook you, misunderstand a rule, hesitate, or
make an error. Observe well ahead, preserve time and space, communicate clearly,
and be willing to slow or yield when that avoids danger. StVO §1 requires
constant caution and mutual consideration and prohibits conduct that harms,
endangers, or more than unavoidably obstructs or annoys another person.
[StVO §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)

This does not mean treating every rule as optional. Follow priority, signs, and
signals, but do not insist on your right when a collision is already foreseeable.
The official theory curriculum expressly teaches thinking for other road users
and, where appropriate, giving up priority with patience. [Annex 1, section 5
FahrschAusbO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fahrschausbo_2012/anlage_1.html)

### Give vulnerable people a larger safety margin

<!-- questions: 1.2.01-003 -->

Show particular care toward children and people who visibly need help or have a
disability. Do not rely on them seeing, hearing, understanding, or reacting like
a fully able and experienced adult. Reduce speed and remain ready to brake so
that danger to children, people needing help, and older people is excluded.
[StVO §3(2a)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

The same defensive logic applies whenever another person's behaviour visibly
shows that reliance would be unsafe—for example an unsteady cyclist. Preserve
distance and respond to the actual risk under the general caution rule.
[StVO §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)

## Situations and exceptions

- Having priority does not remove the duty to avoid a foreseeable collision.
- Expecting mistakes is not permission for arbitrary braking; actions should be
  timely, predictable, and proportionate to the observed risk.
- Heightened care follows both recognised vulnerable groups and visible signs
  that a particular person cannot behave reliably.

## Common exam traps

- Equating “I have priority” with “I may continue regardless.”
- Watching only the vehicle directly ahead instead of the developing scene.
- Assuming a child or visibly disabled person will react predictably.
- Treating consideration as mere politeness rather than a legal safety duty.
- Responding to someone else's error with aggression or counter-pressure.

## Remember this

- **StVO §1:** constant caution and mutual consideration.
- **Drive with foresight:** expect plausible errors and preserve an escape margin.
- **Priority:** follow it, but never use it to justify a collision.
- **Children and visibly vulnerable people:** slow down and be ready to brake.
