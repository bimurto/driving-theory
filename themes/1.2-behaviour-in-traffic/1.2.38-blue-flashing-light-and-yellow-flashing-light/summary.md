# Blue Flashing Light and Yellow Flashing Light — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

Blue flashing light with the emergency siren (*Einsatzhorn*) commands everyone
to clear the way immediately. Blue light alone is a warning, not the same legal
command. Yellow flashing light warns of a hazard that demands particular care.
In every case, respond predictably and avoid creating a second danger.

## Learning goals

After studying this chapter, you should be able to:

- react safely to an emergency vehicle using blue light and siren;
- locate an approaching vehicle before choosing an escape path;
- distinguish blue light alone from blue light with siren;
- form a rescue corridor where required; and
- recognise the hazards indicated by yellow flashing light.

## Core rules

### Blue light plus siren: clear the way immediately

<!-- questions: 1.2.38-001, 1.2.38-104 -->

When blue flashing light and the two-tone siren are used together, all road users
must clear a path **immediately** (*sofort freie Bahn schaffen*). Check mirrors,
signal if useful, reduce speed smoothly, and move aside without endangering
pedestrians, cyclists, oncoming traffic, or the emergency crew. Do not brake
abruptly or make an unpredictable turn. [StVO §38(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__38.html) [StVO §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)

Normally move to the right edge and stop if that creates the clearest route. If
there is genuinely no other way, carefully using a small part of the pavement
may be necessary to fulfil the emergency command—but only slowly, only as far
as required, and never at the expense of people on the pavement. The result must
be space for the emergency vehicle without a new danger. [StVO §38(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__38.html) [StVO §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)

### Hear the siren: locate first, then choose your movement

<!-- questions: 1.2.38-002 -->

Ask two questions immediately: **Where is the emergency vehicle coming from?**
and **Could my position obstruct it?** Check mirrors and intersections before
moving. A calm, informed movement creates space faster than reacting blindly to
sound whose direction you have not identified. [StVO §38(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__38.html) [StVO §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)

### In slow or stationary multi-lane traffic, form the rescue corridor

On Autobahns and rural roads with at least two lanes in one direction, form the
*Rettungsgasse* as soon as traffic is at walking pace or stationary. The corridor
lies between the far-left lane and the lane immediately to its right; all other
lanes move right. Form it before an emergency vehicle is visible and keep it
open. [StVO §11(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__11.html)

### Blue light without siren is a warning

<!-- questions: 1.2.38-101, 1.2.38-102 -->

Blue flashing light **without** siren may warn of an accident or another
deployment scene, accompany an emergency journey, or mark an escort or closed
formation. Increase attention, anticipate the vehicle's needs, and facilitate
its passage safely. [StVO §38(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__38.html)

Unlike blue light plus siren, blue light alone does not activate the express
StVO §38(1) command that everyone must immediately clear the way. Do not panic or
break rules blindly; respond considerately to the warning and actual situation.
[StVO §38(1)–(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__38.html) [StVO §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)

### Yellow flashing light warns of a particular danger

<!-- questions: 1.2.38-003 -->

Yellow flashing light (*gelbes Blinklicht*) warns of hazards, including work or
accident sites and unusually slow, wide, or long vehicles or loads. Slow down as
conditions require, increase clearance, avoid risky overtaking, and be prepared
for workers, obstacles, or an exceptional vehicle path. It warns; it does not
grant the vehicle a universal right of way. [StVO §38(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__38.html) [StVO §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)

## Signal comparison

| Signal | Legal message | Your response |
|---|---|---|
| Blue + siren | Highest urgency; clear the way immediately | Locate, create space now, avoid danger |
| Blue only | Warning of deployment, scene, escort, or formation | Increase attention and facilitate safely |
| Yellow flashing | Warning of a particular hazard | Slow/adapt, increase clearance, expect danger |

These meanings follow the three separate paragraphs of StVO §38.
[StVO §38](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__38.html)

## Dataset warning

The explanations for **1.2.38-101** and **1.2.38-102** can imply that blue light
alone creates the same mandatory immediate-clearance rule as blue light with
siren. Current StVO distinguishes them: §38(1) imposes “clear the way
immediately” for blue light **together with** siren, while §38(2) defines blue
light alone as a warning signal. Facilitate safely, but do not confuse warning
with the stronger command. [StVO §38(1)–(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__38.html)

## Common exam traps

- Hearing a siren and moving before locating its source.
- Braking abruptly in the emergency vehicle's path.
- Creating space by endangering a pedestrian or cyclist.
- Waiting to see emergency lights before forming a rescue corridor in a queue.
- Treating blue light alone as legally identical to blue light plus siren.
- Treating yellow flashing light as a grant of priority instead of a warning.

## Remember this

- **Blue + siren:** clear the way immediately.
- **Look first:** source, direction, obstruction, safe escape path.
- **Rettungsgasse:** leftmost lane left; every lane beside it right.
- **Blue only:** deployment warning; facilitate calmly.
- **Yellow:** hazard warning—slow, give space, expect the unusual.
