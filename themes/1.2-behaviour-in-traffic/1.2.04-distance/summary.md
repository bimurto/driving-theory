# Distance — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

A safe following distance (*Sicherheitsabstand*) gives you time and space to
react if the vehicle ahead brakes suddenly. It also improves your view beyond
that vehicle, helping you drive anticipatively (*vorausschauend*) instead of
reacting at the last moment.

There is no single distance that is safe in every situation. The necessary gap
grows with speed and whenever visibility or road grip becomes worse.

## Learning goals

After studying this chapter, you should be able to:

- explain the legal test for the distance from the vehicle ahead;
- adjust the gap for speed, visibility, and road-surface condition;
- use the “half the speedometer reading” exam rule of thumb;
- restore the gap safely after another vehicle moves into it; and
- react safely when an overtaking vehicle needs room to return to the lane.

## Core rules

### Keep enough space to stop behind the vehicle ahead

<!-- questions: 1.2.04-101 -->

As a rule, your distance from a vehicle ahead (*Abstand zum vorausfahrenden
Fahrzeug*) must be large enough for you to stop behind it even if it suddenly
brakes. This is the legal test; the StVO does not prescribe one universal
number for an ordinary car in all conditions. [Section 4(1) of the StVO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__4.html)

Choose the gap by considering all three exam factors:

- **Speed (*Geschwindigkeit*):** at higher speed you travel farther while
  reacting and need more space to brake.
- **Visibility (*Sichtverhältnisse*):** a larger gap improves the view ahead
  and gives you earlier warning. If fog, rain, spray, darkness, or the vehicle
  ahead restricts your view, increase the gap and reduce speed as necessary.
- **Road-surface condition (*Fahrbahnzustand*):** water, snow, ice, mud, or
  loose material can reduce grip and lengthen the stopping distance, so leave
  more space.

The official driver-training curriculum connects speed, distance, stopping
distance, anticipatory driving, and adaptation to road, weather, and visibility
conditions. [Annex 1, section 8 of the Fahrschüler-Ausbildungsordnung](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fahrschausbo_2012/anlage_1.html)

### Adequate distance improves both sight and safety

<!-- questions: 1.2.04-104 -->

A sufficient gap lets you look farther along the road and notice developing
hazards sooner. That supports anticipatory driving and reduces abrupt reactions
and rear-end-collision risk. It therefore **improves** visibility rather than
restricting it. The official curriculum expressly treats anticipatory driving
and safe distances as connected skills. [Annex 1, section 8(b)–(d) of the Fahrschüler-Ausbildungsordnung](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fahrschausbo_2012/anlage_1.html)

## Numbers and formulas

### “Half the speedometer reading” is a rule of thumb

<!-- questions: 1.2.04-101 -->

For the theory test, estimate a normal minimum following distance in metres as:

```text
following distance (m) ≈ speed in km/h ÷ 2
```

Examples:

| Speed | Calculation | Rule-of-thumb gap |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 50 km/h | 50 ÷ 2 | 25 m |
| 80 km/h | 80 ÷ 2 | 40 m |
| 100 km/h | 100 ÷ 2 | 50 m |

This “half the speedometer reading” (*halber Tachoabstand*) is an **exam rule of
thumb**, not a fixed statutory minimum for every situation. Increase it when
visibility or grip is poor, or whenever the gap would not let you stop safely
behind a suddenly braking vehicle. [Question-bank explanation for 1.2.04-101](https://www.clickclickdrive.de/fragenkatalog/en/1.2.-behaviour-in-traffic/1.2.04-distance/1.2.04-101) [Section 4(1) of the StVO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__4.html)

## Situations and exceptions

### Another vehicle moves into your safety gap

<!-- questions: 1.2.04-102 -->

If a vehicle changes into your lane just ahead and consumes your safety gap,
do not continue unchanged at the same speed. Release the accelerator and brake
as necessary until a sufficient gap exists again. Keep watching behind you and
brake progressively where the situation allows; the aim is to restore space
without creating a new danger.

The duty to maintain enough stopping space still applies after someone else
cuts in. At the same time, a driver ahead may not brake hard without compelling
reason, so avoid retaliatory or unnecessary harsh braking. [Section 4(1) of the StVO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__4.html) [Question-bank scenario 1.2.04-102](https://www.clickclickdrive.de/fragenkatalog/en/1.2.-behaviour-in-traffic/1.2.04-distance/1.2.04-102)

### An overtaking vehicle needs room to move back in

<!-- questions: 1.2.04-103 -->

If you are being overtaken, you must not increase your speed. In the depicted
exam situation, the overtaking car is returning ahead of you and the available
space is tightening, so reduce speed smoothly to let it move back in and to
preserve a safe following gap. [Section 5(6) of the StVO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__5.html) [Question-bank scenario 1.2.04-103](https://www.clickclickdrive.de/fragenkatalog/en/1.2.-behaviour-in-traffic/1.2.04-distance/1.2.04-103)

Do not turn this scene into the false rule “always brake whenever someone
overtakes.” The statutory baseline is **do not accelerate**. Reduce speed when
the developing situation requires it to avoid danger or obstruction and to
create a safe gap; road users must act with constant care and mutual
consideration. [Sections 1 and 5(6) of the StVO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)

## Common exam traps

- Treating one fixed distance as safe regardless of speed, visibility, or grip.
- Remembering speed but overlooking poor visibility or a slippery surface.
- Calling the half-speedometer estimate a statutory distance rather than an
  exam rule of thumb.
- Maintaining speed after another vehicle cuts into the safety gap.
- Accelerating while being overtaken; this is expressly prohibited.
- Assuming that adequate following distance blocks the view—it normally helps
  you see farther ahead and react earlier.

## Remember this

- **Legal test:** be able to stop behind the vehicle ahead if it brakes suddenly.
- **Normal exam estimate:** distance in metres ≈ half the speed in km/h.
- **More speed, worse visibility, or less grip:** leave a larger gap.
- **Vehicle cuts in:** ease off and brake as necessary to rebuild the gap.
- **Being overtaken:** never accelerate; reduce speed when needed to let the
  other vehicle return safely.
- **More distance:** better forward view, earlier reactions, lower collision risk.
