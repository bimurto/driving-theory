# Signs and Instructions from Police Officers — Class B Study Summary

## Core rules

### Police directions come first

Obey a police officer's signs and instructions even when an operating traffic
light or a priority sign says something different. The practical hierarchy is:

1. police signs and instructions;
2. traffic lights;
3. priority-regulating traffic signs; and
4. general rules such as *rechts vor links* (“right before left”).

Police directions take precedence over every other order and rule, while
traffic lights take precedence over priority rules and priority signs.
[StVO §36(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__36.html) [StVO §37(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__37.html)

Precedence does **not** remove the duty of care. Continue watching for people
who misunderstand the officer or enter from a released direction.
[StVO §36(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__36.html)

### Officer facing you or with their back to you: stop

When the officer's arm or arms extend sideways **across your direction of
travel**, stop before the junction. In the familiar pose, traffic seeing the
officer's chest or back is stopped, while cross traffic seeing the officer's
side is released. Wait before the junction even if a light or sign would
otherwise give you priority. [StVO §36(2) no. 1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__36.html)

### Officer side-on to you: your direction is released

When you see the officer in profile and their sideways arm position runs along
your direction of travel, your direction may cross the junction. Proceed
promptly but carefully; “released” is not permission to rush. The officer need
not keep both arms stretched out—the signal continues while the same direction
is waved through or the officer maintains the basic position. [StVO §36(2) no. 1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__36.html)

Released traffic may turn under the normal rules of StVO §9. Signal and position
correctly, yield to the users protected by the turning rules, and do not obstruct
a rail vehicle when turning left. [StVO §36(2) no. 1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__36.html) [StVO §9](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__9.html)

### Raised arm: transition—wait or clear

A raised arm means:

- traffic **before** the junction must wait for the next signal; and
- traffic **already in** the junction must clear it.

Thus the vehicle already inside leaves the conflict area, while waiting drivers
get ready but do not start until the next releasing signal. [StVO §36(2) no. 2](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__36.html)

## Police-vehicle messages

### “STOP POLIZEI” targets the addressed vehicle

If “STOP POLIZEI” is shown toward you by the police car immediately behind, the
instruction is addressed to **you**, not automatically to every nearby vehicle.
Comply and stop safely as directed. Police may stop road users for traffic and
fitness-to-drive checks using suitable technical equipment, a signalling disc
(*Winkerkelle*), or a red light. [StVO §36(5)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__36.html)

### “BITTE FOLGEN” means follow, do not pass, then stop

When “BITTE FOLGEN” (“please follow”) is displayed by the police vehicle directly
in front of you, it applies to you. Follow at a safe distance and stop when the
police vehicle stops; do not interpret the message as applying to all following
traffic. The instruction is a lawful police stop direction and must be obeyed.
[StVO §36(1), (5)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__36.html)

### A displayed police signalling disc can direct you to follow

If a police signalling disc is displayed toward you from the vehicle in front,
do not overtake it. Follow until it stops and then comply with the officers'
instructions. StVO §36(5) expressly permits a signalling disc and allows police
to stop a road user travelling ahead of them as well as one behind.
[StVO §36(5)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__36.html)

## Reading the junction safely

Use this three-step check:

1. **Where am I relative to the officer?** Chest/back means stop; profile means
   the direction is released.
2. **Is the arm raised?** Before the junction, wait; inside it, clear.
3. **What conflicts remain?** Apply turning duties and watch for errors even
   though lights and signs are overridden. [StVO §36(1)–(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__36.html)

## Common exam traps

- Following a green light instead of the police officer.
- Applying a priority sign while an officer is directing the junction.
- Reading the officer from another vehicle's viewpoint rather than your own.
- Starting as soon as an arm is raised instead of waiting for the next signal.
- Remaining inside the junction when the raised arm tells you to clear it.
- Treating side-on release as freedom from normal turning duties.
- Assuming “BITTE FOLGEN” addresses every vehicle in the queue.
- Overtaking a police vehicle that displays a signalling disc toward you.

## Remember this

- **Police > lights > signs > general rules.**
- **Chest or back:** stop before the junction.
- **Profile:** proceed carefully; your direction is released.
- **Arm raised:** wait before, clear if already inside.
- **STOP POLIZEI:** stop safely as directed.
- **BITTE FOLGEN:** follow, do not pass, stop when police stop.
- **Always:** precedence does not cancel due care.
