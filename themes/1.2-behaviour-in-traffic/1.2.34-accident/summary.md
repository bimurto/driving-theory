# Accident — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

After a collision, protect life first without abandoning the legal duties of an
accident participant (*Unfallbeteiligter*). Stop, secure the scene, assess what
happened, help injured people, call emergency services, and enable the required
identification. Tunnel smoke and dangerous-goods leaks demand extra distance
and rapid self-rescue.

## Learning goals

After studying this chapter, you should be able to:

- follow the accident-response sequence safely;
- make an effective emergency call and find motorway help;
- fulfil identification and documentation duties;
- deal with an unattended damaged vehicle without committing hit-and-run;
- secure rural-road and tunnel incidents; and
- keep clear of hazardous substances.

## Core rules

### Stop, secure, assess, help

<!-- questions: 1.2.34-103, 1.2.34-113 -->

Anyone involved must stop immediately, secure traffic, determine the
consequences, and help injured people. With minor damage, move promptly to the
side so the vehicles do not create a new obstruction or danger. Do not remove
accident traces before the necessary findings have been made. [StVO §34(1)–(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__34.html)

### Organise the rescue chain, and delegate when possible

<!-- questions: 1.2.34-007, 1.2.34-102 -->

First protect yourself and secure the danger area. Then give immediate
life-saving first aid and call **112** as early as safely possible; continue
first aid until rescue personnel arrive. If others are present, assign the call
and first aid in parallel. The exam sequence “secure—help—call” expresses the
priorities, but a rigid delay in calling is unsafe when you are alone; the
situation determines whether the call or a particular aid measure comes first.
[BBK — First aid and emergency calls](https://www.bbk.bund.de/DE/Warnung-Vorsorge/Vorsorge/Gesundheit-und-Hygiene/Erste-Hilfe-und-Notruf/erste-hilfe-und-notruf_node.html) [StGB §323c](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__323c.html)

### Identify yourself and exchange the required information

<!-- questions: 1.2.34-006, 1.2.34-110 -->

Tell other participants that you were involved. On request, give your name and
address, show your driving licence (*Führerschein*) and registration certificate
Part I (*Zulassungsbescheinigung Teil I*, traditionally *Fahrzeugschein*) or
operating permit, and provide liability-insurance information to the best of
your knowledge. The insurance contract itself need not be presented. [StVO §34(1) no. 5](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__34.html)

<!-- questions: 1.2.34-008 -->

For a minor collision where everyone agrees police attendance is unnecessary,
exchange contact, vehicle, and insurance details; photograph the positions and
damage from several angles; and jointly record the apparent sequence without
altering traces prematurely. These practical records support the findings that
StVO §34 requires and the later claims process. [StVO §34(1), (3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__34.html)

### Unattended damage: a note alone is not enough

<!-- questions: 1.2.34-002, 1.2.34-009 -->

After damaging a parked vehicle, wait an **appropriate time for the
circumstances**; the law sets no universal number of minutes. If nobody appears,
leave your name and address at the scene and then enable the findings
immediately—for example by notifying a nearby police station and providing your
address, whereabouts, registration number, and the vehicle's location. Giving
details only to an uninvolved witness is insufficient. [StVO §34(1) nos. 6–7](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__34.html)

Unlawfully leaving can be punished with a fine or up to three years'
imprisonment. A driving ban may be imposed; licence withdrawal is the usual
assessment for hit-and-run involving death, non-minor injury, or significant
property damage when the offender knew or should have known. Suitability doubts
arising from serious or repeated traffic offences can also lead the authority
to require a medical-psychological assessment (*MPU*); it is not automatic in
every minor case. [StGB §142](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__142.html) [StGB §44](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__44.html) [StGB §69(2) no. 3](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__69.html) [FeV §11(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fev_2010/__11.html)

## Emergency calls and motorway help

### Know 110 and 112

<!-- questions: 1.2.34-005, 1.2.34-011 -->

In Germany, call **110** for police and **112** for fire and rescue services.
The EU-wide **112** can be called free of charge from fixed or mobile phones
throughout the EU and connects to the appropriate emergency service. **115** is
an authorities' service number, not an emergency number. [BBK emergency guide](https://www.bbk.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/Mediathek/Publikationen/Buergerinformationen/Ratgeber/ratgeber-notfallvorsorge.pdf?__blob=publicationFile) [European Commission — 112](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/112)

### On the Autobahn, use the fastest available contact

<!-- questions: 1.2.34-106, 1.2.34-112 -->

Call 112 or 110, use an emergency call box (*Notrufsäule*), or seek help at a
service area if it can be reached safely. Black directional arrows on motorway
delineator posts point along the shortest route to a call box; do not rely on
guardrail or hard-shoulder markings. German training expressly includes
emergency-call facilities and behaviour after motorway accidents. [FahrschAusbO Annex 1, sections 1.2 and 3.1.6](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fahrschausbo_2012/anlage_1.html)

When calling, give the exact place and direction of travel, what happened, the
number and condition of injured people, and any special hazards; identify
yourself and wait for questions. [BBK — First aid and emergency calls](https://www.bbk.bund.de/DE/Warnung-Vorsorge/Vorsorge/Gesundheit-und-Hygiene/Erste-Hilfe-und-Notruf/erste-hilfe-und-notruf_node.html)

## Securing special accident scenes

### Rural road: be visible well before the scene

<!-- questions: 1.2.34-111 -->

Switch on hazard warning lights immediately. Put on a high-visibility vest,
leave the vehicle on the side away from traffic if possible, and place the
warning triangle so it is clearly visible at a sufficient distance; the exam
rule of thumb on a rural road is **about 100 m**, increased where a bend or crest
requires earlier warning. Warn traffic by hand only without endangering yourself
or anyone else. StVO sets the functional requirement—well visible at sufficient
distance—rather than a fixed 100 m statutory distance. [StVO §15](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__15.html) [StVZO §53a](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__53a.html)

### Tunnel accident or fire: use tunnel safety equipment

<!-- questions: 1.2.34-004, 1.2.34-109 -->

For an accident, switch on hazard warning lights, stop as far right as safely
possible or in a bay, secure the vehicle, and use the SOS telephone where
available; it helps the control centre locate the incident. Do not assume you
must remain inside the vehicle. [StVO §15](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__15.html) [BBK tunnel incident guidance](https://www.bbk.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/Mediathek/Publikationen/KRITIS/ereignismanagement_strassentunnel.pdf?__blob=publicationFile)

If fire or smoke prevents driving out, switch off the engine, leave the ignition
key or transponder in the vehicle so responders can move it, and escape promptly
through the marked emergency exit—never wait in the smoke-filled vehicle. The
BBK guidance stresses rapid self-rescue, leaving the key, and not turning around
in the tunnel. [BBK tunnel incident guidance](https://www.bbk.bund.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/Mediathek/Publikationen/KRITIS/ereignismanagement_strassentunnel.pdf?__blob=publicationFile)

### Dangerous goods: report the panel and keep away

<!-- questions: 1.2.34-010 -->

An orange dangerous-goods panel (*orangefarbene Warntafel*) identifies a
regulated dangerous-goods transport. Tell the emergency operator the numbers on
the panel from a safe location. Keep away from the leaking liquid and its flow
area, avoid ignition sources, and do not try to stop the leak yourself; trained
responders need the identification to select protection and containment.
[GGVSEB §19(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ggvseb/__19.html) [Federal portal — reporting water-hazardous releases](https://verwaltung.bund.de/leistungsverzeichnis/de/leistung/99000000001582)

## Dataset warning

Questions **1.2.34-007** and **1.2.34-102** present different linear orders for
first aid and the emergency call. Current BBK guidance says to protect yourself,
secure the scene if needed, begin life-saving measures, call 112, and continue
aid; tasks should run in parallel when helpers are available. Use the actual
casualty's needs rather than delaying either a critical measure or the call to
fit a memorised sequence. [BBK — First aid and emergency calls](https://www.bbk.bund.de/DE/Warnung-Vorsorge/Vorsorge/Gesundheit-und-Hygiene/Erste-Hilfe-und-Notruf/erste-hilfe-und-notruf_node.html)

The explanation for **1.2.34-114** overstates an MPU as always possible after
minor hit-and-run. FeV §11(3) permits an MPU where specified serious or repeated
offences create suitability doubts; it is an evidence-based administrative
decision, not an automatic consequence of every slight-damage case. [FeV §11(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fev_2010/__11.html)

## Common exam traps

- Photographing first while injured people still need help.
- Treating a paper note as a substitute for prompt follow-up reporting.
- Leaving minor-damage vehicles obstructing moving traffic.
- Giving insurance details but refusing to show licence or registration.
- Calling 115 instead of 110 or 112.
- Waiting inside a smoke-filled tunnel vehicle.
- Touching a dangerous-goods leak or approaching to read the orange panel.
- Treating 100 m as an exact statutory distance in every road layout.

## Remember this

- **Stop—secure—assess—help; call 112 early.**
- **Minor damage:** move aside promptly, then exchange and document.
- **Unattended car:** wait, leave details, report promptly.
- **Germany:** police 110; fire/rescue 112. **EU:** 112.
- **Rural road:** hazard lights and triangle about 100 m as a rule of thumb.
- **Tunnel smoke:** engine off, key left, emergency exit.
- **Orange panel:** report the numbers; stay away from the leak.
