# Priority — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

Solve priority (*Vorfahrt*) in layers: first police and lights, then priority
signs, then the general *rechts vor links* rule. After deciding which road has
priority, apply movement conflicts—especially the left-turner's duty to yield to
oncoming traffic. A priority entitlement never permits you to enter a blocked or
unclear junction blindly.

## Learning goals

After studying this chapter, you should be able to:

- apply *rechts vor links* and its exceptions;
- combine road priority with turning duties;
- distinguish signs 205, 206, 301, and 306;
- solve a turning-priority-road junction systematically;
- distinguish a signed roundabout from an ordinary circular junction; and
- wait, communicate, or creep forward safely when the situation is unclear.

## Core rules

### Right before left is the unsigned-junction default

<!-- questions: 1.3.01-001, 1.3.01-002, 1.3.01-007-M, 1.3.01-013-M, 1.3.01-034-M, 1.3.01-035-M, 1.3.01-044-M, 1.3.01-045-M, 1.3.01-048-M, 1.3.01-055-M, 1.3.01-103-M, 1.3.01-114-M, 1.3.01-115, 1.3.01-116, 1.3.01-118-M, 1.3.01-125-M, 1.3.01-129-M -->

At an intersection or junction without a special priority rule, the vehicle
coming from the **right** has priority: *rechts vor links*. This applies to every
vehicle type, including bicycles, motorcycles, tractors, and
electrokleinstfahrzeuge; size or engine power does not change it. [StVO §8(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__8.html) [eKFV §11](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ekfv/__11.html)

Use two separate checks:

1. **Road priority:** yield to the vehicle from your right.
2. **Movement conflict:** if turning left, also let oncoming traffic travelling
   straight or turning right pass—including cycles, motorcycles, and trams.

This explains why you may need to wait for both a vehicle from the right and an
oncoming vehicle, while still having priority over a vehicle from your left.
[StVO §8(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__8.html) [StVO §9(3)–(4)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__9.html)

### Field and forest paths are exceptions

<!-- questions: 1.3.01-101-M, 1.3.01-112, 1.3.01-128-M -->

A vehicle emerging from a field or forest path (*Feld- oder Waldweg*) onto
another road must wait; being on the other vehicle's right does not create
priority. A driver on the road may therefore move before the vehicle exiting the
track, while still driving defensively. [StVO §8(1) no. 2](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__8.html)

Exiting a property, traffic-calmed area, pedestrian area, or crossing a lowered
kerb is governed even more strictly by StVO §10: danger to every other road user
must be excluded. [StVO §10](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__10.html)

### If priority is unclear, do not force a solution

<!-- questions: 1.3.01-003 -->

Wait, observe, and communicate clearly if necessary. Even someone entitled to
priority must forgo it when the traffic situation requires; rely on another
person's waiver only after mutual understanding. Courtesy gestures never justify
moving without checking every other conflict. [StVO §11(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__11.html) [StVO §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)

## Priority signs

### Sign 205: give way to the priority road

<!-- questions: 1.3.01-016-M, 1.3.01-018-M, 1.3.01-042-M, 1.3.01-122-M -->

Sign 205 (*Vorfahrt gewähren*) requires you to yield to traffic on the priority
road from both directions. Approach at moderate speed and wait whenever you
cannot enter without endangering or substantially obstructing priority traffic.
After that road-priority check, a left-turner must still yield to oncoming
traffic. [StVO §8(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__8.html) [StVO Annex 2, sign 205](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/anlage_2.html) [StVO §9(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__9.html)

### Sign 301 grants priority at the next junction only

<!-- questions: 1.3.01-017-M, 1.3.01-056-M, 1.3.01-126-M -->

Sign 301 (*Vorfahrt*) gives you priority at the **next** intersection or
junction. Side-road vehicles wait, but if you turn left you must still let
oncoming traffic pass. Do not carry sign 301's effect forward to later
junctions. [StVO Annex 3, sign 301](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/anlage_3.html) [StVO §9(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__9.html)

### Sign 306 creates a continuing priority road

<!-- questions: 1.3.01-021-M, 1.3.01-049-M, 1.3.01-050-M, 1.3.01-051-M, 1.3.01-120-M, 1.3.01-121-M -->

Sign 306 (*Vorfahrtstraße*) gives the signed road priority until sign 205, 206,
or 307 ends it. Vehicles entering from subordinate roads wait—even if they are
on your right. Among vehicles on the priority road, apply movement conflicts: a
left-turner yields to an oncoming motorcycle, car, or tram continuing straight,
then proceeds before traffic still waiting on the subordinate road.
[StVO Annex 3, sign 306](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/anlage_3.html) [StVO §9(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__9.html)

### Sign 206: stop completely, then give way

<!-- questions: 1.3.01-047-M, 1.3.01-117-M -->

At sign 206 (*Halt. Vorfahrt gewähren*), make a complete stop at the stop line.
If there is no stop line, stop where the other road can be seen. Then yield to
all priority-road traffic; if turning left, also account for oncoming traffic.
Rolling through is not a stop. [StVO Annex 2, sign 206](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/anlage_2.html) [StVO §9(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__9.html)

### Turning into a priority road requires a full conflict scan

<!-- questions: 1.3.01-053 -->

When turning right from a subordinate road, check priority-road traffic from
both left and right. Also check alongside and behind for cyclists travelling
straight and protect pedestrians crossing the road you enter. Yielding to the
priority road does not replace the separate turning duties. [StVO §8(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__8.html) [StVO §9(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__9.html)

## Turning priority roads

### Read the thick line, then compare movements

<!-- questions: 1.3.01-038-M, 1.3.01-039-M, 1.3.01-040-M, 1.3.01-041-M, 1.3.01-043-M, 1.3.01-046-M, 1.3.01-057-M, 1.3.01-058-M, 1.3.01-059-M, 1.3.01-123-M, 1.3.01-124-M, 1.3.01-127-M -->

The supplementary diagram under sign 306 shows the course of an *abknickende
Vorfahrtstraße*: the **thick line** is the priority road; thin arms are
subordinate. First let every vehicle on the thick line go before vehicles on a
thin arm. This includes bicycles, motorcycles, trams, tractors, and e-scooters.
[StVO Annex 3, sign 306 supplementary diagram](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/anlage_3.html)

Among vehicles on the priority road, compare their paths. A vehicle following
the bend has priority over one crossing or entering from a thin arm; where two
priority-road vehicles conflict, apply the relevant turning and right-before-left
rules. After all priority-road traffic has passed, vehicles on subordinate arms
resolve their relationship with *rechts vor links* unless another rule controls
it. [StVO §8](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__8.html) [StVO §9](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__9.html)

If you **follow the bend**, signal early and clearly. If you leave the priority
road straight ahead, do not signal merely because you leave the thick line;
signal according to your actual physical turning movement. Give pedestrians
special consideration and wait if necessary. [StVO Annex 3, sign 306 supplementary diagram](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/anlage_3.html)

Use this order:

1. identify thick-line vehicles;
2. order conflicts among those vehicles;
3. then order the thin-arm vehicles; and
4. check cyclists and pedestrians before moving.

## Roundabouts and circular layouts

### Only sign 215 together with sign 205 creates the special rule

<!-- questions: 1.3.01-060-M, 1.3.01-113, 1.3.01-130-M -->

At a signed roundabout with sign 215 below sign 205, circulating traffic has
priority. Yield on entry, **do not signal when entering**, and signal when
exiting because that is a turn. [StVO §8(1a)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__8.html) [StVO §9(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__9.html)

A merely circular road layout without that sign combination is not governed by
the special roundabout rule. Apply its actual signs or, if none regulate
priority, *rechts vor links*. Signal any genuine turning movement in such an
ordinary circular junction. [StVO §8(1), (1a)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__8.html) [StVO §9(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__9.html)

## Visibility and traffic flow

### Restricted view: edge forward carefully

<!-- questions: 1.3.01-119 -->

If parked vehicles prevent you seeing the priority road, approach at moderate
speed and carefully edge forward (*hineintasten*) only until you have the view
needed to judge that priority traffic will not be endangered or substantially
obstructed. Creeping is a visibility technique, not a claim to priority.
[StVO §8(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__8.html)

### Do not use your priority to block the junction

<!-- questions: 1.3.01-052 -->

If your left turn is blocked while an oncoming cyclist passes, do not enter and
seal the junction merely because you have priority over a side vehicle. Letting
that vehicle move can preserve flow, but communicate the waiver and recheck all
directions. Never enter an intersection if you would have to wait there and
block it. [StVO §11(1), (3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__11.html) [StVO §9(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__9.html)

## Dataset terminology warning

Many worksheet explanations translate *rechts vor links* as “left yields to
right.” That describes the waiting relationship, but the standard German rule
name and safest memory phrase are **right before left**. It applies only after
checking for priority signs and the field/forest-path exception. [StVO §8(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__8.html)

Question **1.3.01-060-M** calls an unsigned circular layout “circular traffic.”
Do not confuse its ordinary *rechts vor links* solution with a legally signed
*Kreisverkehr*, which requires signs 215 and 205 and prohibits signalling on
entry. [StVO §8(1a)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__8.html)

## Common exam traps

- Applying right-before-left before reading signs.
- Assuming a bicycle, e-scooter, tram, or tractor is exempt from priority rules.
- Solving road priority but forgetting the left-turn conflict.
- Treating sign 301 as a continuing priority-road sign.
- Rolling at STOP rather than stopping completely.
- Using right-before-left for a vehicle emerging from a field or forest path.
- Reading a turning-priority diagram by road geometry instead of the thick line.
- Signalling merely because you leave the thick line straight ahead.
- Applying signed-roundabout rules to every circular layout.
- Entering an unclear or blocked junction because you technically have priority.

## Remember this

- **Order:** signs first; then right before left; then turning conflicts.
- **Left turn:** yield to oncoming straight/right-turning traffic.
- **301:** next junction only. **306:** continuing priority road.
- **206:** full stop, then yield.
- **Field/forest path:** emerging vehicle waits.
- **Thick line:** priority-road course; signal when following its bend.
- **Kreisverkehr:** 215 + 205; no signal on entry, signal on exit.
- **Unclear:** wait, observe, communicate—never force priority.
