# Traffic Facilities — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

Traffic facilities (*Verkehrseinrichtungen*) guide, channel, or protect road
users. On a motorway, the small arrow on a roadside marker (*Leitpfosten*) is
an emergency-location aid: it points towards the nearest emergency call
telephone (*Notrufsäule*) in that direction. It is not a sign for a car park or
the next exit.

## Learning goals

After studying this chapter, you should be able to:

- identify what the arrow on a motorway roadside marker indicates; and
- distinguish this emergency-telephone direction aid from exit and parking signs.

## Core rules

### Follow the arrow to the nearest emergency call telephone

<!-- questions: 2.4.43-002 -->

If you need help on the *Autobahn*, follow the arrow on the roadside marker in
the indicated direction to reach the nearest *Notrufsäule*. German road
guidance uses *Leitpfosten* to mark the course of the road; the StVO lists them
as traffic facilities for identifying the road alignment, normally at about
50-metre intervals. [StVO Annex 4, Section 3, Sign 620](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/anlage_4.html)

The arrow is therefore a directional clue to the emergency telephone, not an
indication of the next *Ausfahrt* (exit) or a *Parkplatz* (car park). The ADAC
describes these small arrows on motorway marker posts as showing the way to the
nearest telephone. [ADAC: locating the emergency call](https://www.adac.de/rund-ums-fahrzeug/unfall-schaden-panne/unfall/standortbestimmung-unfall//)

### Use the facility safely during a breakdown

Once stopped on the motorway, secure the vehicle and get behind the guardrail
(*Leitplanke*) before seeking help. The Autobahn GmbH advises switching on the
hazard warning lights, putting on a high-visibility vest, and taking shelter
behind the guardrail before making the call. [Autobahn GmbH: emergency call
facilities](https://www.autobahn.de/presse/mitteilung/autobahn-gmbh-informiert-ueber-ausfall-der-notfallkommunikationssysteme-1)

## Situations and exceptions

- Read the arrow from the roadside marker on your carriageway and walk only in
  the safe area behind the barrier; never cross the motorway to reach a facility.
- The marker arrow identifies the direction of the nearest *Notrufsäule*; it
  does not provide distance, parking, or exit information.
- If a roadside emergency telephone is unavailable, an emergency call can also
  be made by mobile phone; in a life-threatening emergency use 112. [Autobahn
  GmbH emergency guidance](https://www.autobahn.de/presse/mitteilung/autobahn-gmbh-informiert-ueber-ausfall-der-notfallkommunikationssysteme-1)

## Common exam traps

- Choosing “nearest car park” because the arrow is on the roadside.
- Choosing “next exit”: exits are indicated by motorway direction and exit
  signs, not by the small arrow on a *Leitpfosten*.
- Confusing the arrow’s direction with a distance or kilometre marker.

## Remember this

**Arrow on a motorway roadside marker = follow it to the nearest emergency call
telephone (*Notrufsäule*).**
