# Fatigue and Distraction — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

Fatigue (*Müdigkeit*), strong emotion, conversation, electronic devices, and
in-car activity reduce perception and decision quality. The safe response is to
remove the distraction or stop; willpower, loud radio, and an open window are
not substitutes for rest.

## Learning goals

- recognise early fatigue and stop before microsleep;
- prevent distraction from passengers and devices; and
- use driver-assistance systems without surrendering responsibility.

## Core rules

### Stop when tired

<!-- questions: 2.1.10-001, 2.1.10-002, 2.1.10-005, 2.1.10-006, 2.1.10-103 -->

Heavy eyelids, chills, sudden fright, concentration loss, and brief lapses are
warnings of dangerous fatigue. Interrupt the journey for an adequate break; on a
long trip plan timely, sufficient stops and move around during them. If possible,
let a fit co-driver take over. Young drivers returning from nightlife are at
particular risk when over-tired or physically exhausted. [StVO §2(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__2.html)

Stop before reaching for a fallen object or handling anything in the vehicle.
Pull over safely rather than allowing the task to compete with driving. [StVO §1(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)

### Remove distractions

<!-- questions: 2.1.10-004, 2.1.10-101, 2.1.10-102 -->

Telephoning, lighting a cigarette, animated conversation, emotional agitation,
engrossing radio content, and nagging children can divert eyes, hands, and
attention. Electronic-device use is generally prohibited while driving unless
the device is not picked up or held and only a brief, situation-appropriate use
is made. Do not use it when it requires looking away from traffic. [StVO §23(1a)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__23.html)

Keep the vehicle and passengers settled before moving. If a child, cigarette, or
conversation demands attention, stop at a suitable place. The driver remains
responsible for controlling the vehicle and observing signs. [StVO §1(2), §23(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)

### Driver assistance is assistance, not autonomy

<!-- questions: 2.1.10-003 -->

Assistance systems can misread situations, fail to detect hazards, or create
false expectations. Keep observing traffic, hands and attention ready, and do
not neglect personal responsibility simply because a system is active. [StVO §1(2), §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)

## Situations and exceptions

- A hands-free system does not make fatigue safe; stop when alertness falls.
- Passenger activity and radio content are distractions when they reduce
  attention, even if they are not electronic-device use. [StVO §1(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)
- Driver-assistance features do not transfer the driver’s legal duty to control
  the vehicle. [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

## Common exam traps

- Continuing after the first signs of fatigue.
- Believing coffee, cold air, or loud music replaces a break.
- Picking up a phone or fallen object while moving.
- Treating emotional excitement as harmless.
- Assuming assistance systems see every hazard.

## Remember this

- **Fatigue:** stop; rest; use a fit co-driver if available.
- **Long journey:** plan real breaks and exercise during stops.
- **Distraction:** secure children, devices, cigarettes, and objects before driving.
- **Assistance:** observe and remain responsible.
- **Any concentration loss:** interrupt the journey.
