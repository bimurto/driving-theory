# Speed and Hazard Anticipation — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

Speed changes what you can perceive, how quickly you react, and whether the
tyres can transmit steering and braking forces. A legal maximum is never a
target: select a speed that keeps the vehicle controlled and leaves time and
space for unexpected people, animals, vehicles, bends, and wind.

## Learning goals

- recognise when speed is being underestimated;
- slow before bends, hazards, and overtaking conflicts; and
- anticipate hazards where visibility or escape space is limited.

## Core rules

### Adapt speed before the hazard

<!-- questions: 2.1.05-006, 2.1.05-008, 2.1.05-010, 2.1.05-102, 2.1.05-104-M, 2.1.05-105-M -->

You may drive only as fast as you can constantly control the vehicle. Speed must
match road, traffic, visibility, weather, personal ability, vehicle, and load;
you must be able to stop within the visible distance. [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

Reduce speed before a sharp or narrow bend, not after entering it. Excess speed
uses the available tyre grip for cornering; braking or an abrupt steering input
on the bend can make the vehicle skid (*ins Rutschen geraten*) or leave the road
to the outside of the curve. [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

If a vehicle is overtaking dangerously in front of you, slow down and create
space for it and other traffic to escape. If a slow vehicle, a corner-cutting
oncoming vehicle, or an animal may appear after a blind bend, approach at a
speed that allows immediate braking. [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html), [StVO §5(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__5.html)

### Speed can become psychologically invisible

<!-- questions: 2.1.05-001, 2.1.05-002, 2.1.05-004, 2.1.05-005, 2.1.05-009 -->

After travelling fast for a long time, you can lose your feel for speed
(*Geschwindigkeitsgefühl*), underestimate your speed, concentrate less, react
later, and follow too closely. Wide, smooth, open roads with few trees,
buildings, or fences provide little peripheral movement and make speed harder to
judge. Do not treat this habituation as a legal exception: check the
speedometer, restore a safe following gap, and slow down before an exit or
change in road type. [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html), [StVO §4(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__4.html)

### High-speed motorway risks

<!-- questions: 2.1.05-001, 2.1.05-002, 2.1.05-010 -->

At motorway speed, other road users may underestimate your approach speed and
pull out or overtake into your path. Crosswinds (*Seitenwind*) can deflect the
vehicle, especially when emerging from a cutting, passing a lorry, or crossing
an exposed section. Keep both hands ready, maintain a safe gap, scan far ahead,
and reduce speed as soon as a hazard is developing. [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html), [StVO §4(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__4.html)

### Narrow avenues and half the visible distance

<!-- questions: 2.1.05-101 -->

On a narrow avenue (*Allee*), estimate both lateral clearance to the trees and
the position of oncoming traffic. If the road is so narrow that an oncoming
vehicle could be endangered—particularly a wide or tall vehicle occupying more
of the centre—you must choose a speed allowing you to stop within at least half
the visible distance. [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

### Hidden hazards around people and animals

<!-- questions: 2.1.05-007-B, 2.1.05-102, 2.1.05-103, 2.1.05-105-M -->

Parked vehicles can conceal children or other pedestrians who may run into the
road, and a door may open on the traffic side. Pass at a clearly reduced speed
with braking readiness. [StVO §3(1), §25(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

Expect sudden hazards in forests (*Wald*), especially at dusk when wild animals
may cross; after hilltops where the view is blocked; and near schools or school
bus stops where children may appear. Near children, older people, and people in
need of help, reduce speed and be prepared to brake so that danger is excluded.
If an animal enters the road, slow, brake, and keep control rather than swerving
blindly into traffic. [StVO §3(1)–(2a)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)

## Situations and exceptions

- The speed limit remains an upper boundary; conditions can require a lower
  speed. [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)
- On a narrow road, the half-visible-distance rule applies when oncoming traffic
  could be endangered. [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html)
- A safe following distance must be maintained; it must increase as speed and
  braking risk increase. [StVO §4(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__4.html)
- Overtaking requires sufficient view and must exclude hindering oncoming traffic
  throughout the manoeuvre. [StVO §5(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__5.html)

## Common exam traps

- Slowing only once a bend or hazard has already been reached.
- Assuming a wide, empty road makes a high speed easier to judge or safer.
- Forgetting that habituation reduces concentration and speed awareness.
- Following too closely after a long, monotonous high-speed drive.
- Ignoring crosswind or another driver's dangerous overtaking.
- Passing parked cars quickly despite hidden children or opening doors.
- Looking only at the road surface and not anticipating forest, hilltop, school,
  and bend hazards.

## Remember this

- **Before a bend:** brake early; preserve tyre grip for steering.
- **After long high-speed travel:** check speed, gap, and concentration.
- **Open road:** peripheral cues are weak; do not trust your speed sensation.
- **Narrow avenue:** judge trees and oncoming traffic; stop within half the view.
- **Parked vehicles:** children and doors can appear suddenly.
- **Blind places:** forest, hilltops, schools, and bends demand anticipation.
