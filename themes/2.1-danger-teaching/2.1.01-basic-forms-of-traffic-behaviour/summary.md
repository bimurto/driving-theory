# Basic Forms of Traffic Behaviour — Class B Study Summary

## Overview

Hazards often arise when grip, vehicle condition, visibility, or cooperation is
worse than expected. Preserve a safety margin: enter bends at an adapted speed,
keep the vehicle roadworthy, anticipate blind spots, and help another driver
escape a mistake instead of insisting on priority.

## Learning goals

After studying this chapter, you should be able to:

- identify technical and driving causes of skidding;
- recognise brake, steering, and lighting defects as dangerous;
- respond defensively to priority and overtaking errors;
- cooperate with a large vehicle changing lane; and
- explain how loud music can impair safety and other road users.

## Core rules

### A bend uses the tyre's limited grip reserve

<!-- questions: 2.1.01-001, 2.1.01-003 -->

Excessive bend speed, wet or icy surfaces, and heavy braking can exceed available
tyre grip. Cornering and braking must share the same friction reserve, so strong
braking in a bend can remove the lateral force needed to stay on line. Reduce
speed before the bend, steer smoothly, and avoid abrupt braking within it.
[StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html) [FahrschAusbO Annex 1, section 2.7](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fahrschausbo_2012/anlage_1.html)

Faulty dampers (*Stoßdämpfer*) reduce consistent tyre contact, while incorrect or
unequal tyre pressure changes grip and stability. Use manufacturer-specified
pressure for the load and repair defective suspension promptly; the driver must
ensure vehicle condition does not impair traffic safety. [StVO §23(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__23.html) [StVZO §30](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__30.html)

### Never cut a corner or dazzle oncoming traffic

<!-- questions: 2.1.01-004 -->

Cutting a blind or tight bend puts the vehicle into the opposing lane and can
create an unavoidable head-on conflict. Keep right and choose a speed that lets
you remain within your visible lane. Dip main beam in good time when an oncoming
road user could be dazzled. [StVO §2(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__2.html) [StVO §3(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__3.html) [StVO §17(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__17.html)

### Safety outranks priority

<!-- questions: 2.1.01-005-B -->

If another vehicle does not yield, reduce speed and waive your priority rather
than cause a collision. A warning horn may be used outside built-up areas or
when you see yourself or others endangered, but it is not a substitute for
braking. Priority may need to be waived when the traffic situation requires it.
[StVO §11(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__11.html) [StVO §16(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__16.html)

### Help an overtaker return safely

<!-- questions: 2.1.01-007-M -->

If an overtaking vehicle has misjudged the manoeuvre and needs to pull back in,
reduce speed smoothly and create a gap. Do not accelerate or close the space;
the overtaken driver must not increase speed, and everyone must prevent danger
regardless of who caused it. [StVO §5(6)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__5.html) [StVO §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)

### Stay out of a truck's blind area and cooperate

<!-- questions: 2.1.01-008-M -->

A truck driver may not see a car alongside in a mirror blind spot. If the truck
signals or begins a lane change and a safe gap is available, adapt speed and let
it merge instead of remaining beside it. The truck must ensure its manoeuvre is
safe, but your independent duty of consideration remains. [StVO §7(5)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__7.html) [StVO §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html)

## Vehicle defects

### Unequal brakes, steering play, and failed dipped beam are hazards

<!-- questions: 2.1.01-101, 2.1.01-103 -->

A service brake that acts strongly on one side can make the vehicle swerve under
braking. Excessive steering play delays or weakens directional response. A
faulty dipped headlight reduces both seeing and being seen. Each is a
safety-critical defect: stop normal use and arrange repair rather than adapting
to it as if it were harmless. [StVZO §41](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__41.html) [StVZO §38](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__38.html) [StVZO §49a](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__49a.html) [StVO §23(1)–(2)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__23.html)

## Attention and noise

<!-- questions: 2.1.01-102 -->

Very loud music, especially heavy bass, diverts attention and masks warning
sounds. It can also disorient blind pedestrians who depend on acoustic cues near
the vehicle. Keep volume low enough that sight and hearing remain adequate and
that nobody is endangered or avoidably disturbed. [StVO §23(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__23.html) [StVO §1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__1.html) [StVO §30(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__30.html)

## Common exam traps

- Blaming skids only on speed and overlooking dampers or tyre pressure.
- Braking heavily after entering a bend too fast.
- Treating priority as an obligation to continue into a conflict.
- Accelerating when an overtaker needs to pull back in.
- Remaining beside a truck because its driver bears lane-change responsibility.
- Treating one-sided braking or steering play as minor wear.
- Assuming music is safe merely because the driver enjoys it.

## Remember this

- **Bend:** slow before; smooth steering and braking.
- **Grip:** wet road, bad dampers, and wrong pressure reduce the margin.
- **Conflict:** waive priority and prevent the crash.
- **Overtaker:** create a return gap.
- **Truck:** avoid blind areas and cooperate.
- **Defect:** brakes, steering, or dipped beam—repair promptly.
